# Attempt 008 — Greece / Jurisdiction Applicability Audit

Status: jurisdictional quality gate. Questions only; no model-answer legal advice.

## Conclusion

Attempt 008 is overwhelmingly jurisdiction-neutral clinical psychiatry and is applicable to Greek specialist-board preparation. No clinical domain needs removal because it belongs only to another country's psychiatric system.

Five prompts need jurisdictional tightening to prevent imported UK/US legal frameworks from shaping later answers:

- Q8 informed consent / decision-making ability
- Q90a ECT when valid consent cannot be provided
- Q98 confidentiality
- Q98a threat to another person (remove Tarasoff-style identifiable-victim framing)
- Q99 criminal responsibility / forensic assessment

Q9 is already explicitly Greek and should remain so.

Several other questions are clinically universal but their eventual model answers must use current Greek law, regulation, service organisation or licensing rather than silently importing NICE/NHS, Mental Health Act, Tarasoff, US insanity-defence, or other foreign-system rules.

## Current Greek anchors checked

### Informed consent

Greek Code of Medical Ethics, Law 3418/2005, Article 12 recognises informed consent and the patient's capacity/ability to consent, including rules for minors and patients unable to consent. Future answers must distinguish clinical assessment of ability to consent from broader legal-status concepts and must use the current Greek framework.

### Confidentiality

Law 3418/2005, Article 13 establishes medical confidentiality and permits disclosure in defined circumstances including fulfilment of a legal duty, protection of a substantial legitimate/public interest that cannot otherwise be protected, and necessity/defence. Do not import a freestanding US Tarasoff rule or describe an automatic duty to warn an identifiable victim unless Greek law specifically supports that proposition at answer-writing time.

### Involuntary psychiatric care

Law 2071/1992, Articles 95–97 remains a core statutory framework for involuntary admission. Later Greek legislation has modified important procedural/service aspects, including transport (Article 96A / 2022 reforms) and the mental-health service organisation under Law 5129/2024. Q9 therefore appropriately asks the Greek framework directly and its answer must be freshly verified.

### Child safeguarding

Greece has a national child-abuse reporting/monitoring framework including Law 4837/2021 as amended and the Mila / EKKA system. Q58/58a are clinically universal, but later answers must describe current Greek reporting duties and pathways rather than UK safeguarding structures.

### Criminal responsibility

The Greek Penal Code has its own criminal-responsibility framework; Article 34 addresses inability to attribute criminal responsibility due to mental/intellectual disorder or disturbance of consciousness affecting appreciation of wrongfulness or ability to act accordingly. Future answers must use Greek concepts such as καταλογισμός and must not substitute M'Naghten, US insanity-defence tests, or UK fitness/competence rules.

### Mental-health system

Law 5129/2024 reorganised Greek mental-health services into the National Network of Mental Health Services (EDYPSY) and regional networks. Q100 is intentionally principle-based and remains applicable, but any system-specific answer material should describe the current Greek organisation rather than assuming NHS community mental-health teams, US ACT infrastructure, or another country's service model.

## Architecture rule to carry forward

For the final guide:

1. Diagnostic and clinical-science questions may use DSM-5-TR / ICD-11 and international evidence.
2. Treatment evidence may use current major international guidelines, but availability/licensing/regulation must be checked for Greece/EU when relevant.
3. Law, consent, involuntary care, confidentiality, safeguarding and forensic rules must be Greek.
4. Service organisation must not silently assume NHS, US managed-care, ACT/CMHT, or another national model.
5. Foreign legal concepts may appear only as explicitly labelled comparison/history, never as the Greek exam answer.
