# Greek v5 — founder-approved scale doctrine

Status: **AUTHORITATIVE FOR V5 SCALE-UP**

Branch at calibration: `greek-v5-pilot`

This document records the final editorial doctrine produced by sentence-level founder review of the eight-question Greek-first v5 pilot. It supersedes the stylistic conclusions of the pre-founder Writer self-QA and independent adjudication wherever they conflict.

The approved source/factual layer remains unchanged. This document governs **how verified psychiatric content is turned into Greek oral-board study text**.

## 1. Core objective

The model oral answer must sound like a strong Greek psychiatry specialist candidate answering the examiner directly.

The target is not:

- a translated guideline;
- a polished essay;
- an editorial outline converted into prose;
- a protocol summary;
- a checklist narrated aloud;
- an AI-generated answer made superficially more Greek.

The target is **clinically natural Greek discourse with high retrieval value**.

Accuracy remains prior to style. Naturalisation must not alter verified thresholds, doses, criteria, legal rules, recommendation strength or uncertainty.

## 2. The decisive distinction: information architecture vs clinical discourse architecture

A useful backstage outline is not automatically a useful spoken answer.

For example, an editor may organise alcohol withdrawal as:

`recognition → severity → setting → benzodiazepines → scale → support`

That does **not** justify learner-facing prose such as:

`Η πρώτη απόφαση είναι το κατάλληλο πλαίσιο αντιμετώπισης.`

The clinician actually reasons:

1. recognise the syndrome;
2. assess current symptom severity;
3. assess predictors of complicated withdrawal;
4. begin appropriate treatment/support;
5. determine the safe level of care from that assessment.

The model answer must express the second logic, not narrate the first outline.

### Hard rule

**Outline labels must never be mechanically converted into paragraph openings or spoken transitions.**

Words such as `setting`, `severity`, `monitoring`, `differential`, `first decision`, `next step`, `framework` and `approach` may be useful internally. In the final oral answer they survive only if a psychiatrist would naturally use them in that sentence and they add clinical information.

## 3. Answer the question in the order asked

When a question explicitly asks multiple things, the default structure follows that order.

Examples:

- `κλινική εικόνα, διάγνωση και διαφορική διάγνωση` → clinical picture first, then diagnosis, then differential;
- `αναγνωρίζετε, αξιολογείτε και αντιμετωπίζετε` → recognition, then assessment, then management;
- `τι είναι ... και πώς χρησιμοποιείται` → definition/model first, then use/applications.

Depart from the examiner's order only when clinical safety or logic clearly requires it.

This rule prevents criteria, causes, guidelines or treatment details from taking over before the actual question has been answered.

## 4. Direct opening rule

The first sentence or short paragraph should answer the examiner's first task immediately.

Avoid forced taxonomy and circular definitions.

Bad pattern:

`Η μείζων καταθλιπτική διαταραχή είναι καταθλιπτική διαταραχή...`

If the question asks for clinical picture, begin with the clinical picture.

Avoid introductory commentary about what the candidate intends to demonstrate, organise or distinguish. State the psychiatry directly.

## 5. Clinical causality rule

Every decision in the answer should follow from the information that justifies it.

Examples:

- assessment of severity/risk → disposition;
- identification of underlying cause → etiological treatment;
- diagnosis/phenomenology → differential;
- formulation/mechanism → CBT technique;
- indication and patient factors → medication choice/monitoring.

Do not announce an action as an abstract decision before establishing its clinical basis.

## 6. Read-aloud necessity rule

Every paragraph must survive a literal viva test.

For each sentence ask:

1. Would a strong Greek psychiatrist plausibly say this aloud to an examiner?
2. Does it add psychiatric information, prioritisation or reasoning?
3. Is the wording ordinary professional Greek rather than a possible translation?

If not, rewrite or remove it.

### Language patterns to scrutinise

- `Η πρώτη απόφαση είναι...`
- `Το επόμενο βήμα είναι...`
- `Η προσέγγιση οργανώνεται...`
- `Στην εξέταση θέλω να δείξω...`
- `Για τις εξετάσεις πρέπει...`
- `Δεν είναι απλώς... αλλά...`
- generic conclusions that restate the preceding paragraphs.

These phrases are not universally prohibited. They are prohibited when they merely narrate structure or create generated cadence.

## 7. Greek terminology rule

Use terms actually used in Greek psychiatric/medical practice, not merely grammatically possible translations.

Founder-calibrated examples:

- `πληροφορίες από το περιβάλλον`, not `ετεροαναφορικές πληροφορίες`;
- `υπερδραστηριότητα του αυτόνομου νευρικού συστήματος`, not the calqued `αυτόνομη υπερδραστηριότητα` when natural Greek is preferable;
- `ψυχοκινητική διέγερση`;
- `ασφάλεια`, not awkward protective-language calques;
- `υπερκινητικό`, `υποκινητικό`, `μικτό` ντελίριο are acceptable Greek clinical terms;
- avoid coined abstract compounds when ordinary phrases such as `υπόθεση εργασίας` are clearer.

When terminology is uncertain and consequential to Greek usability, verify actual Greek usage rather than guessing from English.

## 8. Clinical categories should reflect psychopathology, not convenience

Do not merge clinically distinct domains merely because it shortens the answer.

Founder example from depression:

- **γνωστική λειτουργία**: attention, concentration, slowing of thought, decision-making;
- **περιεχόμενο της σκέψης / καταθλιπτικές γνωσίες**: pessimism, hopelessness, guilt, worthlessness, self-esteem, negative expectations.

These belong in separate clinical categories when that distinction improves understanding and oral recall.

## 9. Operational material is subordinate to the clinical answer

Criteria, scales, monitoring schedules, doses and legal articles are often essential, but they should support the clinical answer rather than become its architecture by default.

Use lists/tables when they materially improve retrieval.

Examples:

- diagnostic denominator → show counted items nearby;
- clozapine ANC schedule → table appropriate;
- 4AT → brief components in the oral answer, compact scoring detail in board facts;
- Wernicke treatment dose → visible because high-stakes;
- legal exceptions → table may be appropriate because the content is inherently technical.

Do not turn a model oral answer into a protocol recital simply because verified operational information exists.

## 10. Scales and named tools

A scale should be introduced according to its actual role.

Do not allow a screening tool to become the diagnosis.

For short high-yield instruments, a concise component list is useful when easily memorised.

Founder example: 4AT can be described briefly through:

- alertness;
- four orientation items;
- attention with months backwards;
- acute change/fluctuation;

with `≥4` as probable delirium, while reminding the learner that clinical diagnosis remains necessary.

Full instrument manuals do not belong in the spoken core unless specifically asked.

## 11. Emergency questions

Emergency answers should follow real clinical priorities, not a generic emergency template.

Typical logic:

1. identify the syndrome/emergency;
2. establish current severity and immediate threats;
3. identify predictors/causes that change risk;
4. initiate core treatment and supportive care;
5. determine disposition/escalation from the above;
6. separate major complications when that improves retrieval.

For alcohol withdrawal, distinguish:

- **current severity of withdrawal symptoms**;
- **risk factors for complicated/severe withdrawal**.

Do not collapse these into one paragraph as if they were the same construct.

Important related emergencies may deserve short subsections if that makes distinctions clearer. In Q38, brief separate subsections for **delirium tremens** and **Wernicke encephalopathy** were preferable to burying both inside general withdrawal prose.

## 12. Diagnostic questions

Clinical phenomenology comes before manual mechanics unless the examiner specifically asks for criteria.

Preferred logic:

1. clinical picture/syndrome;
2. severity/red flags where relevant;
3. diagnosis and operational criteria;
4. differential diagnosis;
5. selected discriminators/follow-ups.

DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 remain separate when their structures differ.

Do not force a definition if the question does not require one. If a definition is useful, avoid tautology and classification for its own sake.

## 13. Psychopharmacology questions

The answer should reflect pharmacological mastery without receptor or monitoring overload.

A useful sequence depends on the actual question. The founder-approved clozapine calibration showed that a natural answer may begin:

1. what the drug is / pharmacological identity;
2. how it works at the useful level of detail;
3. main indications/place in treatment;
4. major dangers/adverse effects;
5. monitoring and practical pharmacokinetic issues.

Do not impose `indication first` merely because a production template says so.

State mechanistic uncertainty when appropriate rather than inventing receptor-level certainty.

## 14. Psychotherapy questions

Psychotherapy answers should not become generic essays or technique catalogues.

Preferred CBT logic from the founder-approved pilot:

1. what CBT is;
2. core model/formulation;
3. collaborative therapeutic process;
4. principal techniques linked to mechanisms/problems;
5. selected psychiatric applications;
6. between-session work and relapse prevention.

Technique follows formulation/problem; do not list techniques without showing why they are used.

## 15. Law / ethics questions

Technical language is acceptable when the subject is inherently technical.

Do not simplify Greek legal boundaries into colloquial rules that lose precision.

The oral core should still identify:

1. governing duty/principle;
2. scope;
3. exceptions/legal bases;
4. practical handling/documentation.

High-value legal follow-ups can remain separate so the main answer is not overloaded.

## 17. What the founder pass specifically corrected

### Q001 — initial assessment

- Do not assume a syndrome before assessment.
- Safety/medical urgency first.
- Use ordinary Greek clinical risk terminology.
- `πληροφορίες από το περιβάλλον` is preferred.
- MSE domains should be complete and visible.
- Avoid unnecessary concluding commentary.

### Q012 — schizophrenia

- Start from clinical picture, not criteria.
- Definition → brief aetiology → symptom domains → course/diagnosis → criteria → differential.
- Historical phenomena such as Schneider are concise and clearly labelled.
- No exam-production language inside the oral answer.

### Q019 — clozapine

- Explain what the drug is and the useful mechanism before drowning the learner in monitoring.
- Pharmacological expertise without derivative chemistry or unsupported receptor-mechanism claims.
- Risks and monitoring remain operationally visible.

### Q020 — major depression

- Follow the question: clinical picture → diagnosis → differential.
- Do not force a tautological definition.
- Separate cognitive function from depressive cognitions/content of thought.
- Hopelessness deserves explicit clinical prominence.
- Criteria are subordinate to phenomenology.

### Q038 — alcohol withdrawal

- `setting` is not a natural second-paragraph topic by itself.
- Distinguish current symptom severity from predictors of complicated withdrawal.
- Disposition follows assessment.
- Benzodiazepine paragraph needs clinical logic, not generic protocol prose.
- Keep CIWA-Ar concise.
- Brief separate DT and Wernicke sections improve retrieval.
- Prefer natural Greek terms such as `υπερδραστηριότητα του αυτόνομου νευρικού συστήματος`.

### Q045 — delirium

- Follow the actual wording: definition → recognition → assessment → management.
- Recognition should include acute change, fluctuation, attention/awareness and associated clinical features.
- Assessment integrates patient, family/environment, nursing staff, records, medication chart, observations/vitals, physical/neurological exam and directed investigations.
- Treatment begins unmistakably with the underlying cause.
- Non-pharmacological care precedes symptomatic psychiatric medication.
- 4AT is useful enough to describe briefly, but not enough to organise the whole answer.

### Q090 — CBT

- Compress essay-like exposition.
- Definition/model → formulation → collaborative process → techniques → applications → between-session work/relapse prevention.
- Link techniques to mechanisms/problems.

### Q098 — confidentiality

- Technical legal prose is acceptable when precise.
- Do not over-edit technical material merely to make all questions sound stylistically identical.
- Question-specific asymmetry is a strength.

## 18. Scoring governance correction

The original independent adjudication gave very high scores to some answers that the founder later materially restructured, especially Q038 and Q045.

Therefore:

- numerical rubric scores are **not sufficient evidence of oral quality**;
- an answer cannot pass if clinical sequence or read-aloud naturalness fails, regardless of total score;
- independent adjudication before founder calibration is provisional;
- founder-approved pilot answers now define the stylistic target.

## 19. Mandatory production loop for scale-up

For each new question:

1. Read the verified factual brief/current-verification layer.
2. Read the question literally and identify its requested order.
3. Build a **backstage** content outline.
4. Separately decide the **spoken clinical logic**.
5. Draft in Greek from the clinical meaning, not from English or from v4 sentence order.
6. Read the model answer aloud mentally/synthetically sentence by sentence.
7. Remove architectural narration, meta-language and unnecessary sentences.
8. Check terminology against actual Greek clinical usage when uncertain.
9. Add operational lists/tables only where retrieval improves.
10. Compare with v4 only as a completeness backstop.
11. Re-check consequential facts against the approved source layer.
12. Score against the active v5 rubric.

## 20. Final editorial principle

**The model oral answer is not a visible outline. It is a clinician's answer.**

Structure should be felt through the clinical logic, not announced through editorial narration.
