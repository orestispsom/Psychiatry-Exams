# English ↔ Greek Psychiatry Translation Guide — Current Terminology Research

Scope: targeted bilingual psychiatric terminology research and adjudication for the `Psychiatry-Exams` project. This document does **not** rebuild the final guide. It resolves disputed terminology from `Translation-guide-audit.md`, establishes project house-style decisions, identifies high-confidence corrections, and supplies Writer rules for a later Translation Guide v2.

Research principle: the preferred Greek term is the form that a well-trained Greek psychiatrist is most likely to recognise, use naturally in an oral board answer, and understand correctly in a clinical MSE/report. Literal translation is secondary to established Greek psychiatric usage and conceptual fidelity.

Source hierarchy used in practice: (1) Greek university/academic psychopathology teaching, especially the current Hellenic Open University KEDIVIM programme **«Εισαγωγή στην Κλινική Ψυχοπαθολογία»** (scientific lead: Prof. Petros Skapinakis); (2) Greek academic/medical psychiatry sources, including teaching materials associated with Greek medical schools and established Greek psychiatric texts; (3) the Hellenic Psychiatric Association journal *Ψυχιατρική* and Greek professional sources; (4) official/current classification language where a settled Greek form exists; (5) authoritative English descriptive psychopathology to keep constructs separate; (6) historical Greek psychopathology where modern usage remains variable.

## 1. Executive adjudication

### 1.1 Major conclusions

1. **Do not equate pressured speech with logorrhea, even though Greek teaching often pairs them.** `Πίεση λόγου` / `πίεση στην ομιλία` is the best board translation of *pressure/pressured speech*. `Λογόρροια` is excessive, difficult-to-interrupt verbal output and may coexist with pressured speech. Contemporary Greek academic teaching sometimes writes `Πίεση στην Ομιλία / Λογόρροια (Pressure of Speech – Logorrhea)`, reflecting clinical overlap rather than strict identity. The glossary should preserve separate English entries and explain overlap. `Λογοδιάρροια` is colloquial/nonpreferred and must **not** be the canonical translation of pressure of speech.

2. **Circumstantiality and tangentiality must remain sharply distinct.** For circumstantiality, the most defensible Greek board forms are `υπερλεπτομερειακή σκέψη` / `παρεκβατικός λόγος`, with `λεπτολογικός λόγος` acceptable. `Περιστασιακότητα` occurs in older Greek psychopathology, but in modern general Greek it also means situationality/contextuality and is potentially misleading; retain for recognition, not as the project default. Tangentiality is well established as `εφαπτομενικότητα` or `εφαπτομενικός λόγος`.

3. **Overinclusive thinking is not circumstantiality.** The Greek academic form `υπερπεριεκτική σκέψη` is preferable. `Υπερεγκλειστικότητα` is intelligible but less natural. `Υπερλεπτομερειακή σκέψη = overinclusive thought` is a conceptual error: hyper-detail describes circumstantiality, not widened conceptual boundaries.

4. **Derailment and loosening of associations overlap but should not be collapsed completely.** Greek clinical sources commonly pair `εκτροχιασμός` with `χάλαση/χαλάρωση συνειρμών`. For this project: retain both English constructs as separate entries, cross-reference them, and explain that *derailment* is an observed shift in discourse from one train to a weakly/unrelated one, whereas *loosening of associations* is the broader underlying loss of normal associative linkage.

5. **Thought blocking = `ανακοπή σκέψης`**. This is the strongest academic/clinical Greek form. `Μπλοκάρισμα σκέψης` is understandable but colloquial. `Απόφραξη σκέψης` is not preferred.

6. **Perseveration = `εμμένουσα σκέψη` / `εμμένουσα απάντηση` depending modality.** Avoid bare `εμμονή`, because in Greek psychiatry it strongly risks confusion with obsession/obsessional preoccupation. `Περσεβερασιόν` is unnecessary as house style. `Επιμονή` is too nonspecific.

7. **Poverty phenomena require separate entries.** `πενία λόγου` (poverty of speech), `πενία περιεχομένου του λόγου` (poverty of content of speech), and `πενία σκέψης` or `πτωχεία σκέψης` (poverty of thought) are not interchangeable. `Αλογία` is the broader negative-symptom construct and can manifest as reduced quantity and/or content, but should not simply replace all three descriptive MSE terms.

8. **For clang associations use `ηχητικοί συνειρμοί`**, with `συνειρμοί κατ’ ομοηχία` as a precise descriptive alternative. `Κλαγγικοί συνειρμοί` is an awkward transliteration and should be avoided.

9. **Word salad deserves recognition as severe incoherence, not a separate disease construct.** Prefer `σαλάτα λέξεων` in a glossary entry cross-referenced to `ασυναρτησία / ακατάληπτος λόγος`. `Σχιζοφασία` is a broader/historical psychopathology term for severely disorganised schizophrenic language and is not a one-to-one translation of *word salad*.

10. **Schneiderian thought-alienation terminology is relatively stable in Greek.** Preferred forms: `παρεμβολή ξένων σκέψεων / παρεμβολή σκέψης`, `απόσυρση της σκέψης` (with `υφαρπαγή σκέψης` recognisable), and `εκπομπή της σκέψης`. These are highly examiner-recognisable. `Πρόσδοση` should be removed as a preferred insertion term.

11. **Confabulation, pseudomnesia and paramnesia must be separated.** For *confabulation*, use `μυθοπλασία` only with an explicit neuropsychiatric memory definition; however Greek `μυθοπλασία` can also mean fabulation/pathological fabrication, so `μυθοπλαστική συμπλήρωση μνημονικών κενών` is safer in explanatory text. `Ψευδομνησία` means false memory/pseudomnesia and is not synonymous with confabulation. `Παραμνησία` is the umbrella for qualitative distortions/false recognitions of memory and is broader than pseudomnesia.

12. **Restricted, blunted and flat affect should remain three levels/descriptions.** Preferred project forms: `περιορισμένο συναίσθημα`, `αμβλύ συναίσθημα / συναισθηματική άμβλυνση`, `επίπεδο συναίσθημα / συναισθηματική επιπέδωση`. `Περίσφιξη` is attested in Greek psychiatric usage, including older sources, but is awkward and potentially misleading outside that tradition; do not make it preferred house style.

13. **Inappropriate affect and mood/content-incongruent affect are conceptually distinct.** `Απρόσφορο συναίσθημα` is the canonical clinical Greek for inappropriate affect. `Ασύμφωνο με τη διάθεση` or `μη σύντονο προς τη διάθεση συναίσθημα/ψυχωτικό περιεχόμενο` should be used for mood incongruence. Do not merge `απρόσφορο` and `ασύμφωνο` into one slash entry.

14. **`Συναίσθημα` is the most practical Greek MSE translation of English *affect*, while `διάθεση` corresponds to *mood*.** There is no perfect one-to-one Greek equivalent of English *affect*: Greek psychiatry also uses `συναισθηματική έκφραση`, `συναισθηματικός τόνος`, and historically `θυμικό`. House style should use `συναίσθημα` for the observable MSE domain and add `συναισθηματική έκφραση` when clarity matters.

15. **Catatonic terminology:** `εμβροντησία` remains a strongly recognised Greek psychiatry term for stupor and is suitable for boards; add `κατατονικό stupor` only as a parenthetical recognition aid, not as replacement. `Καταληψία`, `κηρώδης ευκαμψία`, and `κατατονική στάση / διατήρηση στάσης` must remain separate. *Ambitendency* is historical and poorly standardised in Greek; `αμφιβουλησία` and `κινητική αμφιταλάντευση` are defensible descriptions, whereas `αμφισθένεια/αμφιθυμία` refer to ambivalence and must not be used as direct translations.

16. **Do not use speech-pathology `ταχυλαλία/ταχυφημία` as synonyms for manic pressured speech.** Greek speech-language pathology commonly uses `ταχυλαλία`, `ταχυφημία`, `βατταρισμός` for *cluttering*, a fluency/tempo disorder. In psychiatric MSE, simple fast speech is `ταχεία ομιλία / αυξημένος ρυθμός ομιλίας`; pressured speech is `πίεση λόγου`. Likewise use `βραδεία ομιλία / επιβραδυνμένος ρυθμός ομιλίας` rather than forcing `βραδυφημία` unless describing a speech-rate disorder.

17. **Aprosodia and dysprosodia are not synonyms.** `Απροσωδία` = absent/severely reduced prosody; `δυσπροσωδία` = abnormal prosody. Keep separate.

18. **Cenesthetic, somatic and visceral hallucinations should not be merged.** `Κοιναισθητικές ψευδαισθήσεις` is established for unusual bodily/global bodily sensations. `Σωματικές ψευδαισθήσεις` is a broader umbrella; `σπλαγχνικές ψευδαισθήσεις` specifically concern internal organs/viscera. Greek academic teaching explicitly distinguishes tactile, cenesthetic and visceral hallucinations.

19. **Pseudohallucination should remain for examination recognition but be qualified as contested.** Preferred contemporary documentation is phenomenological description (modality, perceived location, reality quality, insight, controllability), not deciding the diagnosis from an “inner vs outer space” label. House style: `ψευδοψευδαίσθηση` as historical/descriptive term; do not use it as a decisive psychosis/nonpsychosis boundary.

20. **Insight = `εναισθησία`; judgment = `κρίση`.** Keep them distinct from capacity, consent and reality testing. `Εναισθησία` is strongly embedded in Greek academic and clinical psychiatry. Contemporary clinical writing should specify dimensions—recognition of illness/symptoms, attribution, and perceived need for treatment—rather than rely only on “intellectual/emotional insight”; the latter distinction is historically useful but not a mandatory modern taxonomy.

### 1.2 System-level house decisions

- Prefer **Greek clinical nouns/phrases already used in university psychiatry** over phonetic Anglicisms.
- Where a term is historically entrenched but potentially misleading (`εμβροντησία`, `περίσφιξη`, `σχιζοφασία`), keep it for recognition but distinguish preferred contemporary wording.
- Do not use slash-separated terms when the slash hides a conceptual distinction.
- For MSE constructs, prefer the **observable phenomenon** (`εμμένουσα απάντηση`, `αυξημένος ρυθμός ομιλίας`) over an uncertain nominal neologism.
- Preserve English in parentheses only when Greek usage is genuinely unstable or the English term itself is a common examiner anchor.

## 3. Detailed adjudication of the 15 CLI research questions

### 3.1 Pressure of speech, pressured speech, logorrhea and rapid speech

**Underlying constructs.** *Pressure of speech* is not simply speech that is fast. It describes increased drive to speak: speech is difficult to interrupt, often forceful, continuous, and subjectively/observably “pressured”, classically in mania. *Logorrhea* is excessive verbal production/verbosity. The two commonly coexist in mania, which explains why some Greek teaching materials pair them, but a patient can be very verbose without having the compelling, difficult-to-interrupt drive implied by pressured speech. *Rapid speech* describes rate only.

**Greek evidence.** Current EAP/KEDIVIM psychopathology teaching explicitly lists `Πίεση στην Ομιλία / Λογόρροια (Pressure of Speech – Logorrhea)`. Greek mood-disorder teaching also uses `αυξημένη ομιλητικότητα ή πίεση λόγου`. This supports `πίεση λόγου` as established Greek and `λογόρροια` as a closely linked clinical term. It does **not** justify replacing every occurrence of *pressure of speech* with `λογόρροια`.

**Adjudication.**
- `Pressure/pressured speech` → **PREFERRED: Πίεση λόγου**; clinical sentence: `ο λόγος είναι πιεστικός, ταχύς και δυσχερώς διακόπτεται`.
- `Logorrhea` → **PREFERRED: Λογόρροια**.
- `Rapid speech` → **PREFERRED: Ταχεία ομιλία / αυξημένος ρυθμός ομιλίας**.
- `Λογοδιάρροια` → **AVOID** in professional house style; colloquial and potentially pejorative.
- `Ταχυλαλία / ταχυφημία` → **CONCEPTUALLY_DISTINCT** in Greek speech pathology because they are used for cluttering/abnormally rapid irregular speech.

**Verdict on current appendix:** `Λογοδιάρροια = pressure of speech` is **incorrect as a preferred one-to-one mapping**.

### 3.2 Circumstantiality, tangentiality and over-detail

*Circumstantiality* is goal-directed but inefficient: excessive unnecessary detail and digressions occur, yet the patient eventually reaches the relevant point. *Tangentiality* fails to return to the question/goal. This distinction is highly viva-useful.

Current Greek academic psychopathology teaching uses `Υπερλεπτομερειακή σκέψη ή Παρεκβατικός Λόγος (Circumstantial Thinking)` and `Εφαπτομενικότητα ή Παράπλευρες απαντήσεις (Tangential Thinking)`. Older Greek psychopathology also uses `περιστασιακότητα` for circumstantiality. That older word is legitimate historical psychiatric vocabulary, but modern Greek linguistics independently uses `περιστασιακότητα` for contextual/situational properties of texts, creating avoidable ambiguity.

**Project decision:**
- Circumstantiality → **PREFERRED: υπερλεπτομερειακή σκέψη / υπερλεπτομερειακός λόγος**; `παρεκβατικός λόγος` acceptable; `λεπτολογική σκέψη` acceptable descriptive variant.
- `Περιστασιακότητα` → **HISTORICAL_EXAM_TERM / recognised variant**, not house default.
- Tangentiality → **PREFERRED: εφαπτομενικότητα**, with `εφαπτομενικός λόγος` acceptable in MSE prose.

Do not translate *circumstantiality* as `περιφραστικότητα` by default: that term can describe wordiness/periphrasis without preserving the psychopathological “eventually reaches the goal” structure.

### 3.3 Derailment, loosening of associations, incoherence and word salad

Greek clinical material often writes `χάλαση συνειρμού, εκτροχιασμός, ασχετολογία, ... ασυναρτησία`, and some explanatory sources explicitly parenthesise `χάλαση των συνειρμών (ή εκτροχιασμός)`. This reflects real overlap in clinical observation. In English descriptive psychopathology, however, *loosening of associations* is broader and *derailment* is one manifestation: discourse slips onto a weakly related or unrelated track.

**Project decision:** retain both entries.
- Derailment → `εκτροχιασμός σκέψης/λόγου`.
- Loosening of associations → `χάλαση/χαλάρωση συνειρμών`.
- Cross-reference: “often overlapping in severe formal thought disorder.”

At greater severity, `ασυναρτησία` is preferred for incoherence. `Σαλάτα λέξεων` is a memorable descriptive/exam term for extreme incoherence. `Σχιζοφασία` should remain a historical/broader term for markedly disorganised schizophrenic language; it is not a strict synonym of word salad and should not be used when the diagnosis is not schizophrenia.

### 3.4 Overinclusiveness

The audit correctly identified a conceptual problem, but the best Greek replacement is not necessarily `υπερεγκλειστικότητα`. Current Greek academic psychopathology uses **`υπερπεριεκτική σκέψη (Over-inclusive thinking)`**. Older Greek medical psychopathology sources also use `υπερπεριεκτική σκέψη`.

Overinclusive thinking refers to overly broad conceptual categories/boundaries and admission of normally irrelevant exemplars or associations. It is not defined by excessive narrative detail. Therefore:
- `Overinclusive thinking` → **PREFERRED: υπερπεριεκτική σκέψη**.
- `Υπερεγκλειστική σκέψη / υπερεγκλειστικότητα` → acceptable but less natural variant.
- `Υπερλεπτομερειακή σκέψη` → **CONCEPTUALLY_DISTINCT** and reserved for circumstantiality/hyper-detail.

### 3.5 Thought blocking and perseveration

Current Greek academic material uses `Ανακοπή Σκέψης` explicitly. It is the clean board and report term. Use `αιφνίδια ανακοπή της ροής της σκέψης/ομιλίας` when defining it. Do not confuse it with the patient’s delusional explanation that a thought was removed; *thought withdrawal* is a content/ownership phenomenon.

For perseveration, current academic material uses `Εμμένουσα Σκέψη (Perseveration)`. In descriptive examination, however, it is often more precise to name the output: `εμμένουσα απάντηση`, `εμμένουσα λεκτική επανάληψη`, `εμμένουσα κινητική αντίδραση`. Bare `εμμονή` is dangerous because Greek clinicians use `εμμονές/ιδεοληψίες` around obsessional phenomena. Rumination is `ιδεομηρυκασμός / μηρυκασμός`, also distinct.

**House rule:** perseveration → `εμμένουσα σκέψη/απάντηση`, not `εμμονή`.

### 3.6 Poverty phenomena and alogia

The current Greek EAP teaching usefully distinguishes:
- `Πενία λόγου / Πτωχεία Σκέψης (Poverty of Speech, Poverty of Thought)`
- `Πενία του Περιεχομένου του Λόγου (Poverty of the content of Speech)`.

The concepts should therefore be separated in Guide v2:
1. **Poverty of speech** — reduced amount/spontaneous output → `πενία λόγου`.
2. **Poverty of content of speech** — adequate quantity but little information → `πενία περιεχομένου του λόγου`.
3. **Poverty of thought** — reduced ideational productivity → `πενία σκέψης / πενία ιδεών`.
4. **Alogia** — broader negative-symptom construct → `αλογία`.

`Ιδεοπενία` is understandable and historically used, but `πενία σκέψης/ιδεών` is more transparent. For orthographic style prefer `πενία` rather than `φτώχεια` or purist `πτωχεία` in canonical entries, while preserving `πτωχεία` as a recognised synonym because it remains common in Greek psychiatric teaching.

### 3.7 Clang associations and language disorganisation

`Ηχητικοί συνειρμοί` is the most transparent contemporary Greek rendering of *clang associations*: associations are driven by sound/rhyme rather than meaning. `Συνειρμοί κατ’ ομοηχία` is precise and elegant in board explanations. `Κλαγγικοί συνειρμοί` should be removed from preferred vocabulary.

For word salad, `σαλάτα λέξεων` is widespread and examiner-recognisable. It should be marked as a descriptive severe-disorganisation term and paired with the more formal `ασυναρτησία / ακατάληπτος λόγος`.

### 3.8 Schneiderian thought alienation

Greek academic and clinical usage is unusually consistent here.

- **Thought insertion:** `παρεμβολή ξένων σκέψεων` is maximally clear; `παρεμβολή σκέψης` shorter. `Επιβολή σκέψης` is also recognisable but can overlap with influence/passivity wording. Avoid `πρόσδοση`.
- **Thought withdrawal:** `απόσυρση της σκέψης` is the preferred neutral form. `Υφαρπαγή σκέψης` is vivid and commonly recognised in older clinical teaching; keep as accepted historical synonym. `Αφαίρεση σκέψης` is understandable.
- **Thought broadcasting:** `εκπομπή της σκέψης` is the strongest board term. `Μετάδοση` or `διάδοση` can clarify but need not replace it.

Use singular generic forms in the glossary (`της σκέψης`), while plural can appear naturally in clinical prose.

### 3.9 Confabulation, pseudomnesia, paramnesia and amnesia direction

The current mapping `Μυθοπλασία / ψευδομνησία = Confabulation` must be split.

**Confabulation** is production of false or distorted memories without deliberate deception, classically filling memory gaps in amnestic/frontal syndromes. Greek `μυθοπλασία` is historically used in psychopathology, but also means fabulation/pathological storytelling in broader Greek psychiatric and general language. Therefore the safest glossary entry is `μυθοπλασία (μνημονική)`, with definition “ασυνείδητη παραγωγή ψευδών αναμνήσεων/συμπλήρωση μνημονικών κενών”.

**Pseudomnesia / false memory** → `ψευδομνησία / ψευδής μνήμη`: a recollection experienced as memory but inaccurate/nonexistent. It need not function as gap filling.

**Paramnesia** → `παραμνησία`: umbrella for qualitative distortions of recognition/recollection (including false familiarity phenomena in many taxonomies). Do not make it synonymous with confabulation.

**Anterograde amnesia.** Greek medical usage varies among `εμπροσθόδρομη`, `εμπροσθοδρομική`, `προσθοδρομική`, and occasionally inaccurate machine-like forms. Choose **`εμπροσθόδρομη αμνησία`** as house style: morphologically clear and well attested in Greek medical use. Preserve `προσθοδρομική` as synonym for recognition.

**Retrograde amnesia.** Choose **`οπισθόδρομη αμνησία`** as house style. `Οπισθοδρομική` and `παλίνδρομη` are recognised. Avoid mixing all variants in one canonical entry.

### 3.10 Affect and mood terminology

Greek psychiatric MSE convention often uses:
- `διάθεση` for the sustained internal mood state;
- `συναίσθημα` for observable moment-to-moment affective expression.

This is not a perfect linguistic correspondence, but it is the most practical project mapping. `Θυμικό` remains a broader/historical clinical term and can be retained as a synonym/recognition term, not the default translation of *affect*.

**Restricted affect**: `περιορισμένο συναίσθημα`; `συσταλμένο` acceptable. Older Greek psychiatry uses `περιεσφιγμένο/περίσφιγξη`, and this is not literally “wrong” inside that tradition. Nonetheless, it is a poor house choice because outside psychiatry `περίσφιξη` strongly denotes constriction/strangulation and obscures the graded affect continuum.

**Blunted affect**: `αμβλύ συναίσθημα / συναισθηματική άμβλυνση`.

**Flat affect**: `επίπεδο συναίσθημα / συναισθηματική επιπέδωση`.

These should remain graded descriptions rather than a single slash entry.

**Inappropriate affect**: `απρόσφορο συναίσθημα` is deeply established in Greek psychiatry and appears in Hellenic Psychiatric Association material. It means affect that is inappropriate to accompanying thought/content/context.

**Mood incongruence**: `ασύμφωνο / μη σύντονο προς τη διάθεση`. This is conceptually different: a psychotic belief or affect can be incongruent with the dominant mood without being globally “inappropriate” in the MSE sense.

### 3.11 Catatonia: stupor, ambitendency, catalepsy, waxy flexibility, posturing

`Εμβροντησία` remains highly recognisable in Greek psychiatric education and current clinical teaching for *stupor*. It should **not** be removed merely because it sounds classical. For boards it is arguably preferable to an English loanword. A modern definition should accompany it: profound reduction/absence of psychomotor activity and active response while not simply unconscious.

**Ambitendency** is much harder. It is a historical catatonic sign: the motor tendency to initiate and oppose/withdraw the same act, producing hesitant alternating movement. It is not Bleulerian emotional ambivalence. Greek older literature contains overlapping words `αμφιβουλησία`, `αμφιπραξία`, `κινητική αμφιτιμία`, `αμφιρρέπεια`; contemporary diagnostic systems do not require the term. For this project:
- PREFERRED for recognition: **`κινητική αμφιταλάντευση (ambitendency)`**.
- Accepted historical: `αμφιβουλησία` / `αμφιπραξία` where the motor meaning is explicit.
- AVOID: `αμφισθένεια`, `αμφιθυμία` as direct translations of ambitendency.
- Status: **CONTESTED / HISTORICAL_EXAM_TERM**.

**Catalepsy** → `καταληψία`: passive placement of a posture retained against gravity.

**Waxy flexibility** → `κηρώδης ευκαμψία`: slight even resistance to passive positioning followed by maintenance.

**Posturing** → `κατατονική στάση`, or in explanatory prose `αυθόρμητη λήψη και διατήρηση στάσης`. Do not translate posturing as catalepsy; the former is spontaneous/active, the latter is induced.

### 3.12 Speech-rate terms and prosody

Greek neurological/speech-language terminology creates a trap. Speech-language sources explicitly use `ταχυλαλία`, `ταχυφημία`, `βατταρισμός`, `ταχυαρρυθμία` for **cluttering**: rapid and irregular speech with fluency/organisation/articulation abnormalities. Therefore these must not be mapped automatically to psychiatric *rapid speech* or *pressured speech*.

Project mapping:
- rapid speech → `ταχεία ομιλία / αυξημένος ρυθμός ομιλίας`;
- pressured speech → `πίεση λόγου`;
- cluttering → `βατταρισμός / ταχυλαλία` (speech-language term);
- slow speech → `βραδεία ομιλία / επιβραδυνμένος ρυθμός ομιλίας`;
- avoid `βραδυφημία` as routine psychiatric MSE translation unless the source is explicitly discussing speech-rate pathology.

**Prosody:**
- dysprosody → `δυσπροσωδία`, any abnormality of prosodic modulation;
- aprosodia → `απροσωδία`, absent/severely reduced emotional or linguistic prosody.

The current slash mapping `Απροσωδία / δυσπροσωδία = Dysprosody` is therefore conceptually imprecise and must be split.

### 3.13 Perception: cenesthetic hallucinations, pseudohallucinations and delusional misidentification

Greek academic psychopathology explicitly lists `Απτικές, κοιναισθητικές και σπλαγχνικές ψευδαισθήσεις` as distinguishable categories.

- **Cenesthetic hallucination** → `κοιναισθητική ψευδαίσθηση`: bizarre/global bodily experiences (changes in body shape, inner transformation, unusual bodily states).
- **Somatic hallucination** → `σωματική / σωματοαισθητική ψευδαίσθηση`: broad bodily percept without stimulus.
- **Visceral hallucination** → `σπλαγχνική ψευδαίσθηση`: perceived internal-organ sensation/event.

These overlap phenomenologically but should not be collapsed in the glossary.

**Pseudohallucination.** Greek academic programmes still teach the distinction (`ψευδο-ψευδαισθήσεις`), so the term should remain for board recognition. Contemporary descriptive psychopathology questions its reliability as a categorical distinction; insight, internal/external localisation and sensory vividness vary continuously. House entry: `ψευδοψευδαίσθηση`, status `CONTESTED / HISTORICAL_EXAM_TERM`; instruct Writer to define the phenomenology rather than treating preserved insight as proof of a “pseudo” phenomenon.

**Delusional misidentification.** `Παραληρητική παραγνώριση` is recognisable. Older Greek psychopathology also uses `παραληρητικές ψευδοαναγνωρίσεις`. Preferred project phrase: `παραληρητική παραγνώριση`, with `σύνδρομο παραληρητικής εσφαλμένης αναγνώρισης` if a more explicit formulation is needed. It belongs under **thought content/delusional syndromes**, not under perception merely because recognition is involved.

### 3.14 Insight and judgment

`Εναισθησία` is unequivocally established in Greek psychiatry. Greek academic and clinical material uses it for awareness/recognition of psychiatric illness and need for treatment. It should be the canonical `Insight` mapping.

For better contemporary precision, the final guide should add subdimensions:
- recognition that experiences/symptoms are abnormal;
- recognition/acceptance of having an illness or mental-health problem;
- attribution/explanatory model;
- recognition of need for treatment/help;
- consequences for adherence and risk.

`Νοητική/διανοητική εναισθησία` vs `συναισθηματική/βιωματική εναισθησία` is a classic psychopathology distinction: a patient may intellectually acknowledge illness without emotionally integrating that knowledge. It remains useful for examination recognition, but contemporary clinical notes are clearer when they state the actual dimension rather than assigning a two-category label.

`Κρίση` is the canonical `judgment`. It means ability to evaluate situations and consequences and make sensible decisions in the MSE. It is **not** synonymous with `ικανότητα για συναίνεση`, legal/clinical decision-making capacity, `εναισθησία`, or `έλεγχος της πραγματικότητας`. These concepts can correlate but must remain separate entries.

### 3.15 Orthography and house-style adjudication

**Πενία vs πτωχεία vs φτώχεια.** All are understandable. `Πενία` is concise and professional, avoids katharevousa feel and colloquial register, and already appears in current academic psychopathology. House style: **πενία**. Preserve `πτωχεία` as accepted academic variant; avoid `φτώχεια` in canonical glossary labels although it is not clinically wrong.

**Ηχολαλία vs ηχωλαλία; ηχοπραξία vs ηχωπραξία.** Both orthographies circulate. Greek Language Portal and many psychiatric sources use `ηχολαλία`; catatonia teaching also commonly uses `ηχολαλία/ηχοπραξία`. House style: **ηχολαλία, ηχοπραξία**. Record `ηχωλαλία/ηχωπραξία` as recognised spelling variants, not parallel canonical forms.

**Anterograde / retrograde.** House style: **εμπροσθόδρομη αμνησία / οπισθόδρομη αμνησία**. Accepted: εμπροσθοδρομική, προσθοδρομική / οπισθοδρομική, παλίνδρομη.

**Pseudohallucination spelling.** House style: **ψευδοψευδαίσθηση** (closed compound). `Ψευδο-ψευδαίσθηση` can be retained only when quoting a source/title; do not mix both throughout the guide.

**Disinhibition.** House style: **άρση αναστολών** when describing behaviour in ordinary Greek clinical prose; `αποαναστολή` is an accepted technical neuropsychological/behavioural term and useful as a synonym. Avoid treating them as different constructs.

## 4. Critical corrections to the current guide

The following mappings should change in Translation Guide v2.

1. `Λογοδιάρροια = pressure of speech` → **replace** with `Πίεση λόγου = pressured/pressure of speech`; retain separate `Λογόρροια = logorrhea`.
2. `Ταχυλαλία/ταχυφημία = rapid or pressured speech` → **remove from psychiatric MSE mapping**; reserve for cluttering/speech-rate pathology. Use `ταχεία ομιλία` for rapid speech.
3. `Περιστασιακή λεπτολογία = circumstantiality` → **standardise** to `Υπερλεπτομερειακή σκέψη/λόγος`; retain `περιστασιακότητα` and `παρεκβατικός λόγος` as variants.
4. `Εφαπτομενικός λόγος` → keep, but make canonical noun **`Εφαπτομενικότητα`**.
5. `Υπερλεπτομερειακή σκέψη = overinclusive thought` → **critical correction** to `Υπερπεριεκτική σκέψη`.
6. `Μπλοκάρισμα σκέψης` or `Απόφραξη σκέψης` → canonical **`Ανακοπή σκέψης`**.
7. `Εμμονή στη σκέψη = perseveration` → **replace** with `Εμμένουσα σκέψη/απάντηση` to avoid obsession confusion.
8. `Ιδεοπενία / πτωχή σκέψη / φτώχεια λόγου` mixed entry → **split** into poverty of thought, poverty of speech and poverty of content of speech; retain `Αλογία` separately.
9. `Κλαγγικοί συνειρμοί` → **replace** with `Ηχητικοί συνειρμοί` / `Συνειρμοί κατ’ ομοηχία`.
10. `Word salad = σχιζοφασία` → **separate**. `Σαλάτα λέξεων` is the direct descriptive term; `σχιζοφασία` is broader/historical.
11. `Πρόσδοση σκέψεων = thought insertion` → **drop**; use `Παρεμβολή ξένων σκέψεων`.
12. `Αναμετάδοση σκέψης = thought broadcasting` → demote; use **`Εκπομπή της σκέψης`**.
13. `Μυθοπλασία / ψευδομνησία = confabulation` → **split completely**.
14. Anterograde amnesia multiple inconsistent forms → house standard **`Εμπροσθόδρομη αμνησία`**.
15. Retrograde amnesia multiple inconsistent forms → house standard **`Οπισθόδρομη αμνησία`**.
16. `Συναισθηματική περίσφιξη = restricted affect` → canonical **`Περιορισμένο συναίσθημα`**; preserve περίσφιξη only as older recognised variant.
17. `Απρόσφορο / ασύμφωνο συναίσθημα = incongruent affect` → **split** inappropriate (`απρόσφορο`) from mood/content incongruence (`ασύμφωνο/μη σύντονο`).
18. `Αμφιτιμία / αμφισθένεια = ambitendency` → **remove**. Use `κινητική αμφιταλάντευση (ambitendency)` with historical `αμφιβουλησία/αμφιπραξία`.
19. `Απροσωδία / δυσπροσωδία = dysprosody` → **split** into aprosodia vs dysprosodia.
20. `Κοιναισθητική = somatic/visceral hallucination` blanket mapping → **separate common bodily/cenesthetic, somatic and visceral categories**.
21. `Ψευδο-ψευδαισθήσεις` → orthographic house form **`ψευδοψευδαισθήσεις`** and status `CONTESTED/HISTORICAL_EXAM_TERM`.
22. `Παραληρητική παραγνώριση` under Perception → **move** to Thought Content / Delusional Misidentification.
23. `Insight` alternatives → canonical **`Εναισθησία`**; keep dimensions rather than vague synonyms.
24. `Judgment = capacity` style overlap → **separate** `Κρίση` from `ικανότητα για συναίνεση/λήψη συγκεκριμένης απόφασης`.
25. `Ηχολαλία / ηχωλαλία` and `Ηχοπραξία / ηχωπραξία` slash forms → canonical **`ηχολαλία`, `ηχοπραξία`** with variants in notes only.

## 5. Historical / contested terms

### 5.1 Εμβροντησία
Retain. Although classical in register, it remains routinely recognisable in Greek psychiatric and medical-school teaching. Label as `HISTORICAL_EXAM_TERM` only to signal register—not obsolescence. In a Greek oral exam it is often more natural than transliterated `στούπορ`.

### 5.2 Περίσφιξη / περιεσφιγμένο συναίσθημα
This is genuinely attested Greek psychiatric vocabulary, including teaching materials that distinguish `περιεσφιγμένο ή περιορισμένο` from `αμβλύ` and `επίπεδο`. The CLI audit’s statement that it is simply a “severe mistranslation” is too categorical. It is semantically awkward outside psychiatry, so Guide v2 should prefer `περιορισμένο συναίσθημα`, but **do not mark περίσφιξη as fake/nonpsychiatric**.

### 5.3 Περιστασιακότητα
Attested in older Greek psychopathology for circumstantiality, but semantically ambiguous in modern Greek. Keep as recognition-only; prefer `υπερλεπτομερειακή σκέψη` for board/clinical clarity.

### 5.4 Σχιζοφασία
Recognised historical Greek psychopathology term for severe language disorganisation in schizophrenia. Do not use as a generic synonym for incoherence in delirium, mania or aphasia.

### 5.5 Αμφισθένεια
Historically linked with Bleulerian ambivalence. It should not be used for motor ambitendency. `Αμφιθυμία` is the current more intelligible ambivalence term.

### 5.6 Ambitendency terminology
No single Greek form has achieved stable contemporary standardisation. Because the English term itself is now mainly historical/descriptive and not central to modern catatonia criteria, the safest project form is `κινητική αμφιταλάντευση (ambitendency)`, with `αμφιβουλησία/αμφιπραξία` noted as historical variants.

### 5.7 Ψευδοψευδαισθήσεις
Retain for exam recognition because Greek academic psychopathology still teaches the distinction. Mark as contested and never use the label to infer diagnostic category mechanically. Describe phenomenology.

### 5.8 Intellectual versus emotional insight
Keep as historical/exam language. Contemporary clinical documentation should instead state what the patient recognises, attributes and accepts regarding symptoms, illness and treatment need.

### 5.9 Gastaut-style classical eponyms and obsolete syndrome labels
If these appear elsewhere in Translation.docx, retain only when the 100-question product explicitly tests historical recognition. Do not let historical descriptive psychopathology drive current clinical wording.

## 8. Remaining unresolved terminology

### UNRESOLVED 1 — single canonical Greek for ambitendency
No current high-authority Greek source located establishes one modern canonical translation. `Κινητική αμφιταλάντευση` is the safest descriptive house wording; `αμφιβουλησία/αμφιπραξία` should be indexed for recognition. Do not present any one of these as an official ICD/DSM term.

### UNRESOLVED 2 — exact preferred Greek lexicalisation of confabulation across all Greek psychiatry schools
`Μυθοπλασία` is historically and lexically established, but it is polysemous and can denote pathological fabrication/storytelling outside amnestic syndromes. The project should therefore keep `μυθοπλασία (confabulation)` only with a memory-specific definition. If a future authorised Greek neuropsychiatry text consistently prefers `παραμνησιακή μυθοπλασία` or another compound, revisit.

### UNRESOLVED 3 — strict national orthographic preference for anterograde amnesia
Greek medical literature variably uses `εμπροσθόδρομη`, `εμπροσθοδρομική`, `προσθοδρομική` and other forms. This is stylistic, not conceptual. House style can safely standardise to `εμπροσθόδρομη`.

### UNRESOLVED 4 — whether `περίσφιξη συναισθήματος` should be classified historical versus acceptable contemporary variant
It remains genuinely used in Greek psychiatric teaching and cannot be called erroneous. The project should simply prefer `περιορισμένο συναίσθημα` for clarity.

### UNRESOLVED 5 — exact scope of `σχιζοφασία`
Greek sources use it variably for severe formal thought/language disorganisation in schizophrenia. It should stay recognition-only and never be the sole canonical equivalent of an English descriptive sign.

### UNRESOLVED 6 — official Greek ICD-11 lexical standardisation
WHO ICD-11 is authoritative conceptually, but a comprehensive, officially promulgated Greek clinical-description terminology set was not identified during this pass. Where an official Greek ICD-11 translation later becomes available, diagnostic-label entries should be mechanically reconciled without reopening stable descriptive psychopathology terms unless the official form directly conflicts.

## 9. Writer directives for Translation Guide v2

1. **Never merge two English constructs merely because Greek clinicians sometimes use one shorthand for both.** Preserve the conceptual distinction first; then note overlap.
2. Use `Πίεση λόγου` for *pressured speech* and `Λογόρροια` for *logorrhea*; do not use `λογοδιάρροια` as canonical terminology.
3. Use `ταχεία ομιλία` for psychiatric rapid speech; reserve `ταχυλαλία/ταχυφημία` for cluttering/speech-pathology contexts.
4. Distinguish circumstantiality (`υπερλεπτομερειακή σκέψη`, eventual endpoint) from tangentiality (`εφαπτομενικότητα`, endpoint not reached).
5. Map overinclusive thinking to `υπερπεριεκτική σκέψη`; never to `υπερλεπτομερειακή σκέψη`.
6. Keep `εκτροχιασμός` and `χάλαση/χαλάρωση συνειρμών` as separate, cross-referenced entries rather than forced synonyms.
7. Use `ανακοπή σκέψης` for thought blocking.
8. Use `εμμένουσα σκέψη/απάντηση` for perseveration; never use bare `εμμονή` when obsession could be inferred.
9. Create separate entries for `πενία λόγου`, `πενία περιεχομένου του λόγου`, `πενία σκέψης`, and `αλογία`.
10. Use `ηχητικοί συνειρμοί` for clang associations; optionally explain `κατ’ ομοηχία`.
11. Keep `σαλάτα λέξεων` as severe incoherence/exam-recognition terminology; do not equate it automatically with `σχιζοφασία`.
12. Use Schneiderian forms `παρεμβολή ξένων σκέψεων`, `απόσυρση της σκέψης`, `εκπομπή της σκέψης` as the canonical triad.
13. Split confabulation, pseudomnesia and paramnesia into separate entries with short concept definitions.
14. Standardise amnesia direction to `εμπροσθόδρομη` and `οπισθόδρομη`, with other forms searchable aliases only.
15. In MSE material, map *mood* to `διάθεση` and *affect* primarily to `συναίσθημα`/`συναισθηματική έκφραση`; do not pretend the Greek lexicon offers an exact one-word structural mirror.
16. Preserve the graded affect distinctions: `περιορισμένο` → `αμβλύ` → `επίπεδο`, without implying a rigid quantitative scale where the clinical source does not.
17. Use `απρόσφορο συναίσθημα` for inappropriate affect and `ασύμφωνο/μη σύντονο προς τη διάθεση` for mood incongruence.
18. Retain `εμβροντησία` as a board-recognition term for stupor; define it in modern language.
19. For ambitendency, write `κινητική αμφιταλάντευση (ambitendency)` and index `αμφιβουλησία/αμφιπραξία`; explicitly state that `αμφιθυμία/αμφισθένεια` are ambivalence terms.
20. Keep `καταληψία`, `κηρώδης ευκαμψία`, and `κατατονική στάση/posturing` as three separate catatonic signs.
21. Split `απροσωδία` from `δυσπροσωδία`.
22. Split `κοιναισθητική`, `σωματική`, and `σπλαγχνική ψευδαίσθηση`; use cross-references rather than synonym slashes.
23. Retain `ψευδοψευδαίσθηση` only as `CONTESTED / HISTORICAL_EXAM_TERM`; contemporary clinical notes should describe phenomenology instead of relying on the label.
24. Move `παραληρητική παραγνώριση` to Thought Content / Delusional Syndromes.
25. Use `εναισθησία` as the canonical insight term and specify dimensions when clinically relevant.
26. Use `κρίση` for MSE judgment. Never use it as a synonym for decision-specific capacity or valid consent.
27. Use canonical orthography `ηχολαλία`, `ηχοπραξία`, `ψευδοψευδαίσθηση`, and `πενία`; list variants once in a synonym field, not throughout body text.
28. Use `άρση αναστολών` as the default prose term for disinhibition; index `αποαναστολή` as a technical synonym.
29. Tag each glossary entry explicitly as **preferred**, **accepted variant**, **historical/exam**, **contested**, or **conceptually distinct**; never use an unexplained slash between non-equivalent terms.
30. When a Greek term has no stable consensus, preserve the English term in parentheses and describe the phenomenon rather than inventing lexical certainty.

## Sources actually inspected / compared

### Greek academic / professional terminology
1. **Ελληνικό Ανοικτό Πανεπιστήμιο, ΚΕΔΙΒΙΜ — «Εισαγωγή στην Κλινική Ψυχοπαθολογία»**, current programme (2024–2026 cycles), scientific lead Prof. Petros Skapinakis. The study-guide indexing was especially useful for formal thought/speech terms: `Υπερλεπτομερειακή σκέψη / Παρεκβατικός Λόγος`, `Εφαπτομενικότητα`, `Πίεση στην Ομιλία / Λογόρροια`, `Πενία λόγου`, `Πενία περιεχομένου`, `Ανακοπή σκέψης`, `Εμμένουσα Σκέψη`, `Υπερπεριεκτική σκέψη`, and thought alienation.
2. **Greek medical-school teaching materials associated with Aristotle University of Thessaloniki**, used only as corroborating usage evidence for `εμβροντησία`, `λογόρροια`, `εκτροχιασμός`, `λεπτολογία`, catatonic terms and Greek MSE structure.
3. **Journal Ψυχιατρική / Hellenic Psychiatric Association**, Greek-language clinical articles, used to confirm established professional forms such as `αμβλύ συναίσθημα`, `απρόσφορο συναίσθημα`, `εναισθησία` and ordinary Greek psychiatric register.
4. **Greek Language Portal / Centre for the Greek Language**, including academic material on psychopathological language, used for `ηχολαλία`, `σχιζοφασία` and language terminology.
5. **Academy of Athens — Χρηστικό Λεξικό της Νεοελληνικής Γλώσσας**, used cautiously for lexical status of terms such as `μυθοπλασία`, not as authority for psychopathological construct boundaries.
6. Greek medical/psychiatric textbook material indexed online, including older descriptive psychopathology usage of `υπερπεριεκτική σκέψη`, `περιστασιακότητα`, `ανακοπή σκέψης`, `παραμνησίες`, `εμβροντησία`, `αμφιβουλησία`, and `απροσωδία/δυσπροσωδία`.
7. Greek speech-language pathology sources used specifically to prevent psychiatric misuse of `ταχυλαλία/ταχυφημία`: these sources map the terms to `βατταρισμός / cluttering` and therefore support keeping them distinct from pressured speech.

### Authoritative English construct checking
8. *Oxford / major descriptive psychopathology frameworks* used conceptually to maintain distinctions among circumstantiality, tangentiality, derailment, loosening, poverty phenomena, thought alienation, hallucination subtypes and insight/judgment.
9. Current catatonia diagnostic/consensus frameworks used to distinguish stupor, catalepsy, waxy flexibility and posturing and to recognise ambitendency as primarily historical rather than a core modern diagnostic criterion.
10. Current descriptive-psychopathology literature on pseudohallucinations used to justify retaining the term for recognition but downgrading its categorical diagnostic authority.

### Source-strength note
The EAP/KEDIVIM psychopathology programme is the strongest directly accessible contemporary Greek academic terminology source located in this pass. Individual websites, uploaded lecture notes and online textbook indexes were used only to corroborate actual Greek clinical usage, not to override conceptual definitions from authoritative psychopathology. Where usage remained genuinely inconsistent—especially ambitendency, confabulation lexicalisation and amnesia morphology—the report explicitly retains `UNRESOLVED`/variant status instead of manufacturing a national standard.
