# Model-Answer Archetypes

These are composition patterns, not rigid templates. The dossier assigns one primary archetype and may borrow a secondary pattern when necessary.

## 1. Disorder / clinical entity

Use for: schizophrenia, MDD, OCD, PTSD, BPD, Alzheimer's disease, etc.

Typical oral movement:

1. Define/frame the disorder and the diagnostic centre of gravity.
2. Give the characteristic clinical picture/psychopathology.
3. State the key diagnostic framework and difficult differentials.
4. Address risk/medical exclusion/investigations when clinically relevant.
5. Give treatment in sequence rather than as a drug list.
6. Close with maintenance/prognosis/monitoring only if important to the question.

Avoid:
- reciting every criterion before explaining what the syndrome looks like;
- treating epidemiology/aetiology/treatment as equal-weight textbook headings;
- long treatment lists without sequencing.

## 2. Clinical presentation / emergency

Use for: agitation, catatonia, delirium, suicidal crisis, medically compromised anorexia, overdose.

Typical oral movement:

1. State the immediate clinical priority and why the presentation is dangerous.
2. Stabilise safety/ABC/containment as applicable.
3. Define the syndrome using discriminating findings.
4. Search for causes and dangerous mimics.
5. Give immediate investigations and treatment in practical order.
6. State disposition, monitoring and what would make management escalate.

The examiner should hear safety before sophistication.

## 3. Treatment / longitudinal management

Use for: acute depression treatment, schizophrenia maintenance, TRD, bipolar maintenance, alcohol relapse prevention.

Typical oral movement:

1. Confirm diagnosis/severity/risk and relevant prior treatment.
2. State first-line strategy and the factors that choose among options.
3. Define what counts as an adequate trial and how response is assessed.
4. State what happens after partial response/non-response.
5. Integrate psychotherapy/rehabilitation/physical health where relevant.
6. Close with continuation/maintenance/relapse prevention/monitoring.

The answer must show **sequence and decision points**, not merely available interventions.

## 4. Differential / discrimination question

Use for: schizophrenia vs schizoaffective, DLB vs PDD, apathy vs depression, NMS vs serotonin syndrome.

Typical oral movement:

1. State the most discriminating principle first.
2. Compare phenomenology and time course.
3. Compare context/causes/associated findings.
4. State investigations or collateral information that resolve uncertainty.
5. Explain why the distinction matters for treatment/risk.

Prefer a few high-discrimination features over a long side-by-side catalogue.

## 5. Drug / drug-class question

Use for: clozapine, lithium, valproate, antipsychotic selection, antidepressant selection.

Typical oral movement:

1. Place the drug/class in treatment: why and when it is used.
2. State the main indications and important limitations.
3. Explain practical initiation/use only to the level required by the question.
4. Cover major monitoring and common/serious adverse effects.
5. Cover the interactions/toxicities/special populations most likely to change practice.
6. Close with the key counselling or safety principle.

Exact numerical details normally belong in the **board-fact block**, unless essential to answering the spoken question.

## 6. Psychotherapy / psychological intervention

Use for: CBT, IPT, psychoeducation, psychodynamic concepts.

Typical oral movement:

1. Define the model/intervention clearly.
2. Explain its central formulation or therapeutic mechanism.
3. Describe the structure/techniques sufficiently to show practical understanding.
4. State principal psychiatric indications.
5. Mention selection, limitations, adaptations or evidence only where discriminating.
6. For relational concepts, give a brief clinical example if it clarifies the concept.

Avoid turning psychotherapy answers into generic statements about empathy/support.

## 7. Foundational science / mechanism

Use for: schizophrenia neurobiology, dopamine pathways, neuroanatomical circuits, genetics, sleep physiology.

Typical oral movement:

1. State the organising model.
2. Explain the strongest established findings/mechanisms.
3. Connect them to observable clinical phenomena or treatment.
4. Distinguish established evidence from hypotheses/associations.
5. End with limitations: psychiatry should not be reduced to a single pathway/biomarker.

The answer should show clinical relevance, not become a basic-science lecture.

## 8. Evidence / appraisal

Use for: deciding whether a study should change practice.

Typical oral movement:

1. Ask whether the question/design is appropriate.
2. Assess internal validity: bias, confounding, randomisation/blinding where relevant.
3. Interpret effect size, precision and clinically meaningful metrics.
4. Assess applicability to the patient/population.
5. Balance benefits, harms, feasibility and consistency with the wider evidence.

Calculations such as NNT/NNH can sit in the board-fact block unless directly asked.

## 9. Law / ethics / forensic / systems

Use for: involuntary admission, confidentiality, criminal responsibility, community care.

Typical oral movement:

1. State the governing clinical/legal/ethical principle.
2. Identify the **Greek** legal or service framework where jurisdiction matters.
3. Apply it to the actual decision/problem.
4. Address proportionality, least-restrictive practice, documentation and review.
5. Distinguish clinical from legal/expert roles where relevant.

Never import UK/US statutory tests or service structures as the default Greek answer.

# Composition rules shared by all archetypes

## The opening must do work

Weak opening:

`Lithium is a mood stabiliser used in psychiatry.`

Better opening pattern:

`Lithium is a first-line maintenance treatment for bipolar disorder with particular value in preventing manic and depressive relapse and a distinctive anti-suicidal evidence base; its use is limited by a narrow therapeutic index, so safe prescribing depends on serum-level interpretation, renal/thyroid monitoring and interaction awareness.`

The opening should compress the answer's hierarchy without becoming overloaded.

## Organise by decisions, not textbook headings

Prefer:

`First I would confirm... If severe... Once stabilised... If there is no response...`

Over:

`Definition. Epidemiology. Aetiology. Diagnosis. Treatment. Prognosis.`

## Use enumeration selectively

Lists are appropriate when the examiner expects a finite set (e.g. EPS syndromes, dopamine pathways, sleep stages). They are poor substitutes for reasoning in management questions.

## Make high-risk information audible

Dangerous alternatives, contraindications, urgent complications and monitoring should not be hidden in the final sentence.

## Distinguish certainty

Use language such as:

- `established`
- `supported`
- `associated with`
- `proposed`
- `historically emphasised`
- `current guidance differs`

when the evidence level matters.

## Do not force completeness into the spoken core

The model oral answer should contain what earns oral-exam value. The board-fact block exists specifically to prevent exact recall material from making the spoken answer unreadable.

```markdown
# QXX. [Question]

## Model oral answer

[Orally natural prose. Opening functions as short answer; later paragraphs deepen it.]

## Must-know board facts

- [Only verified high-yield facts assigned to this question]

## Examiner follow-ups

### [follow-up]
[concise answer]

## Exam answer vs current practice

[Include only if a meaningful discrepancy exists.]
```

The final publication may alter typography, but the semantic components should remain stable.
