## Q28. Panic disorder and agoraphobia

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Recognise recurrent panic attacks with abrupt crescendo of intense autonomic/cognitive fear symptoms.
- Distinguish panic attacks from panic disorder by persistent concern/behavioural change and from medical/substance causes.
- Identify anticipatory anxiety and avoidance.
- Recognise agoraphobic fear/avoidance of situations where escape/help may be difficult.
- Differential includes cardiac, respiratory, endocrine, neurological and substance/medication causes plus other anxiety disorders.
- Treatment principles: CBT with cognitive/exposure elements and antidepressant pharmacotherapy.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Panic attack phenomenology and anticipatory anxiety.
- Agoraphobic avoidance pattern.
- Medical/substance differential.
- CBT/exposure and SSRI/SNRI principles.

### Requires current verification / update
- Panic/agoraphobia classification architecture in ICD-11/DSM-5-TR.
- Any old panic-with/without-agoraphobia formulation.
- Current treatment sequencing.

## Q29. Generalized anxiety disorder

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Excessive pervasive worry across domains with tension/autonomic/cognitive symptoms.
- Chronicity, distress and impairment matter.
- Differential from depression, panic, OCD, health anxiety, substances and medical causes.
- CBT and antidepressant pharmacotherapy are core treatments; pregabalin appears as an option in the source.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Pervasive worry phenotype.
- Associated motor/autonomic/cognitive symptoms.
- CBT and SSRI/SNRI treatment principles.

### Requires current verification / update
- Whether a strict 6-month rule is universal under ICD-11.
- Current stepped-care hierarchy and pregabalin positioning.

## Q30. Social anxiety disorder

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Fear of scrutiny/negative evaluation in social or performance situations.
- Avoidance and functional impairment.
- Distinguish from normal shyness, panic/agoraphobia, autism and avoidant personality disorder.
- CBT/exposure and antidepressant pharmacotherapy are core treatments.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve

- Avoidant-PD differential.
- CBT/exposure and SSRI principles.

### Requires current verification / update

### Base-source answer skeleton

- Assess insight and impairment.
- ERP-based CBT and SSRIs are core treatments.
- Clomipramine and antipsychotic augmentation are later/resistant options.

## Q32. Post-traumatic stress disorder

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Trauma-linked syndrome with re-experiencing, avoidance, hyperarousal/current-threat phenomena and negative/numbing features.
- Differentiate from acute stress responses, depression, anxiety, adjustment disorder and substance use.
- Trauma-focused CBT and EMDR are core treatments; medication has a secondary role.
- Routine debriefing was discouraged in the source.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Trauma linkage and core phenomenology.
- Trauma-focused CBT and EMDR.
- Differential architecture.
- Avoid routine psychological debriefing.

### Requires current verification / update
- ICD-11 compact PTSD architecture.
- ICD-11 Complex PTSD.
- Current medication positioning and debriefing recommendation.

## Q33. Acute stress disorder versus adjustment disorder

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Distinguish by nature of stressor/trauma, symptom pattern, timing and course.
- ASD is trauma-specific in DSM-style teaching and occupies the immediate post-trauma period.
- Adjustment disorder follows an identifiable stressor and presents with maladaptive emotional/behavioural response without the full trauma-specific syndrome.
- Distinguish both from normal stress response and PTSD.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Trauma versus broader stressor distinction.
- Timing/course as a key discriminator.
- Adjustment-disorder symptom burden/impairment.

### Requires current verification / update
- DSM-5-TR ASD timing and symptom architecture.
- ICD-11 Acute Stress Reaction status.
- ICD-11 adjustment disorder architecture.

## Q34. Dissociative disorders

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Cover depersonalization, derealization, dissociative amnesia, fugue and DID at a high-yield level.
- Preserve reality testing in depersonalization/derealization.
- Assess trauma/context but also exclude neurological, epileptic, substance and sleep-related causes.

- Temporal-lobe epilepsy/neurological differential.

## Q35. Somatic symptom disorder versus illness anxiety disorder

### Base-source answer skeleton
- SSD: prominent bodily symptoms plus excessive distress/preoccupation/behaviour.
- IAD: illness preoccupation with absent/minimal bodily symptoms.
- Genuine medical illness can coexist.
- Differentiate from delusional disorder, anxiety/depression, FND and medical disease.
- Management emphasizes continuity, proportionate investigation and collaborative biopsychosocial formulation.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Symptom burden versus illness-preoccupation distinction.
- Avoid unnecessary repetitive investigation while still reassessing new red flags.
- Treat comorbidity and maintain continuity.

### Requires current verification / update
- DSM-5-TR versus ICD-11 architecture.
- ICD-11 bodily distress disorder and hypochondriasis/health anxiety.
- Removal of “medically unexplained symptoms” as a required basis.

## Q36. Functional neurological disorder

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Recognise functional weakness, movement/gait, sensory and seizure-like presentations.
- Diagnose using positive internal inconsistency/incongruity signs rather than exclusion alone.
- Use appropriate neurological differential and investigations.
- Treatment is multidisciplinary: explanation, physiotherapy/rehabilitation and psychological treatment where indicated.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Hoover sign and tremor entrainment as examples of positive signs.
- Positive-diagnosis model.
- Multidisciplinary rehabilitation.

### Requires current verification / update
- Modern rule-in criteria/consensus framing.
- Psychological stress/trauma not being required.
- Current functional-seizure terminology and multidisciplinary treatment principles.

### Q36a. Explaining FND
- Validate symptoms as real and involuntary.
- Explain positive signs and preserved capacity.
- Avoid implying fabrication or “all psychological.”
- A software-versus-hardware analogy may be used if helpful but should not become dogma.

### Q36b. Factitious disorder versus malingering versus FND
- FND symptoms are involuntary.
- Factitious disorder involves intentional falsification/induction linked to presenting as ill, without obvious external reward.
- Malingering involves intentional production/exaggeration for external incentives and is not itself a psychiatric disorder.
- Do not infer intent from unusual phenomenology alone.
