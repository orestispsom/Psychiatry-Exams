## 2. Reusable chapter map

### 2.1 Clinical phenomenology & core syndromes

#### Adult ADHD (Ch. 16, pp. 450–454)
- **Core Triad & Executive Dysfunction:** Persistent pattern of:
  1. *Inattention:* Short attention span, difficulty sustaining focus during lengthy tasks, chronic disorganization, failure to finish projects, losing essential items, forgetfulness, high distractibility.
  2. *Hyperactivity:* Inner restlessness, inability to sit still for prolonged periods, fidgeting, excessive talking, subjective feeling of being driven by a motor.
  3. *Impulsivity:* Interrupting others, blurting out answers, hasty decision-making without considering consequences, severe impatience, driving hazards.
  4. *Executive Dysfunction:* Time blindness, severe procrastination, task initiation paralysis, emotional dysregulation.
- **Developmental Requirement & Setting Rules:** Onset of symptoms during childhood (ICD-10 requires onset before age 7; DSM-5 before age 12). Symptoms must cause significant functional impairment across **≥2 settings** (home, occupational, academic, social). Symptoms must not be better explained by another psychiatric condition.

#### Adult Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) (Ch. 16, pp. 445–450)
- **Core Dyad:** Lifelong neurodevelopmental pattern characterized by:
  1. *Social Communication & Interaction Deficits:* Impaired social-emotional reciprocity (difficulty with back-and-forth conversation, reduced sharing of interests), deficits in non-verbal communication (eye contact, facial expressions, body language), difficulty developing, maintaining, and understanding relationships.
  2. *Restricted, Repetitive Patterns of Behaviour & Interests:* Stereotyped motor mannerisms or speech (echolalia), insistence on sameness / rigid adherence to non-functional routines, highly fixated interests of abnormal intensity or focus, sensory hyper- or hypo-reactivity (aversion to textures, sounds, or intense fascination with visual lights/movement).
- **Assessment Approach:** Comprehensive developmental history from an informant (parent/relative), direct clinical observation, standardized tools (ADOS-2, ADI-R, Autism Spectrum Quotient [AQ]). High rate of co-occurring depression, anxiety, OCD, and ADHD.

#### Intellectual Disability (ID) (Ch. 17, pp. 485–494)
- **Core Diagnostic Criteria:** Significant impairment of intellectual functioning (IQ < 70) AND adaptive functioning (conceptual, social, practical domains), originating during the developmental period (<18 years).
- **Severity Classification:** Mild (IQ 50–69), Moderate (IQ 35–49), Severe (IQ 20–34), Profound (IQ < 20).
- **Diagnostic Overshadowing:** The dangerous clinical error of attributing new behavioral or emotional symptoms solely to the person's intellectual disability, missing treatable underlying medical illness or psychiatric disease.

#### Eating Disorders (Ch. 13, pp. 313–324)
- **Anorexia Nervosa (AN):**
  - *Core Features:* (1) Markedly low body weight (BMI < 17.5 kg/m² in adults or weight >15% below expected); (2) Active restriction of energy intake or persistent behavior preventing weight gain (purging, excessive exercise, appetite suppressants); (3) Core psychopathology: intense fear of gaining weight/fat, and profound body-image distortion / overvaluation of shape and weight (self-worth disproportionately tied to weight/shape).
  - *Subtypes:* Restricting type vs Binge-eating/purging type.
- **Bulimia Nervosa (BN):**
  - *Core Features:* (1) Recurrent binge eating (eating an objectively large amount of food with a sense of loss of control); (2) Recurrent inappropriate compensatory behaviors (self-induced vomiting, laxative abuse, fasting, excessive exercise); (3) Frequency ≥1/week for 3 months; (4) Overvaluation of shape and weight.
  - *Body Weight:* Typically **normal or slightly overweight** (distinguishes BN from anorexia binge-purge subtype).

#### Sleep Disorders & Sleep Architecture (Ch. 9, p. 214 & Ch. 13, pp. 324–330)
- **Chronic Insomnia:** Difficulty initiating sleep, maintaining sleep, or early morning awakening, occurring despite adequate opportunity for sleep, causing significant daytime impairment, present ≥3 nights/week for ≥3 months.
- **Narcolepsy Tetrad:** (1) Irresistible daytime sleep attacks; (2) **Cataplexy** (sudden bilateral loss of muscle tone triggered by emotions like laughter, with preserved consciousness); (3) Sleep paralysis; (4) Hypnagogic/hypnopompic hallucinations. Pathophysiology: deficiency of hypothalamic **orexin (hypocretin)** neurons. Diagnostic test: PSG followed by Multiple Sleep Latency Test (MSLT — mean latency <8 min and ≥2 SOREMPs).
- **REM Sleep Behaviour Disorder (RBD):** Loss of normal REM sleep muscle atonia (REM without atonia on PSG), leading to motor enactment of vivid dreams. High association with **alpha-synucleinopathies** (Dementia with Lewy Bodies, Parkinson's disease, MSA), preceding motor/cognitive decline by 5–15 years.
- **Normal Sleep EEG Stages:** N1 (alpha loss, theta waves), N2 (**Sleep Spindles** 12–14 Hz & **K-complexes**), N3 (Slow-Wave Delta waves 0.5–2 Hz), REM (low-voltage sawtooth waves, rapid eye movements, muscle atonia).
- **Depression Sleep Architecture:** Classic triad: (1) **Reduced REM latency** (<60 min); (2) **Increased REM density & duration** (especially first REM period); (3) **Reduced Slow-Wave Sleep (N3)**.

#### Personality Disorders (Ch. 15, pp. 395–418)
- **General Definition:** Enduring pattern of inner experience and behaviour that deviates markedly from cultural expectations, is pervasive, inflexible, has onset in adolescence/early adulthood, is stable over time, and leads to distress or impairment.
- **Borderline Personality Disorder (BPD):** Pervasive pattern of instability in relationships, self-image, and affect, with marked impulsivity: intense fear of abandonment, "splitting" relationships, identity disturbance, self-damaging impulsivity, recurrent suicidal behaviour / non-suicidal self-injury (NSSI), extreme affective reactivity (shifts lasting hours), chronic emptiness, inappropriate anger, transient stress-related paranoia/dissociation.
- **Antisocial Personality Disorder (ASPD):** Disregard for and violation of the rights of others since age 15, with history of Conduct Disorder before age 15. Features: law-breaking, deceitfulness, impulsivity, aggressiveness, reckless disregard for safety, irresponsibility, lack of remorse.

#### Sexual & Gender Psychiatry (Ch. 13, pp. 330–342)
- **Sexual Dysfunction Domains:** Desire (hypoactive desire), Arousal (erectile dysfunction), Orgasm (premature/delayed ejaculation, anorgasmia), Pain (vaginismus, dyspareunia). Causes: physical illness, SSRIs (anorgasmia in up to 50%), antipsychotics (hyperprolactinemia via D2 blockade).
- **Paraphilic Disorders:** Paraphilia = atypical sexual interest. Paraphilic Disorder = paraphilia causing **clinically significant distress/impairment** OR involving **harm/non-consenting individuals** (e.g. Pedophilic Disorder).
- **Gender Dysphoria:** Distress resulting from marked incongruence between experienced gender and assigned sex at birth. Non-pathologizing approach: gender diversity is not a mental illness; care focuses on relieving dysphoria and supporting autonomous decisions.

### 2.2 Diagnostic & classification distinctions

#### Childhood Impairment in Adult ADHD (Q59a) (Ch. 16, pp. 450–452)
- Adult ADHD requires historical evidence of childhood symptoms (school reports, parent collateral). Retrospective self-report alone suffers from recall bias.
- *Late Recognition vs Late Onset:* Late recognition occurs when high intelligence, supportive structure, or masking delayed impairment until adult academic/work demands escalated. Genuinely "adult-onset ADHD" without childhood roots is NOT recognized in ICD-10/DSM-5.

#### Masking / Camouflaging in Adult Autism (Q60a)
- *Oxford 7th ed Status:* Oxford 7th ed (2017) does NOT cover the concept of "masking" or "camouflaging" in adult autism. **Explicitly flag as an Oxford text gap/insufficiency.**

#### ARFID vs Anorexia Nervosa (Q62a) (Ch. 13, p. 313)
- **ARFID (Avoidant/Restrictive Food Intake Disorder):** Restrictive eating or food avoidance based on sensory characteristics, fear of choking/vomiting, or lack of interest in food, **WITHOUT body image distortion or fear of gaining weight**. (AN requires shape/weight overvaluation and fear of weight gain).

#### Delayed Sleep-Wake Phase Disorder vs Insomnia (Q65a) (Ch. 13, pp. 328–329)
- **DSWPD:** Major delay in habitual sleep timing (e.g. 3 AM to 11 AM). **Key distinction:** When allowed to sleep on preferred schedule, sleep quality and duration are completely normal. Insomnia features poor sleep quality/duration despite appropriate opportunity and timing.

#### Avoidant PD vs Social Anxiety Disorder (Q68a) (Ch. 15, p. 409)
- **Avoidant PD:** Pervasive lifelong self-concept of inadequacy and extreme hypersensitivity to rejection embedded across all social interactions.
- **Social Anxiety Disorder:** Marked fear/anxiety circumscribed to specific social/performance situations where scrutiny may occur.

#### OCPD vs OCD (Q68b) (Ch. 15, p. 410)
- **OCPD (Anankastic):** **Ego-syntonic** personality pattern of perfectionism, orderliness, and control at the expense of flexibility. No discrete intrusive obsessions or compulsions.
- **OCD:** **Ego-dystonic** clinical syndrome of intrusive unwanted obsessions causing anxiety, and repetitive compulsions performed to reduce anxiety.

#### BPD Affective Instability vs Bipolar Disorder (Q69a) (Ch. 15, pp. 401–405)
- **BPD Affective Shifts:** Rapid mood shifts lasting **hours**, highly reactive to interpersonal rejection/abandonment, returning to baseline emptiness.
- **Bipolar Episodes:** Autonomous mood elevation (mania/hypomania) or depression lasting **days to weeks**, independent of acute interpersonal triggers, with grandiosity and decreased sleep requirement.

#### ASPD vs Psychopathy (Q70) (Ch. 15, p. 396 & PCL-R)
- **ASPD:** Behavioral pattern of law-breaking and social irresponsibility requiring childhood conduct disorder history.
- **Psychopathy (Hare PCL-R):** Broader construct combining behavioral deviance (Factor 2) PLUS core affective/interpersonal traits (Factor 1: glibness, grandiosity, pathological lying, callousness, lack of empathy). Only ~25%–30% of ASPD individuals meet Psychopathy criteria.

### 2.4 Aetiology, neurobiology & biomarkers

- **Narcolepsy:** Loss of ~70,000 hypocretin/orexin-producing neurons in the lateral hypothalamus. Associated with HLA-DQB1*06:02 allele.
- **REM Sleep Behaviour Disorder:** Loss of brainstem (sublaterodorsal nucleus) GABA/glycine motor inhibition during REM sleep. Biomarker for alpha-synuclein accumulation (DLB, PD, MSA).
- **Depression Sleep EEG:** Cholinergic-aminergic imbalance leading to premature REM onset (reduced REM latency <60 min) and loss of N3 slow-wave delta sleep.
- **Sexual Dysfunction Mechanisms:** Prolactin elevation via antipsychotic D2 blockade inhibits GnRH/gonadotropins; SSRI 5-HT2A stimulation inhibits sexual arousal and ejaculation.

### 2.5 Course & prognosis

- **Adult ADHD:** Symptoms persist into adulthood in ~50%–60% of childhood cases (hyperactivity decreases, inattention/exec dysfunction persist).
- **Anorexia Nervosa:** Highest mortality of any psychiatric disorder (~5% per decade). Recovery in ~50%, chronic course in ~20%.
- **Bulimia Nervosa:** Better prognosis than AN; ~70% achieve remission with CBT-BN.
- **BPD:** High crisis frequency in 20s; significant symptom attenuation ("burning out") by 40s/50s. Suicide mortality ~8%–10%.

### 2.6 Pharmacological & treatment principles

#### Adult ADHD Management (Ch. 16, p. 452)
- **First-Line:** Stimulants (**Methylphenidate**, Lisdexamfetamine). Monitor BP, heart rate, weight.
- **Second-Line:** Non-stimulants (**Atomoxetine** — SNRI mechanism).

#### Minimally Verbal ID Management (Ch. 17, pp. 506–508)
- Systematic search for physical causes (pain, constipation, UTI, retention, dental, drug akathisia). Environmental adaptation. **Do NOT use antipsychotics as first-line chemical restraint.**

#### Medically Compromised Anorexia & Refeeding (Ch. 13, pp. 318–320)
- Inpatient medical resuscitation. Slow refeeding starting at 10–20 kcal/kg/day. Mandatory **thiamine (Pabrinex)** and phosphate replacement BEFORE nutrition to prevent fatal Refeeding Syndrome.

#### Bulimia Nervosa Treatment (Ch. 13, pp. 323–324)
- **First-Line Psychotherapy:** Manualized CBT-BN.
- **Pharmacotherapy:** High-dose **Fluoxetine (60 mg/day)** reduces bingeing/purging frequency.

#### Insomnia Treatment (Ch. 13, pp. 326–327)
- **First-Line:** **CBT-I** (stimulus control, sleep restriction, cognitive restructuring, sleep hygiene).
- **Hypnotics:** Z-drugs (zopiclone, zolpidem) short-term only (≤2–4 weeks).

#### BPD Management (Ch. 15, pp. 404–406)
- **First-Line:** Structured psychotherapy (**DBT**, MBT, Schema Therapy).
- Crisis boundaries, written crisis plans. **Avoid polypharmacy; medication is NOT primary treatment.**

#### Paraphilic Disorder Management (Ch. 13, pp. 337–339)
- CBT (cognitive restructuring of distorted minimizations, relapse prevention). Anti-androgens (Cyproterone acetate, GnRH analogues like triptorelin) for severe high-risk paraphilic disorders.

## 3. Question map

### Q59 — Adult ADHD
- **Spine:** (1) Triad: inattention, hyperactivity, impulsivity; (2) Executive dysfunction; (3) Mandatory childhood onset (before age 7/12); (4) Impairment across ≥2 settings; (5) Collateral/school reports; (6) Mimics: anxiety, depression, bipolar, substance use, sleep apnea, ASD, BPD; (7) Treatment: stimulants (methylphenidate/lisdexamfetamine) first-line, atomoxetine second-line.
- **Keepers:** Childhood onset requirement; 2-setting impairment rule; methylphenidate first-line.
- **Verify currently:** Modern adult diagnostic criteria age limits (DSM-5-TR / ICD-11) and prescribing rules.
- **Boundaries:** Childhood ADHD belongs in child psychiatry.

### Q59a — No Clear Childhood Impairment
- **Spine:** (1) Historical evidence mandatory (school reports, parent collateral); (2) Retrospective self-report limitations; (3) Distinguish late recognition (high IQ/structure masking impairment until adulthood) from late-onset symptoms; (4) Genuinely "adult-onset ADHD" without childhood roots is NOT recognized in ICD-10/DSM-5.
- **Keepers:** Late recognition vs late onset concept; collateral necessity.
- **Verify currently:** Current consensus on adult-onset ADHD claims in literature.
- **Boundaries:** General ADHD diagnosis in Q59.

### Q60 — Adult Autism
- **Spine:** (1) Social communication & interaction deficits; (2) Restricted, repetitive patterns of behaviour/interests; (3) Sensory hyper/hypo-reactivity; (4) Developmental history & collateral; (5) Standardized tools (ADOS-2, ADI-R, AQ); (6) Differentials: SAD, schizoid PD, OCD, ADHD, psychosis, ID; (7) High co-occurring depression/anxiety.
- **Keepers:** Core dyad of ASD; sensory reactivity feature; high psychiatric comorbidity.
- **Verify currently:** ICD-11 Autism Spectrum Disorder criteria (6A02).
- **Boundaries:** Childhood autism in child modules.

### Q60a — Masking / Camouflaging
- **Spine:** Oxford 7th ed (2017) does NOT cover masking or camouflaging in adult autism. **Flag explicitly as an Oxford text gap/insufficiency.**
- **Keepers:** Identify gap in 2017 text.
- **Verify currently:** Modern CAT-Q (Camouflaging Autistic Traits Questionnaire) and clinical literature on autistic camouflaging.
- **Boundaries:** Core ASD diagnosis in Q60.

### Q61 — Intellectual Disability Assessment
- **Spine:** (1) IQ <70 + adaptive impairment originating <18 yr; (2) Diagnostic overshadowing warning; (3) Baseline comparison; (4) Systemic search for physical causes of behavioral change (pain, UTI, constipation, retention, dental, seizures, drug side effects); (5) Environmental triggers; (6) Carer collateral.
- **Keepers:** Diagnostic overshadowing concept; baseline comparison mandate; physical cause screen.
- **Verify currently:** ICD-11 Disorders of Intellectual Development terminology.
- **Boundaries:** Genetic syndromes belong in child/genetics modules.

### Q61a — Minimally Verbal Adult with Aggression & Insomnia
- **Spine:** (1) Rule out physical organic causes FIRST: pain (dental, fracture), infection (UTI, chest), constipation, urinary retention, reflux, seizures, drug akathisia; (2) Environmental/communication triggers (staff change, noise, abuse); (3) Functional behavioral formulation; (4) **Do NOT use antipsychotics as first-line chemical restraint.**
- **Keepers:** Organic causes first rule; chemical restraint prohibition.
- **Verify currently:** STOMP / STAMP-OP UK guidance on stopping over-medication in ID.
- **Boundaries:** General ID assessment in Q61.

### Q62 — Anorexia Nervosa
- **Spine:** (1) Low body weight (BMI <17.5 kg/m²); (2) Active energy restriction / weight loss behavior; (3) Intense fear of weight gain & body image distortion / shape overvaluation; (4) Restricting vs binge-purge subtypes; (5) Physical complications (bradycardia, hypothermia, edema, osteopenia); (6) Psychotherapy (CBT-ED, MANTRA, SSCM).
- **Keepers:** BMI <17.5 threshold; shape overvaluation core psychopathology; MANTRA / CBT-ED.
- **Verify currently:** ICD-11 / DSM-5-TR diagnostic criteria (removal of amenorrhea).
- **Boundaries:** Medical emergency management in Q63.

### Q62a — ARFID (Avoidant/Restrictive Food Intake Disorder)
- **Spine:** (1) Restrictive eating based on sensory traits, choking fear, or low interest; (2) **Absence of body image distortion or fear of weight gain** (distinguishes from AN); (3) Oxford 7th ed contains minimal ARFID text (DSM-5 note); flag gap.
- **Keepers:** Lack of weight/shape psychopathology in ARFID.
- **Verify currently:** ICD-11 ARFID (6B83) criteria and adult management evidence.
- **Boundaries:** Anorexia nervosa in Q62.

### Q63 — Medically Compromised Anorexia Nervosa
- **Spine:** (1) Medical admission triggers: BMI <13, HR <40, BP <90/60, QTc >470 ms, temp <35°C, severe hypokalemia/hypophosphatemia, hypoglycemia; (2) **Refeeding Syndrome:** insulin surge -> massive intracellular shift of phosphate, potassium, magnesium -> fatal hypophosphatemia, cardiac failure, arrhythmias; (3) Prevention: slow refeeding start, mandatory prophylactic **thiamine (Pabrinex)** and phosphate BEFORE feeding.
- **Keepers:** Medical emergency thresholds; Refeeding Syndrome mechanism (hypophosphatemia); thiamine before feeding rule.
- **Verify currently:** MARSIPAN / MEED (Medical Emergencies in Eating Disorders 2022) guidelines.
- **Boundaries:** Outpatient anorexia treatment in Q62.

### Q64 — Bulimia Nervosa
- **Spine:** (1) Recurrent binge eating + loss of control; (2) Recurrent compensatory purging (vomiting, laxatives, fasting, exercise); (3) Frequency ≥1/week for 3 months; (4) Shape/weight overvaluation; (5) Body weight is **normal or slightly overweight**; (6) Complications (hypokalemia, dental erosion, parotid enlargement); (7) Treatment: CBT-BN first-line, high-dose **Fluoxetine (60 mg/day)**.
- **Keepers:** Normal weight distinction from AN; Fluoxetine 60 mg/day dose; CBT-BN.
- **Verify currently:** Modern NICE NG69 eating disorder guidelines.
- **Boundaries:** Anorexia binge-purge subtype in Q62.

### Q65 — Chronic Insomnia
- **Spine:** (1) Initiation/maintenance/early waking difficulty despite adequate opportunity, causing daytime impairment, ≥3 nights/wk for ≥3 months; (2) Assessment: sleep diary (2 wk), medical/psychiatric/substance screen; (3) **CBT-I FIRST-LINE** (stimulus control, sleep restriction, hygiene); (4) Hypnotics (Z-drugs) short-term only (≤2–4 weeks).
- **Keepers:** CBT-I first-line mandate; Z-drugs ≤2–4 week limit.
- **Verify currently:** Orexin receptor antagonists (Daridorexant) availability/licensing.
- **Boundaries:** Sleep apnea in respiratory medicine.

### Q65a — Delayed Sleep-Wake Phase Disorder
- **Spine:** (1) Major delay in habitual sleep timing (e.g. 3 AM to 11 AM); (2) **Key distinction:** When allowed preferred schedule, sleep quality and duration are completely normal; (3) Treatment: morning light therapy, evening exogenous melatonin, phase advance.
- **Keepers:** Normal sleep quality on preferred schedule distinction.
- **Verify currently:** Melatonin licensing for circadian rhythm disorders.
- **Boundaries:** General insomnia in Q65.

### Q66 — Excessive Daytime Sleepiness & Narcolepsy
- **Spine:** (1) EDS differential: insufficient sleep, OSA, sedatives, depression, narcolepsy; (2) Narcolepsy tetrad: sleep attacks, cataplexy (emotion-triggered weakness), sleep paralysis, hypnagogic hallucinations; (3) Hypothalamic **orexin (hypocretin)** deficiency; (4) Workup: PSG followed by MSLT (latency <8 min, ≥2 SOREMPs); (5) Treatment: modafinil, pitolisant, sodium oxybate.
- **Keepers:** Cataplexy pathognomonic feature; orexin deficiency; SOREMPs on MSLT.
- **Verify currently:** Type 1 vs Type 2 Narcolepsy diagnostic criteria (ICSD-3).
- **Boundaries:** Sleep apnea in respiratory medicine.

### Q66a — REM Sleep Behaviour Disorder (RBD)
- **Spine:** (1) Loss of REM sleep muscle atonia (REM without atonia on PSG); (2) Motor enactment of vivid/violent dreams; (3) Injury risk to self/partner; (4) Strong association with **alpha-synucleinopathies** (DLB, PD, MSA), preceding onset by 5–15 years; (5) Treatment: environment safety, clonazepam, melatonin.
- **Keepers:** REM without atonia; alpha-synucleinopathy precursor (DLB/PD); clonazepam/melatonin.
- **Verify currently:** Modern neuroprotective trial status in RBD.
- **Boundaries:** DLB diagnosis in Q48.

### Q67 — Sleep Stages & EEG
- **Spine:** (1) N1: alpha loss, theta waves; (2) N2: **Sleep Spindles** (12–14 Hz) & **K-complexes** (~50% sleep); (3) N3: Slow-Wave **Delta waves** (0.5–2 Hz, >20% epoch); (4) REM: low-voltage sawtooth waves, rapid eye movements, muscle atonia.
- **Keepers:** Sleep spindles & K-complexes in N2; Delta waves in N3; REM muscle atonia.
- **Verify currently:** AASM sleep staging manual standardization.
- **Boundaries:** Clinical sleep disorders in Q65–Q66.

### Q67a — Depression Sleep Architecture
- **Spine:** (1) **Reduced REM latency** (<60 min); (2) **Increased REM density & duration** (especially first REM period); (3) **Reduced Slow-Wave Sleep (N3)**; (4) Sleep fragmentation.
- **Keepers:** Shortened REM latency classic board fact; increased REM density.
- **Verify currently:** Diagnostic utility of sleep EEG in modern depression practice.
- **Boundaries:** Major depression in Q20.

### Q68 — Personality Disorder Classification & Formulation
- **Spine:** (1) Enduring, pervasive, inflexible pattern from adolescence causing distress/impairment; (2) DSM-5 10 types in Clusters A, B, C; (3) **ICD-11 Dimensional Model:** single diagnosis of Personality Disorder (Mild, Moderate, Severe) + 5 Domain Traits (Negative Affectivity, Detachment, Dissociality, Disinhibition, Anankastia); (4) Formulation: temperament, attachment, trauma, schemas, interpersonal patterns.
- **Keepers:** General PD definition; ICD-11 dimensional model shift.
- **Verify currently:** ICD-11 personality disorder operationalization.
- **Boundaries:** Specific PDs in Q68a–Q70.

### Q68a — Avoidant PD vs Social Anxiety Disorder
- **Spine:** (1) Avoidant PD: pervasive lifelong self-concept of inadequacy and hypersensitivity to rejection; (2) Social Anxiety Disorder: marked fear/anxiety circumscribed to specific social/performance situations; (3) High overlap/comorbidity.
- **Keepers:** Pervasive self-concept vs circumscribed situational anxiety.
- **Verify currently:** DSM-5-TR continuity of avoidant PD.
- **Boundaries:** General anxiety in Q30.

### Q68b — Obsessive-Compulsive PD vs OCD
- **Spine:** (1) OCPD (Anankastic): **ego-syntonic** perfectionism, orderliness, and control without intrusive obsessions/rituals; (2) OCD: **ego-dystonic** intrusive obsessions causing anxiety, and compulsions performed to relieve anxiety.
- **Keepers:** Ego-syntonic (OCPD) vs Ego-dystonic (OCD) distinction.
- **Verify currently:** ICD-11 Anankastia trait qualifier.
- **Boundaries:** OCD diagnosis in Q31.

### Q69 — Borderline Personality Disorder Management
- **Spine:** (1) Core features: instability of mood/relationships/self-image, impulsivity, NSSI/suicidality, abandonment fear, splitting; (2) **Psychotherapy FIRST-LINE** (DBT, MBT, Schema Therapy); (3) Crisis management & boundaries; (4) **Pharmacotherapy NOT primary treatment; avoid polypharmacy.**
- **Keepers:** Psychotherapy first-line; DBT / MBT; polypharmacy prohibition.
- **Verify currently:** NICE CG78 BPD guidelines and deprescribing protocols.
- **Boundaries:** Bipolar differential in Q69a.

### Q69a — BPD Affective Instability vs Bipolar Disorder
- **Spine:** (1) BPD mood shifts: rapid (lasting **hours**), highly reactive to interpersonal rejection, returning to baseline emptiness; (2) Bipolar mood episodes: autonomous elevation/depression lasting **days to weeks**, with grandiosity and reduced sleep need.
- **Keepers:** Hours (BPD) vs Days/Weeks (Bipolar) duration distinction; interpersonal reactivity.
- **Verify currently:** Bipolar II vs BPD diagnostic differentiation tools.
- **Boundaries:** Bipolar disorder in Q23.

### Q70 — ASPD vs Psychopathy
- **Spine:** (1) ASPD (DSM-5 / Dissocial ICD-10): law-breaking behavior since 15 with childhood Conduct Disorder history; (2) Psychopathy (Hare PCL-R): broader construct combining behavioral deviance (Factor 2) PLUS core affective/interpersonal traits (Factor 1: glibness, grandiosity, callousness, lack of empathy); (3) Only ~25%–30% of ASPD patients meet Psychopathy criteria.
- **Keepers:** Conduct disorder history requirement for ASPD; Hare PCL-R Factor 1 (callous/affective) vs Factor 2 (behavioral).
- **Verify currently:** PCL-R forensic risk assessment applications.
- **Boundaries:** Forensic psychiatry in forensic modules.

### Q71 — Sexual Dysfunction
- **Spine:** (1) Domains: desire, arousal (erectile), orgasm (premature/delayed), pain (vaginismus); (2) Causes: medical/cardiovascular, SSRIs (anorgasmia), antipsychotics (hyperprolactinemia); (3) Workup: physical exam, prolactin, testosterone; (4) Management: switch SSRI (bupropion/mirtazapine), PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil), psychosexual therapy.
- **Keepers:** SSRI anorgasmia; antipsychotic hyperprolactinemia; PDE5 inhibitors.
- **Verify currently:** ICD-11 Sexual dysfunctions classification (moved to sexual health chapter).
- **Boundaries:** Paraphilias in Q72.

### Q72 — Paraphilic Disorder & Risk Assessment
- **Spine:** (1) Paraphilia (atypical interest) vs Paraphilic Disorder (causes **distress/impairment** OR involves **harm/non-consenting individuals**); (2) Offending history & risk to others; (3) Management: CBT (relapse prevention), Anti-androgens (cyproterone, GnRH analogues) for severe high risk.
- **Keepers:** Distress/harm threshold for disorder; GnRH analogues for high risk.
- **Verify currently:** ICD-11 Paraphilic disorders (6D30) diagnostic criteria and legal frameworks.
- **Boundaries:** General forensic psychiatry.

### Q73 — Adult Gender Dysphoria
- **Spine:** (1) Distress from incongruence between experienced gender and assigned sex; (2) **Non-pathologizing approach:** gender diversity is NOT a mental illness; (3) Assess distress, evaluate comorbidity without assuming causation, support autonomous consent; (4) Multidisciplinary Gender Identity Clinic (GIC) care (social, psychological, endocrine, surgical).
- **Keepers:** Non-pathologizing principle; GIC multidisciplinary referral.
- **Verify currently:** ICD-11 **Gender Incongruence** (HA60) classification under Sexual Health.
- **Boundaries:** Child gender identity in child modules.

## 4. 15 Most Important Claims Requiring Current Verification

1. **ICD-11 Personality Disorder Dimensional Classification:** Oxford 7th ed presents DSM-5/ICD-10 categorical types. ICD-11 single diagnosis of Personality Disorder (Mild/Moderate/Severe) and 5 Domain Traits (Negative Affectivity, Detachment, Dissociality, Disinhibition, Anankastia) require verification.
2. **ICD-11 Gender Incongruence Reclassification:** Oxford covers Gender Dysphoria / Gender Identity Disorder under mental disorders. ICD-11 reclassification to *Gender Incongruence* under *Conditions related to sexual health* requires verification.
3. **ARFID Diagnostic Criteria & Management:** Oxford 7th ed contains minimal ARFID text. Current ICD-11 / DSM-5-TR criteria and adult management evidence require verification.
4. **Autistic Masking / Camouflaging Evidence:** Oxford 7th ed lacks coverage of masking/camouflaging. Modern research and assessment tools (CAT-Q) require verification.
5. **Adult ADHD Age of Onset Criteria:** Oxford cites childhood onset (age 7/12). Current DSM-5-TR / ICD-11 diagnostic flexibility and adult prescribing guidelines require verification.
6. **Orexin Receptor Antagonists in Insomnia:** Oxford covers CBT-I and Z-drugs. Licensing and first-line positioning of dual orexin receptor antagonists (Daridorexant) require verification.
7. **MARSIPAN / MEED Guidelines in Anorexia Nervosa:** Oxford outlines medical admission triggers. Modern MEED (2022) risk assessment and refeeding protocols require verification.
8. **NICE Guidelines on Pharmacotherapy in BPD:** Oxford warns against polypharmacy. Current NICE CG78 guidelines prohibiting routine drug treatment for BPD require verification.
9. **Pimavanserin in PD Psychosis / RBD Management:** Oxford covers quetiapine/clozapine. Current status of 5-HT2A inverse agonists and RBD neuroprotective trials requires verification.
10. **STOMP / STAMP-OP Guidelines in Intellectual Disability:** Oxford covers behavioral management. UK national guidance on stopping over-medication of people with ID requires verification.
11. **Fluoxetine Dosing in Bulimia Nervosa:** Oxford cites Fluoxetine 60 mg/day. Current NICE eating disorder guideline recommendations require verification.
12. **CBT-I as Universal First-Line for Insomnia:** Oxford covers sleep hygiene and hypnotics. European and AASM guidelines establishing CBT-I as mandatory first-line before any hypnotic require verification.
13. **Type 1 vs Type 2 Narcolepsy (ICSD-3):** Oxford describes narcolepsy tetrad. ICSD-3 biomarker classification (CSF hypocretin-1 levels ≤110 pg/mL) requires verification.
14. **ICD-11 Paraphilic Disorders Classification:** Oxford covers DSM-5 paraphilias. ICD-11 criteria requiring non-consenting individuals or severe distress require verification.
15. **ICD-11 Sexual Dysfunctions Reclassification:** Oxford covers sexual dysfunctions under mental disorders. ICD-11 movement to *Conditions related to sexual health* requires verification.
