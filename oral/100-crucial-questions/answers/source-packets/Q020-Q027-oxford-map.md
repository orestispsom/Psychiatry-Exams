## Q20. Major depressive disorder / depressive episode: clinical features, diagnosis and differentials

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Define and recognise the depressive syndrome clinically rather than from low mood alone.
- Cover affective, cognitive, biological/somatic and psychomotor domains.
- Include suicidality and functional impairment.
- Recognise psychotic depression and severe psychomotor retardation/stupor where present.
- Mention clinically useful patterns such as melancholic/somatic and atypical features without turning them into separate disorders.
- Build a differential that includes bipolar depression, adjustment/bereavement-related presentations, substance/medication effects and medical/neurological causes.
- Diagnosis is longitudinal and should include attention to previous elevated/activated episodes before concluding unipolar depression.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Core depressive phenomenology.
- Melancholic/somatic pattern as a classic exam distinction.
- Psychotic depression and Cotard-type nihilistic phenomena as high-yield severe-depression material.
- Atypical depressive phenomenology as a classic descriptive pattern.
- Importance of differential diagnosis and exclusion of secondary causes.

### Requires current verification / update
- Oxford-era ICD-10 diagnostic hierarchy and symptom thresholds versus ICD-11.
- Current classification names and architecture.
- Any old suicide/lifetime-risk percentages.
- Any old recurrence percentages if they would otherwise become board facts.

## Q20a. PMDD versus premenstrual worsening of another disorder

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Establish a recurrent temporal relationship between symptoms and the menstrual cycle.
- Distinguish a discrete premenstrual syndrome from worsening of an underlying disorder that persists outside the premenstrual phase.
- Functional impairment matters.
- Prospective ratings are preferable to retrospective recall when diagnostic certainty is needed.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Premenstrual symptom clustering and functional impact.
- PMS/PMDD as a cyclical rather than continuously present syndrome.
- SSRI treatment as a classic therapeutic principle.

### Requires current verification / update
- Exact current PMDD diagnostic timing and threshold requirements.
- Whether prospective charting across a specified number of cycles should be made a board fact.
- Continuous versus luteal-phase SSRI dosing if included.

## Q21. Treatment of a major depressive episode

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Assess severity, suicide risk, psychotic features, medical/substance contributors and previous treatment response before choosing treatment.
- Use psychological treatment, antidepressant medication or combined treatment according to severity, clinical profile, patient factors and preference.
- ECT has an important role in severe, psychotic, life-threatening or treatment-refractory depression.
- Continue successful treatment after remission rather than stopping immediately.
- Reassess response, adherence and diagnosis before declaring failure.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Pretreatment assessment and severity-based clinical reasoning.
- Main antidepressant class principles.
- Psychological therapies as core treatment rather than adjunctive decoration.
- ECT as an established treatment for severe depression.
- Continuation and relapse-prevention principles.

### Requires current verification / update
- Oxford’s 2009-era NICE sequencing versus current NICE guidance.
- Current timing for early response review / adequate trial.
- Current continuation-treatment duration anchors.
- Modern switching versus augmentation strategy where needed.
- Any rigid “mild/moderate/severe” ladder if current guidance is less categorical.

## Q22. Treatment-resistant depression

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Before calling treatment resistance, reassess diagnosis and pseudoresistance.
- Check dose, duration, adherence, tolerability, comorbidity, substance use and medical contributors.
- Options after inadequate response include switching, augmentation, combination strategies and ECT.
- Lithium augmentation is a classic high-yield strategy.
- Atypical-antipsychotic augmentation is an established option.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Pseudoresistance audit before escalation.
- Switching versus augmentation as the key strategic distinction.
- Lithium augmentation.
- Atypical-antipsychotic augmentation.
- ECT for resistant/severe depression.

### Requires current verification / update
- Current practical TRD definition.
- Whether a broader difficult-to-treat-depression framework should be mentioned.
- Current place of rTMS.
- Current place of esketamine/ketamine in Europe/Greece.
- Avoid freezing older treatment-effect numbers such as NNTs from the textbook as board facts unless independently justified.

## Q23. Bipolar disorder: diagnosis and differential

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Diagnose longitudinally from episodes, not from isolated “mood swings.”
- Distinguish mania, hypomania and depressive episodes.
- Bipolar I and bipolar II should be separated by the presence of mania versus hypomania plus depression.
- Consider mixed features and cycling patterns where clinically relevant.
- Differential includes ADHD, borderline affective instability, schizophrenia-spectrum illness, substances/medications, medical causes and unipolar depression.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Manic syndrome phenomenology.
- Hypomania as a less severe but clearly episodic activated state.
- Bipolar I versus bipolar II as a high-yield classification distinction.
- ADHD/BPD/schizophrenia/unipolar differential architecture.
- Importance of antidepressant-associated activation as a clue requiring careful longitudinal interpretation.

### Requires current verification / update
- ICD-11 bipolar I/II architecture and current duration wording.
- Mixed-features classification.
- Exact treatment-emergent mania/hypomania rule.
- Avoid importing DSM wording as though it were universal ICD wording.

## Q24. Acute mania treatment

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Assess risk, severity, psychosis, agitation, substance use, medical causes and need for admission.
- Pharmacological treatment centres on antipsychotics, lithium and valproate, with combination treatment used when clinically indicated.
- Short-term benzodiazepines may be adjunctive for severe behavioural activation/agitation.
- ECT is an option in severe or refractory mania.
- Antidepressants should not be continued uncritically during mania.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Antipsychotic/lithium/valproate as core antimanic treatment classes.
- Combination treatment as an established strategy.
- ECT as a treatment option for severe/refractory mania.
- Immediate safety/setting assessment.

### Requires current verification / update
- Current guideline sequencing and monotherapy-versus-combination hierarchy.
- Current valproate reproductive-safety restrictions.
- Exact doses, pregnancy restrictions and monitoring if mentioned.
- Any old network-meta-analysis ranking should not be frozen as current truth without verification.

## Q25. Bipolar depression treatment

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Bipolar depression should not be managed as ordinary unipolar depression.
- Oxford-era core options include quetiapine, olanzapine–fluoxetine, lurasidone, lamotrigine and lithium.
- Antidepressants require caution because of switch/mixed-state concerns; antidepressant monotherapy is particularly problematic in bipolar I.
- ECT is relevant for severe, urgent or refractory depressive episodes.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Quetiapine, lithium and lamotrigine as classic high-yield bipolar-depression agents.
- Antidepressant switch-risk concept.
- Avoidance of simplistic antidepressant monotherapy framing.
- ECT for severe/refractory presentations.

### Requires current verification / update
- Current first-line hierarchy.
- Whether newer agents such as cariprazine materially belong in a European/Greek board answer.
- EU licensing versus international guideline recommendations for lurasidone/cariprazine.
- Avoid importing US-only treatments as routine European practice.

## Q26. Long-term management of bipolar disorder

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Maintenance is relapse prevention plus functional recovery, adherence, psychoeducation and physical-health monitoring.
- Lithium is a central/gold-standard maintenance treatment.
- Valproate, lamotrigine and selected antipsychotics are alternatives according to previous response and polarity.
- Psychological/psychoeducational intervention is part of maintenance care.
- Monitor relevant physical-health and medication adverse effects.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Lithium’s central maintenance role.
- Valproate, lamotrigine, olanzapine and quetiapine as established maintenance options in the textbook framework.
- CBT/psychoeducation and adherence work.
- Renal, thyroid, calcium and metabolic monitoring concepts where relevant to chosen treatment.

### Requires current verification / update
- Current maintenance hierarchy.
- Current valproate reproductive restrictions, including current European regulatory rules.
- Exact maintenance duration recommendations if any.
- Current strength/wording of lithium’s suicide-prevention evidence.
- Avoid duplicating the later dedicated lithium-monitoring question.

## Q27. Postpartum psychosis

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Recognise postpartum psychosis as a rare but severe psychiatric emergency, classically beginning abruptly soon after childbirth.
- Typical phenomenology may include mania, depression, psychosis, confusion/disorganisation and rapidly changing mental state.
- Strong relationship with bipolar-spectrum illness is exam-relevant.
- Risk assessment must include suicide and infant safety.
- Admission is required for severe illness; mother-and-baby-unit care is preferred where available.
- Acute treatment may include antipsychotic treatment, mood-stabilising treatment and ECT according to severity and clinical picture.
- Future relapse risk and future pregnancy planning are clinically important.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Abrupt onset typically within the early postpartum period.
- Emergency nature.
- Bipolar association.
- Mother-and-baby-unit principle.
- Acute treatment and ECT principles.

### Requires current verification / update
- Exact current timing language.
- Current admission/service pathway.
- Lithium positioning and relapse prevention.
- Breastfeeding/reproductive prescribing details.
- Avoid freezing old recurrence percentages without current support.

## Q27a. Postpartum depression

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Distinguish postpartum depression from short-lived maternity blues and from postpartum psychosis.
- Assess depressive severity, suicide risk, infant safety, functioning, support and bonding/care context.
- Management broadly follows evidence-based depression care, modified for perinatal prescribing and breastfeeding considerations.
- Psychological treatment and antidepressants are both relevant depending on severity and preference.
- Severe presentations may require specialist/perinatal services and ECT where clinically indicated.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Distinction between blues, depression and psychosis.
- Standard depression-treatment principles with perinatal modification.
- Sertraline as a classic lactation-compatible antidepressant signal in the textbook, but exact safety claims require current verification.

### Requires current verification / update
- Current screening and perinatal-service recommendations.
- Current breastfeeding-compatible prescribing advice.
- New postpartum-depression treatments such as zuranolone/brexanolone and their European relevance.
