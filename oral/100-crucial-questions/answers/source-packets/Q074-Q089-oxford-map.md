## 2. Reusable chapter map

### 2.1 Clinical phenomenology & core syndromes

#### Pharmacokinetic & Pharmacodynamic Principles (Ch. 25, pp. 709–716)
- **Pharmacokinetics (PK - What the body does to the drug):**
  - *Absorption & Bioavailability ($F$):* Fraction of unchanged drug reaching systemic circulation. Affected by gastric pH, gut motility, food intake, and first-pass hepatic metabolism.
  - *Distribution & Protein Binding:* Volume of distribution ($V_d$). Lipophilic psychotropics cross the blood-brain barrier (BBB) easily and distribute extensively into lipid-rich brain tissue and systemic fat. High plasma protein binding (e.g. albumin for acidic drugs like valproate; alpha-1 acid glycoprotein for basic drugs like TCAs). Only unbound (free) drug crosses the BBB and exerts pharmacological activity.
  - *Metabolism:* Hepatic biotransformation:
    - **Phase I Reactions:** Oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis mediated primarily by Cytochrome P450 (CYP) isoenzymes (**CYP1A2, CYP2D6, CYP3A4, CYP2C9, CYP2C19**). Creates active or inactive metabolites. Active metabolites: desipramine (from imipramine), norfluoxetine (from fluoxetine, half-life 7–15 days), paliperidone (9-hydroxyrisperidone from risperidone).
    - **Phase II Reactions:** Conjugation (glucuronidation, sulfation) creating hydrophilic inactive excretable metabolites. Benzodiazepines lacking Phase I CYP metabolism (**Lorazepam, Oxazepam, Temazepam**) undergo direct Phase II glucuronidation, making them safer in hepatic impairment.
  - *Elimination & Half-Life ($t_{1/2}$):* Elimination half-life determines time to reach steady state ($4–5 \times t_{1/2}$) and time for elimination. Primary renal elimination applies to hydrophilic drugs (**Lithium, Amisulpride, Gabapentin, Pregabalin, Acamprosate**).
  - *First-Pass Metabolism:* Presystemic extraction by gut wall and liver, significantly reducing oral bioavailability (e.g. chlorpromazine, propranolol).
- **Pharmacodynamics (PD - What the drug does to the body):**
  - *Receptor Interactions:* Full Agonism, Partial Agonism (high affinity, intrinsic activity <1; e.g. **Aripiprazole** at D2, **Buprenorphine** at mu-opioid), Competitive Antagonism, Inverse Agonism.
  - *Dose-Response & Therapeutic Index:* Relationship between drug dose and clinical effect. Narrow therapeutic index drugs (**Lithium, TCAs**) carry high toxicity risk with small dose increases; wide therapeutic index drugs (**SSRIs, Benzodiazepines**) are safer in accidental overdose.

#### Important Psychotropic Drug Interactions (Ch. 25, pp. 712–716, 746, 755–763)
- **CYP Isoenzyme Inhibition (Raises drug levels -> Toxicity):**
  - *Fluoxetine & Paroxetine:* Potent **CYP2D6** inhibitors. Raise plasma levels of risperidone, haloperidol, atomoxetine, TCAs, and beta-blockers.
  - *Fluvoxamine:* Potent **CYP1A2** and **CYP2C19** inhibitor. Causes massive surges in plasma levels of **clozapine, olanzapine, agomelatine**, and theophylline.
  - *Ketoconazole, Erythromycin & Grapefruit Juice:* Potent **CYP3A4** inhibitors. Raise levels of quetiapine, aripiprazole, and carbamazepine.
- **CYP Isoenzyme Induction (Lowers drug levels -> Treatment failure):**
  - *Carbamazepine, Phenytoin, Phenobarbital, St John's Wort:* Potent **CYP3A4** inducers. Accelerate metabolism and lower plasma concentrations of quetiapine, aripiprazole, oral contraceptives, methadone, and valproate.
  - *Tobacco Smoke:* Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons in tobacco smoke induce **CYP1A2**, lowering clozapine and olanzapine levels by 30%–50%. Smoking cessation causes clozapine levels to surge.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
  - *Serotonergic Combinations:* MAOIs + SSRIs / SNRIs / TCAs / Pethidine / Tramadol / St John's Wort -> dangerous serotonin accumulation precipitating fatal **Serotonin Syndrome**.
  - *QTc Prolongation:* Combining QTc-prolonging psychotropics (Haloperidol, Pimozide, Methadone, Citalopram, Escitalopram, Amisulpride) with antiarrhythmics or macrolide antibiotics -> increased risk of **Torsades de Pointes (TdP)**.
  - *CNS Depression:* Benzodiazepines + Alcohol / Opioids -> severe respiratory depression and death.
  - *Lithium Clearance Reductions:* **NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, Thiazide diuretics** reduce renal lithium clearance, precipitating acute **Lithium Toxicity**.
  - *Valproate + Lamotrigine:* Valproate inhibits lamotrigine glucuronidation, doubling lamotrigine half-life and sharply increasing the risk of **Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS)**. Requires halving the lamotrigine starting dose.
  - *SSRIs + NSAIDs / Anticoagulants:* Combined depletion of platelet serotonin and antiplatelet/anticoagulant effects increases upper GI bleeding risk 3- to 6-fold.

#### Antipsychotic Selection & Adverse Effect Profiles (Ch. 25, pp. 724–744)
- **First-Generation (FGAs) vs Second-Generation (SGAs):**
  - *FGAs (Haloperidol, Chlorpromazine, Flupentixol, Zuclopenthixol):* High D2 affinity. Higher risk of Extrapyramidal Side Effects (EPS), tardive dyskinesia, and hyperprolactinemia.
  - *SGAs (Olanzapine, Risperidone, Quetiapine, Aripiprazole, Amisulpride, Clozapine):* 5-HT2A/D2 dual antagonism or partial D2 agonism. Lower EPS risk, but variable metabolic risk.
- **Clozapine Indication:** Gold-standard treatment for **Treatment-Resistant Schizophrenia (TRS)** (failure of ≥2 adequate trials of different antipsychotics, at least one SGA, at adequate dose for ≥6 weeks). Also indicated for persistent severe aggression or recurrent suicidal behavior in schizophrenia.
- **Extrapyramidal Side Effects (EPS):**
  1. *Acute Dystonia:* Onset within **hours to days** (1–5 days). Involuntary muscle spasms (oculogyric crisis, torticollis, trismus, laryngeal dystonia — airway emergency). Risk: young males, high-potency FGAs. Treatment: Parenteral anticholinergic (**Procyclidine 5–10 mg IV/IM**).
  2. *Parkinsonism:* Onset within **days to weeks** (5–30 days). Bradykinesia, rigidity (lead-pipe/cogwheel), resting tremor, mask-like facies, shuffling gait. Treatment: Reduce dose, switch to low-EPS SGA (quetiapine, aripiprazole), or oral anticholinergic (procyclidine).
  3. *Akathisia:* Onset within **days to weeks** (5–60 days). Subjective intense feeling of inner restlessness and urge to move, accompanied by observable motor restlessness (pacing, rocking, foot shifting). **Temporal link to starting/increasing medication.** Highly distressing; suicide risk if misdiagnosed as psychotic agitation. Treatment: Reduce dose / switch SGA; **Propranolol (beta-blocker)** first-line; mirtazapine / benzos second-line. Anticholinergics are ineffective!
  4. *Tardive Dyskinesia (TD):* Onset after **months to years** (>6–12 months). Involuntary choreoathetoid movements of mouth, tongue, face (lip smacking, tongue protrusion, grimacing) and limbs. Risk: elderly, long exposure, FGAs, mood disorders. Treatment: Prevention; stop anticholinergics (worsen TD!); switch to Clozapine or Quetiapine; VMAT2 inhibitors (Valbenazine, Deutetrabenazine).
- **Metabolic, Endocrine & Cardiovascular Profiles:**
  - *Weight Gain & Diabetes:* Highest risk: **Clozapine, Olanzapine**; Intermediate: Quetiapine, Risperidone; Lowest: **Aripiprazole, Ziprasidone, Lurasidone**.
  - *Hyperprolactinemia:* Highest risk: **Risperidone, Paliperidone, Amisulpride, Haloperidol**. Prolactin-sparing: Aripiprazole, Quetiapine, Clozapine. Causes galactorrhea, amenorrhea, sexual dysfunction, osteopenia.
  - *QTc Prolongation:* Highest risk: **Pimozide, IV Haloperidol, Sertindole, Amisulpride**. QTc >500 ms requires drug cessation.
  - *Clozapine Cardiac Risks:* **Myocarditis** (first 2–8 weeks; fever, chest pain, tachycardia, elevated troponin/CRP) and **Cardiomyopathy** (late onset; heart failure signs).
- **Monitoring Protocol:** Baseline: Weight/BMI, waist circumference, BP, Fasting Glucose / HbA1c, Fasting Lipids, U&Es, LFTs, Prolactin, baseline ECG. Re-check weight monthly; glucose/lipids/BP at 12 weeks, 6 months, then annually.

#### Antidepressant Selection, Switching & Discontinuation (Ch. 25, pp. 735–751)
- **Major Classes:** SSRIs (Sertraline, Fluoxetine, Citalopram, Escitalopram, Paroxetine), SNRIs (Venlafaxine, Duloxetine), NaSSA (Mirtazapine — sedating, weight gain, appetite stimulation), NDRI (Bupropion — minimal sexual side effects, no weight gain), TCAs (Amitriptyline, Imipramine, Clomipramine — anticholinergic, cardiotoxic, lethal in overdose), MAOIs (Phenelzine, Tranylcypromine — tyramine dietary restriction).
- **Discontinuation Syndrome:** FINISH symptoms (Flu-like, Insomnia, Nausea, Imbalance/dizziness, Sensory disturbances [brain zaps], Hyperarousal/anxiety). Highest risk: **Paroxetine and Venlafaxine** (short half-life); lowest risk: **Fluoxetine** (long half-life norfluoxetine).
- **Switching & Washout Rules:**
  - *SSRI to MAOI:* Mandatory **5-week washout** for Fluoxetine (due to norfluoxetine); 1–2 weeks for other SSRIs.
  - *MAOI to SSRI/TCA:* Mandatory **2-week washout** for irreversible MAOIs.

#### Lithium, Valproate & Lamotrigine (Ch. 25, pp. 751–760)
- **Lithium:**
  - *Indications:* Acute mania, bipolar maintenance, augmentation in resistant depression. **Reduces suicide risk** in mood disorders.
  - *Therapeutic Drug Monitoring (TDM):* Target blood level: **0.6–0.8 mmol/L** (maintenance); **0.8–1.0 mmol/L** (acute mania/breakthrough). Blood drawn exactly **12 hours post-dose** at steady state (5–7 days).
  - *Adverse Effects:* Fine tremor, polyuria/polydipsia (Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus), hypothyroidism, weight gain, metallic taste, hypercalcemia. Long-term: chronic kidney disease.
  - *Toxicity:* Mild (1.5–2.0 mmol/L: coarse tremor, vomiting, diarrhea, ataxia, dysarthria); Severe (>2.0–2.5 mmol/L: confusion, hyperreflexia, seizures, coma, collapse). Level >2.5–3.0 mmol/L requires **Emergency Hemodialysis**.
  - *Gastroenteritis + NSAID Emergency Protocol (Q81a):* Dehydration + sodium depletion + NSAID prostaglandin inhibition -> severe drop in renal clearance. **IMMEDIATELY HOLD LITHIUM**, stop NSAID, IV saline rehydration, urgent lithium level/U&Es.
- **Valproate:**
  - *Indications:* Acute mania, bipolar maintenance (rapid cycling/mixed).
  - *Adverse Effects:* Weight gain, sedation, tremor, alopecia, thrombocytopenia, transient LFT elevation. Rare: fatal hepatic failure, acute pancreatitis, hyperammonemic encephalopathy.
  - *Teratogenicity & PPP Restrictions:* Causes **neural tube defects (spina bifida 1%–2%)** and **major neurodevelopmental impairment (30%–40% risk of lower IQ / autism)**. **CONTRAINDICATED in female children, adolescents, women of childbearing potential, and pregnant women**, unless a strict Pregnancy Prevention Programme (PPP) is in place.
- **Lamotrigine:**
  - *Indications:* Bipolar maintenance, specifically preventing/treating **Bipolar Depression** (ineffective in acute mania).
  - *Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):* **Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS)** / **Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN)**. Risk minimized by low starting dose (25 mg/day) and **slow titration** (double dose every 2 weeks).
  - *Valproate Interaction:* Valproate doubles lamotrigine half-life -> **halve the lamotrigine starting dose and titration schedule**.

#### Benzodiazepines & Hypnotics (Ch. 25, pp. 755–760)
- **Mechanism:** Positive allosteric modulators at GABA-A receptors -> increase chloride channel opening frequency -> neuronal hyperpolarization.
- **Adverse Effects & Risks:** Sedation, cognitive impairment, anterograde amnesia, motor incoordination, falls/fractures in elderly, respiratory depression (especially with alcohol/opioids), paradoxical disinhibition.
- **Dependence & Prescribing Rules:** Physical dependence within **3–4 weeks**. Withdrawal: rebound anxiety, insomnia, tremor, sweating, seizures, delirium. Prescribe short-term ONLY (**maximum 2 to 4 weeks**). Z-drugs (zopiclone, zolpidem) carry similar dependence and fall risks.

#### Adult ADHD Pharmacotherapy (Ch. 16, pp. 450–454 & Ch. 25, pp. 760–761)
- **First-Line:** Stimulants (**Methylphenidate**, Lisdexamfetamine / Dexamfetamine). Block DA/NA reuptake (and release DA/NA). Monitor BP, heart rate, weight.
- **Second-Line:** Non-stimulant **Atomoxetine** (selective NA reuptake inhibitor) or alpha-2 agonists (Guanfacine).
- **Psychosis or Substance Use History (Q85a):**
  - *Psychosis History:* Stimulants contraindicated in active mania/psychosis. In stable remitted psychosis, use with extreme caution or select non-stimulant **Atomoxetine**.
  - *Substance Use History:* High risk of stimulant misuse, abuse, and diversion. Select non-stimulant **Atomoxetine** (zero abuse potential). If stimulants needed: select prodrug **Lisdexamfetamine** or long-acting formulations with supervised dispensing.

#### Pregnancy & Lactation Psychopharmacology (Ch. 22, pp. 668–673 & Ch. 25)
- **General Principle:** Balance risks of untreated maternal illness (relapse, suicide, poor self-care) vs fetal/neonatal drug toxicity. Use monotherapy at lowest effective dose.
- **Drug-Specific Risks:**
  - *Valproate:* **STRICT CONTRAINDICATION.** Spina bifida (1%–2%), neurodevelopmental deficits (30%–40%).
  - *Carbamazepine:* Neural tube defects, dysmorphic features.
  - *Lithium:* Cardiac defects, specifically **Ebstein's anomaly** (tricuspid valve displacement; risk ~1:1,000 vs 1:20,000 baseline). Neonatal hypotonia ("floppy infant syndrome").
  - *Antidepressants:* SSRIs generally low absolute risk. Paroxetine carries slight VSD/ASD risk. 3rd trimester exposure -> Poor Neonatal Adaptation Syndrome (PNAS) and rare Persistent Pulmonary Hypertension of the Newborn (PPHN).
  - *Lactation:* Sertraline and Olanzapine have minimal breast milk transfer (preferred); Lithium enters breast milk in high concentrations (contraindicated/caution).

#### Emergency Psychiatric Syndromes & Overdose (Ch. 21, 22, 25)
- **NMS vs Serotonin Syndrome (Q87):**
  - *NMS:* D2 antagonists; onset over **days to weeks**; **lead-pipe rigidity**, hyporeflexia, extreme fever (>38°C–40°C), massive **CK elevation (>10,000 U/L)**, normal pupils. Treatment: stop drug, ICU support, cooling, **Dantrolene** / **Bromocriptine**.
  - *Serotonin Syndrome:* Serotonergic drugs; rapid onset (**hours**); **neuromuscular hyperexcitability (clonus, hyperreflexia)**, tremor, diarrhea, **mydriasis**, mild/moderate CK. Treatment: stop drugs, ICU support, cooling, **Cyproheptadine**.
- **TCA Overdose (Q88):**
  - *Toxicity:* Fast sodium channel ($I_{Na}$) blockade, alpha-1 blockade, H1 blockade, anticholinergic blockade. Anticholinergic toxidrome, hypotension, seizures, coma, severe cardiac arrhythmias.
  - *ECG Red Flags:* **QRS duration >100 ms** (predicts seizures); **QRS >160 ms** (predicts VT/VF); terminal R wave in aVR.
  - *First-Line Antidote:* **Intravenous Sodium Bicarbonate (1–2 mmol/kg IV bolus)** for QRS >100 ms or hypotension. Alkalinizes blood and unblocks sodium channels. **Avoid Class Ia and Ic antiarrhythmics.**

#### ECT & Neuromodulation (Ch. 25, pp. 761–774)
- **ECT (Q89):**
  - *Indications:* Severe life-threatening Major Depression (especially with psychotic features, severe psychomotor retardation, stupor, or food/fluid refusal requiring rapid response), severe acute Mania, Catatonia (schizophrenia, organic, or mood-related), treatment-resistant severe depression.
  - *Practical Principles:* Done under general anesthesia (propofol, methohexital) and muscle relaxation (**Suxamethonium / Succinylcholine** to prevent traumatic fractures). Brief-pulse electrical stimulus delivered via Bilateral (bitemporal) or Unilateral (d'Elia placement on non-dominant hemisphere). Unilateral carries less memory impairment; Bilateral is faster-acting and more potent. Typical course: 6 to 12 treatments (twice weekly).
  - *Adverse Effects:* Retrograde and anterograde amnesia (resolves over weeks to months, but spotty retrograde gaps may persist), post-ictal confusion, headache, muscle soreness, transient cardiovascular surge (hypertension, tachycardia).
  - *Contraindications:* No absolute medical contraindications; high relative risk: raised intracranial pressure (ICP), recent myocardial infarction (<3 months), cerebral aneurysm, retinal detachment.
- **Incapacity, Consent & Legal Framework (Q89a):**
  - *Clinical Ethics:* Patient with capacity must give informed voluntary consent. If patient lacks capacity, treatment decision must be made under legal framework prioritizing best interests and medical necessity.
  - *Legal Framework Warning:* Oxford 7th ed details UK Mental Health Act Section 58A / Section 62 rules. **Do NOT import UK legal rules into Greek practice.** In Greece, involuntary ECT and capacity decisions operate under **Law 2071/1992** and civil court guardianship provisions.
- **Repetitive Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (rTMS) (Q89b):**
  - *Mechanism:* Non-invasive focal magnetic pulses applied to the **dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC)** to modulate cortical excitability without general anesthesia or muscle relaxation.
  - *Indication:* Treatment-Resistant Depression (mild to moderate failure of antidepressants).
  - *Comparison with ECT:* rTMS has zero cognitive impairment and requires no anesthesia, but is **less efficacious than ECT** for severe psychotic or catatonic depression. Seizure risk is very low (<0.1%).

### 2.2 Diagnostic & classification distinctions

#### Akathisia vs Psychotic Agitation (Q77a) (Ch. 25, p. 733)
- **Akathisia:** Subjective limb/inner restlessness + objective motor restlessness (pacing, rocking), temporally linked to antipsychotic start/dose increase. Responsive to propranolol.
- **Agitation:** Driven by internal fear, persecutory delusions, or mania without specific subjective leg restlessness.

#### NMS vs Serotonin Syndrome (Q87) (Ch. 22, pp. 673–674 & Ch. 25)
- **NMS:** D2 antagonists, gradual onset (days/weeks), lead-pipe rigidity, hyporeflexia, massive CK elevation, normal pupils.
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Serotonergic agents, rapid onset (hours), clonus, hyperreflexia, diarrhea, mydriasis, mild/moderate CK.

#### ASPD vs Psychopathy & BPD vs Bipolar (Cross-Reference Ch. 15)
- BPD mood shifts last hours (interpersonal triggers); Bipolar episodes last days/weeks (autonomous).

### 2.4 Aetiology, neurobiology & receptor binding matrices

- **D2 Receptor Blockade:** Mesolimbic -> antipsychotic efficacy; Nigrostriatal -> EPS & tardive dyskinesia; Tuberoinfundibular -> hyperprolactinemia; Mesocortical -> cognitive/negative worsening.
- **5-HT2A Receptor Antagonism:** SGA property (olanzapine, risperidone) that disinhibits dopamine release in nigrostriatal pathway, reducing EPS.
- **Fast Sodium Channel ($I_{Na}$) Blockade:** TCA cardiotoxicity mechanism causing QRS widening, conduction block, and ventricular arrhythmias.
- **Orexin Deficiency:** Narcolepsy pathophysiology.
- **Dopamine Depletion:** NMS pathophysiology (central D2 receptor blockade or abrupt withdrawal of dopaminergic agonists).

### 2.5 Course & prognosis

- **Lithium Suicide Reduction:** Lithium uniquely reduces all-cause mortality and suicide rates in bipolar disorder by 60%–80%.
- **Clozapine TRS Response:** Clozapine achieves clinical response in 30%–60% of patients with treatment-resistant schizophrenia who failed other SGAs.
- **NMS & SS Mortality:** NMS mortality historically 10%–20% (now <5% with early recognition); Serotonin Syndrome mortality <1% with supportive care.

### 2.6 Pharmacological & emergency protocols

#### Lithium Monitoring & Toxicity Protocol (Q81, Q81a)
1. TDM: 12-hour trough level, target 0.6–0.8 mmol/L (maintenance). Baseline U&Es, TFTs, calcium, eGFR.
2. Sick-Day / NSAID Protocol: If patient develops gastroenteritis/dehydration or takes an NSAID: **IMMEDIATELY HOLD LITHIUM**, stop NSAID, give IV normal saline, check urgent lithium level and U&Es. Hemodialysis if level >2.5–3.0 mmol/L with severe toxicity.

#### Valproate Pregnancy Prevention Programme (PPP) (Q82)
- Valproate is **CONTRAINDICATED in females of childbearing potential** unless a formal Pregnancy Prevention Programme is active, annual risk acknowledgment signed, and effective contraception used.

#### Lamotrigine Titration Protocol (Q83)
- Start 25 mg/day x 2 weeks -> 50 mg/day x 2 weeks -> 100 mg/day -> 200 mg/day. **Halve dose if co-prescribed with valproate.**

#### TCA Overdose Protocol (Q88)
- Continuous ECG monitoring. If QRS >100 ms or hypotension: administer **IV Sodium Bicarbonate (1–2 mmol/kg bolus)**. Avoid Class Ia/Ic antiarrhythmics.

#### NMS & Serotonin Syndrome Emergency Protocols (Q87)
- **NMS:** Stop antipsychotics -> ICU support -> IV fluids & cooling -> **Dantrolene** / **Bromocriptine**.
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Stop serotonergic drugs -> ICU support -> IV fluids & cooling -> **Cyproheptadine**.

### Q75 — Important Psychotropic Drug Interactions

- **Boundaries:** EPS management in Q77; Metabolic monitoring in Q78.

### Q77 — Extrapyramidal Side Effects (EPS) Management
- **Spine:** (1) Acute Dystonia (hours/days, muscle spasms, oculogyric crisis; treatment: IV/IM procyclidine); (2) Parkinsonism (days/weeks, bradykinesia, rigidity, tremor; treatment: reduce dose, switch SGA, oral procyclidine); (3) Akathisia (days/weeks, inner restlessness; treatment: propranolol); (4) Tardive Dyskinesia (months/years, choreoathetosis, orofacial dyskinesias; treatment: prevention, stop anticholinergics, switch to clozapine/quetiapine, VMAT2 inhibitors).
- **Keepers:** Procyclidine IV/IM for acute dystonia; Propranolol for akathisia; Anticholinergic prohibition in Tardive Dyskinesia.
- **Verify currently:** VMAT2 inhibitors (Valbenazine, Deutetrabenazine) licensing and availability.
- **Boundaries:** Akathisia vs agitation differential in Q77a.

### Q77a — Akathisia vs Agitation
- **Spine:** (1) Akathisia: subjective intense inner/leg restlessness + objective motor restlessness (pacing, rocking), temporally linked to starting/increasing antipsychotic; (2) Psychotic Agitation: driven by internal distress, mania, or persecutory delusions without subjective leg restlessness; (3) Distress & suicide risk in un-recognized akathisia; (4) Treatment: dose reduction, switch SGA, Propranolol first-line.
- **Keepers:** Subjective leg restlessness distinction; Propranolol first-line treatment.
- **Verify currently:** Mirtazapine second-line evidence for akathisia.
- **Boundaries:** General EPS in Q77.

### Q78 — Antipsychotic Metabolic, Endocrine & Cardiovascular Adverse Effects
- **Spine:** (1) Metabolic: weight gain, T2DM, dyslipidemia (highest: Clozapine, Olanzapine; lowest: Aripiprazole, Ziprasidone, Lurasidone); (2) Endocrine: hyperprolactinemia via tuberoinfundibular D2 blockade (highest: Risperidone, Paliperidone, Amisulpride; prolactin-sparing: Aripiprazole, Quetiapine); (3) QTc Prolongation: Torsades de Pointes risk (Pimozide, IV Haloperidol, Amisulpride; QTc >500 ms stop rule); (4) Clozapine myocarditis (first 2–8 wk) and cardiomyopathy; (5) Monitoring protocol (baseline weight, glucose, lipids, BP, ECG, prolactin; follow-up schedule).
- **Keepers:** Clozapine/Olanzapine highest metabolic risk; Risperidone/Amisulpride highest prolactin risk; QTc >500 ms stop threshold; Clozapine myocarditis timing.
- **Verify currently:** Local physical health monitoring guidelines for severe mental illness (SMI).
- **Boundaries:** Antipsychotic selection in Q76.

### Q79 — Antidepressant Selection
- **Spine:** (1) Selection criteria: diagnosis/subtype, prior response, comorbidity (cardiovascular, epilepsy, pain), adverse effects, overdose toxicity, suicide risk, patient preference; (2) Classes: SSRIs (first-line), SNRIs (venlafaxine, duloxetine for pain/severe depression), NaSSA (mirtazapine for insomnia/weight loss), NDRI (bupropion for no sexual side effects), TCAs (lethal in overdose), MAOIs (refractory depression).
- **Keepers:** SSRIs first-line safety; Duloxetine for pain comorbidity; Mirtazapine for insomnia/weight loss; TCA overdose lethality.
- **Verify currently:** Current NICE NG222 antidepressant prescribing guidelines.
- **Boundaries:** Adverse effects and switching in Q80.

### Q80 — Antidepressant Adverse Effects, Interactions, Switching & Discontinuation
- **Spine:** (1) Adverse effects: SSRI GI distress, sexual dysfunction (50%), hyponatremia (SIADH), upper GI bleeding; TCA anticholinergic, cardiotoxic, QTc prolongation; (2) Discontinuation syndrome (FINISH symptoms; highest risk Paroxetine/Venlafaxine, lowest Fluoxetine); (3) Switching rules: SSRI to MAOI requires **5-week washout** for Fluoxetine (norfluoxetine half-life); MAOI to SSRI requires **2-week washout** for irreversible MAOIs.
- **Keepers:** FINISH mnemonic; Paroxetine/Venlafaxine high discontinuation risk; Fluoxetine 5-week washout before MAOI rule.
- **Verify currently:** Maudsley Prescribing Guidelines switching tables.
- **Boundaries:** TCA overdose in Q88; Serotonin syndrome in Q87.

### Q81 — Lithium
- **Spine:** (1) Indications: mania, bipolar maintenance, anti-suicidal property; (2) TDM: 12h trough level, target 0.6–0.8 mmol/L (maintenance), 0.8–1.0 mmol/L (acute mania); (3) Adverse effects: fine tremor, polyuria/polydipsia (NDI), hypothyroidism, weight gain, hypercalcemia, CKD; (4) Toxicity: mild (1.5–2.0 mmol/L: coarse tremor, ataxia, dysarthria, vomiting) vs severe (>2.0–2.5 mmol/L: confusion, seizures, coma); level >2.5–3.0 mmol/L requires **Emergency Hemodialysis**; (5) Drug interactions: NSAIDs, ACEi, ARBs, thiazides reduce clearance.
- **Keepers:** 12h post-dose trough sampling; 0.6–0.8 target level; anti-suicidal property; Hemodialysis threshold >2.5–3.0 mmol/L.
- **Verify currently:** Local trust TDM protocols and long-term renal monitoring frequency.
- **Boundaries:** Gastroenteritis + NSAID emergency in Q81a.

### Q81a — Lithium + Gastroenteritis + NSAID Emergency
- **Spine:** (1) Dehydration + sodium depletion from gastroenteritis decreases renal lithium clearance; (2) NSAID prostaglandin inhibition further decreases clearance (by 30%–60%); (3) Synergistic rapid surge in serum lithium -> acute life-threatening toxicity; (4) Emergency Management: **IMMEDIATELY HOLD LITHIUM**, stop NSAID, IV normal saline rehydration, urgent lithium level, U&Es, and renal function assessment.
- **Keepers:** Dehydration + NSAID dual mechanism; immediate lithium hold rule; IV normal saline rehydration.
- **Verify currently:** Renal replacement therapy criteria for acute lithium toxicity.
- **Boundaries:** General lithium monograph in Q81.

### Q82 — Valproate
- **Spine:** (1) Bipolar indications: acute mania, maintenance (rapid cycling/mixed); (2) Adverse effects: weight gain, sedation, tremor, alopecia, thrombocytopenia; rare fatal hepatic failure, pancreatitis, hyperammonemic encephalopathy; (3) **Teratogenicity & Regulatory Restrictions:** spina bifida (1%–2%), major neurodevelopmental deficits (30%–40%); **CONTRAINDICATED in females of childbearing potential** unless strict Pregnancy Prevention Programme (PPP) active.
- **Keepers:** Teratogenic neural tube & 30%–40% neurodevelopmental risk; Pregnancy Prevention Programme (PPP) absolute mandate.
- **Verify currently:** EMA / MHRA / Greek EOF regulatory restrictions on valproate prescribing.
- **Boundaries:** Lamotrigine interaction in Q83.

### Q83 — Lamotrigine
- **Spine:** (1) Bipolar indication: maintenance, preventing/treating **Bipolar Depression** (ineffective in acute mania); (2) SCARs risk: **Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS)** / **TEN**; (3) Mandatory slow titration schedule (double dose every 2 weeks: 25 -> 50 -> 100 -> 200 mg); (4) **Valproate Interaction:** valproate doubles lamotrigine half-life -> **halve the lamotrigine starting dose and titration schedule**.
- **Keepers:** Bipolar depression specific indication; SJS/TEN risk; slow titration rule; valproate dose halving rule.
- **Verify currently:** Exact titration schedules in co-prescription with valproate or enzyme inducers.
- **Boundaries:** General mood stabilizers in Q81–Q82.

### Q84 — Benzodiazepines & Hypnotics
- **Spine:** (1) GABA-A positive allosteric modulator mechanism; (2) Indications & adverse effects (sedation, amnesia, ataxia, falls in elderly, respiratory depression, paradoxical disinhibition); (3) Dependence, tolerance, and withdrawal (rebound anxiety, insomnia, seizures, delirium); (4) Short-term prescribing rule (**maximum 2 to 4 weeks**); (5) Z-drugs (zopiclone, zolpidem) carry similar dependence and fall risks.
- **Keepers:** GABA-A mechanism; 2–4 week maximum prescribing limit; Z-drug fall/dependence equivalence.
- **Verify currently:** Controlled drug prescribing rules and Ashton taper guidelines.
- **Boundaries:** Benzodiazepine withdrawal taper in Q43.

### Q85 — Adult ADHD Pharmacotherapy
- **Spine:** (1) First-line: Stimulants (**Methylphenidate**, Lisdexamfetamine); (2) Second-line: Non-stimulant **Atomoxetine** (SNRI) or alpha-2 agonists; (3) Baseline assessment: BP, heart rate, weight, cardiovascular screen, substance use screen; (4) Monitoring: BP, pulse, weight at dose changes and every 6 months.
- **Keepers:** Methylphenidate / Lisdexamfetamine first-line; baseline BP/HR screen; Atomoxetine non-stimulant second-line.
- **Verify currently:** NICE NG87 ADHD prescribing and monitoring algorithms.
- **Boundaries:** History of psychosis or SUD in Q85a; Diagnostic criteria in Q59.

### Q85a — Adult ADHD Pharmacotherapy with History of Psychosis or SUD
- **Spine:** (1) Psychosis History: stimulants contraindicated in active mania/psychosis; in stable remitted psychosis, use with extreme caution or select non-stimulant **Atomoxetine**; (2) Substance Use History: high risk of stimulant misuse/diversion; select non-stimulant **Atomoxetine** (zero abuse potential) first-line; if stimulants required, select prodrug **Lisdexamfetamine** or long-acting formulations with supervised dispensing.
- **Keepers:** Active psychosis contraindication; Atomoxetine zero abuse potential advantage; Lisdexamfetamine prodrug selection.
- **Verify currently:** Adult ADHD comorbid substance use management protocols.
- **Boundaries:** General ADHD pharmacotherapy in Q85.

### Q86 — Pregnancy & Reproductive Psychopharmacology
- **Spine:** (1) Risk-benefit framework: untreated maternal illness risks vs teratogenicity/neonatal toxicity; (2) Monotherapy at lowest effective dose; (3) Drug risk profiles: Valproate (STRICT CONTRAINDICATION, spina bifida & 30%–40% neurodevelopmental deficit); Carbamazepine (neural tube defects); Lithium (Ebstein's anomaly 1:1,000, floppy infant syndrome); Antipsychotics (SGAs preferred, gestational diabetes monitor); SSRIs (Sertraline/Fluoxetine preferred, Paroxetine VSD/ASD risk, PNAS/PPHN 3rd trimester); (4) Lactation: Sertraline/Olanzapine preferred (low milk excretion); Lithium contraindicated/caution.
- **Keepers:** Valproate absolute contraindication; Lithium Ebstein anomaly; Sertraline lactation safety; SSRI PPHN risk.
- **Verify currently:** UKTIS / Motherisk perinatal psychopharmacology guidelines.
- **Boundaries:** Individual drug monographs in Q76–Q83.

### Q87 — Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS) vs Serotonin Syndrome
- **Spine:** (1) NMS: D2 antagonists, gradual onset (days/weeks), **lead-pipe rigidity**, hyporeflexia, extreme hyperthermia, massive **CK elevation (>10,000 U/L)**, normal pupils; treatment: stop drug, ICU, cooling, **Dantrolene** / **Bromocriptine**; (2) Serotonin Syndrome: serotonergic agents, rapid onset (hours), **clonus**, hyperreflexia, tremor, diarrhea, **mydriasis**, mild/moderate CK; treatment: stop drugs, ICU, cooling, **Cyproheptadine**.
- **Keepers:** Lead-pipe rigidity (NMS) vs Clonus/Hyperreflexia (SS); Days/Weeks (NMS) vs Hours (SS) onset; Dantrolene (NMS) vs Cyproheptadine (SS).
- **Verify currently:** Intensive care management guidelines for NMS and Serotonin Syndrome.
- **Boundaries:** Individual drug toxicity in Q76/Q80.

### Q88 — Tricyclic Antidepressant (TCA) Overdose
- **Spine:** (1) Pathophysiology: fast sodium channel ($I_{Na}$) blockade, alpha-1 blockade, anticholinergic blockade; (2) Clinical picture: anticholinergic toxidrome, severe hypotension, seizures, coma, fatal ventricular arrhythmias; (3) ECG Red Flags: **QRS duration >100 ms** (predicts seizures); **QRS >160 ms** (predicts VT/VF); terminal R wave in aVR; (4) Emergency Treatment: ABCDE, continuous ECG, **Intravenous Sodium Bicarbonate (1–2 mmol/kg IV bolus)** for QRS >100 ms or hypotension; **avoid Class Ia and Ic antiarrhythmics**.
- **Keepers:** Fast sodium channel blockade mechanism; QRS >100 ms seizure / >160 ms arrhythmia threshold; IV Sodium Bicarbonate antidote; Class Ia/Ic contraindication.
- **Verify currently:** National Poisons Information Service (TOXBASE) TCA overdose protocols.
- **Boundaries:** General antidepressant selection in Q79.

### Q89 — ECT Indications, Principles & Adverse Effects
- **Spine:** (1) Indications: severe life-threatening Major Depression (psychotic depression, severe psychomotor retardation, stupor, food/fluid refusal), severe acute Mania, Catatonia, treatment-resistant depression; (2) Principles: general anesthesia + muscle relaxation (**Suxamethonium**); Bilateral (bitemporal) vs Unilateral (d'Elia); 6–12 sessions (twice weekly); (3) Adverse effects: retrograde/anterograde amnesia, post-ictal confusion, headache; (4) Relative risks: raised ICP, recent MI (<3 months), cerebral aneurysm.
- **Keepers:** Suxamethonium muscle relaxant; Bilateral vs Unilateral trade-off; Psychotic depression / Catatonia prime indications.
- **Verify currently:** Modern NICE TA59 ECT guidelines and anesthetic standards.
- **Boundaries:** Legal consent in Q89a; rTMS in Q89b.

### Q89a — ECT Incapacity, Consent & Legal Framework (Greek vs UK)
- **Spine:** (1) Clinical Ethics: competent patient must give voluntary informed consent; incapacitated patient decision prioritized under legal best interests; (2) **Legal Framework Warning:** Oxford 7th ed details UK Mental Health Act Section 58A / 62 rules. **Do NOT import UK legal rules into Greek practice.** Greek practice operates under **Law 2071/1992** and civil court guardianship rules.
- **Keepers:** Separate UK MHA rules from Greek legal statutory framework.
- **Verify currently:** Greek Law 2071/1992 provisions on involuntary ECT and legal guardianship.
- **Boundaries:** General ECT indications in Q89.

### Q89b — Repetitive Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (rTMS)
- **Spine:** (1) Mechanism: non-invasive magnetic pulses applied to the **dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC)** to modulate cortical excitability without general anesthesia or muscle relaxation; (2) Indication: Treatment-Resistant Depression (mild to moderate); (3) Comparative role: zero cognitive deficit, no anesthesia required, but **less effective than ECT** for severe psychotic or catatonic depression; seizure risk <0.1%.
- **Keepers:** DLPFC stimulation site; no anesthesia advantage; lower efficacy than ECT in severe/psychotic depression.
- **Verify currently:** Modern NICE IPG731 rTMS guidance for depression.
- **Boundaries:** ECT in Q89.

7. **Antipsychotic QTc Prolongation Stop Thresholds:** Oxford lists QTc risks. Current cardiology and psychopharmacology guidelines defining exact QTc prolongation stop thresholds (>500 ms or >60 ms increase from baseline) require verification.
8. **Fluoxetine 5-Week Washout Before MAOI:** Oxford specifies a 5-week washout for fluoxetine before starting an MAOI due to norfluoxetine. Current Maudsley switching guidelines require verification.
9. **Antidepressant Upper GI Bleeding Risk with NSAIDs:** Oxford notes bleeding risk. Current prescribing guidance recommending co-prescription of a PPI (proton pump inhibitor) when combining SSRIs with NSAIDs or anticoagulants requires verification.
10. **TCA Overdose Sodium Bicarbonate Indications & ECG Cut-Offs:** Oxford covers TCA toxicity. Current emergency poisons (TOXBASE) guidelines establishing QRS >100 ms as the threshold for IV Sodium Bicarbonate require verification.

14. **Greek Legal Framework for Involuntary ECT (Law 2071/1992):** Oxford details UK Mental Health Act rules. Statutory Greek legal requirements for involuntary ECT, capacity evaluation, and judicial authorization require verification.
15. **Perinatal SSRI Exposure and PPHN Risk:** Oxford notes SSRI safety in pregnancy. Current epidemiological evidence quantifying Persistent Pulmonary Hypertension of the Newborn (PPHN) risk requires verification.
