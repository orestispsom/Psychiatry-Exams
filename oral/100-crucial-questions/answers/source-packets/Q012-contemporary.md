# Q12 Contemporary Research Packet — Schizophrenia Clinical Picture & Diagnosis

Research stream: **independent contemporary evidence only**. The prescribed Oxford Shorter 2017 edition and the user's local psychiatry textbooks were deliberately not consulted. The older `Psych` oral-answer bank was not used as factual authority; repository mappings were used only to define required coverage and board-fact targets.

## 1. Question target

A specialist candidate must be able to describe schizophrenia as a heterogeneous, multidimensional psychotic disorder rather than as a single positive-symptom syndrome, and then show how the diagnosis is established clinically and longitudinally. The answer package must distinguish current DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 frameworks, cover positive, negative, disorganised, cognitive, affective and psychomotor/catatonic phenomena, and explicitly handle functional course, mood chronology, substances/medications and medical/neurological causes. Historical constructs such as Schneiderian first-rank symptoms and classical schizophrenia subtypes should be recognisable but not presented as current pathognomonic or subtype-based diagnosis.

## 2. Proposed answer spine

1. **Phenotype by domains** — positive psychotic phenomena, disorganisation, negative symptoms, cognition, affective symptoms and psychomotor/catatonic phenomena.
2. **Describe psychopathology precisely** — delusions, hallucinations/perceptual abnormalities, formal thought disorder/disorganised speech and behaviour, without treating any one phenomenological feature as schizophrenia-specific.
3. **Clarify deficit-like phenomena** — identify the five consensus negative-symptom domains and distinguish primary/persistent symptoms from secondary negative-like states; recognise cognitive dysfunction as clinically important but not independently diagnostic.
4. **Apply current classification** — use DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 separately, especially symptom threshold architecture, duration, functioning and current dimensional/course characterisation.
5. **Diagnose longitudinally and by exclusion** — reconstruct onset/course and baseline function; establish the temporal relationship of psychosis to mood syndromes, substances/medications and medical/neurological illness; do not equate a first episode of psychosis with schizophrenia prematurely.
6. **Place classical concepts correctly** — Schneiderian first-rank symptoms and paranoid/hebephrenic/catatonic/etc. subtypes are historically important, but first-rank symptoms have limited diagnostic specificity and current systems no longer privilege the classical subtype scheme.

## 4. Dangerous misses / examiner traps

- **Calling any Schneiderian first-rank symptom pathognomonic or sufficient for schizophrenia.** Historical importance does not equal current diagnostic specificity (`Q12_CUR_HIST_02`, `Q12_CUR_HIST_03`).
- **Reciting ICD-10's symptom hierarchy as ICD-11.** ICD-11 retained a one-month threshold but changed the architecture: two-of-seven symptoms with at least one core symptom, plus dimensional/course qualifiers (`Q12_CUR_ICD_01`, `Q12_CUR_ICD_05`).
- **Saying DSM and ICD use the same duration rule.** The present systems retain a major duration difference; DSM also has schizophreniform disorder whereas ICD-11 does not (`Q12_CUR_DSM_02`, `Q12_CUR_ICD_03`).
- **Saying ICD-11 requires functional deterioration for schizophrenia.** Functional status is clinically important, but the formal requirement differs from DSM (`Q12_CUR_ICD_04`).
- **Using current subtype language such as 'paranoid schizophrenia' or 'catatonic schizophrenia' as if these were retained formal subtypes.** They are historical (`Q12_CUR_HIST_04`, `Q12_CUR_PSYCHOM_01`).
- **Equating social withdrawal, reduced speech or low activity with primary negative symptoms without assessing secondary causes.** Depression, active psychosis, extrapyramidal symptoms, sedation, substance use and environmental deprivation can mimic/worsen negative symptoms (`Q12_CUR_NEG_02`).
- **Treating cognitive impairment as a diagnostic criterion or using the group-average ~1-SD deficit as an individual threshold.** Cognition is highly relevant to phenotype/function, but heterogeneous and non-diagnostic (`Q12_CUR_COG_01`, `Q12_CUR_COG_02`).
- **Treating hallucination modality/content or formal thought disorder as schizophrenia-specific.** Both are transdiagnostic; diagnosis rests on syndrome, course and exclusions (`Q12_CUR_PHEN_03`, `Q12_CUR_FTD_02`).
- **Saying mood symptoms automatically exclude schizophrenia.** The chronology/proportion/concurrence of syndromal mood and psychotic symptoms matters, and DSM/ICD conceptualise schizoaffective disorder differently (`Q12_CUR_AFF_01`, `Q12_CUR_DIAG_04`).
- **Equating first-episode psychosis with schizophrenia before duration and differential diagnosis are established** (`Q12_CUR_DIAG_05`).
- **Implying a routine MRI, EEG or laboratory panel 'diagnoses schizophrenia.'** Medical investigations support exclusion of secondary causes; NICE does not recommend routine structural imaging for every first episode (`Q12_CUR_DIAG_02`, `Q12_CUR_INV_01`).
- **Letting Q12 become Q13.** Q12 should show that mood chronology and adjacent psychotic disorders determine the boundary, but detailed schizophrenia-vs-schizoaffective-vs-mood-disorder discrimination belongs to the next question.

## 5. Exact facts requiring later verification

1. **Exact DSM-5-TR schizophrenia criteria wording.** The authoritative PsychiatryOnline chapter exposed the five psychosis domains but not the full proprietary criteria. Before dossier freeze, verify directly in an authorised DSM-5-TR copy:
   - exact active-phase symptom threshold and wording;
   - which symptoms satisfy the mandatory 'core' part of the threshold;
   - exact duration language, including treatment-shortened active-phase wording and what may count in prodromal/residual periods;
   - exact functional-impairment criterion;
   - exact schizoaffective/depressive/bipolar exclusion clause;
   - exact substance/medication/medical-condition exclusion clause;
   - exact autism-spectrum/childhood communication-disorder clause.
2. **DSM-5-TR current-update status.** The September 2025 cumulative APA update supplement was inspected and its table of contents did not identify a schizophrenia criteria/text change. The base DSM-5-TR text still requires direct verification rather than reconstruction from DSM-5 antecedent sources.
3. **Exact ICD-11 CDDR wording.** WHO's 2024 CDDR is the governing clinical manual, but its 7.7-MB PDF download returned HTTP 403 through this research interface. The two-of-seven/one-core/one-month framework is strongly triangulated from WHO-linked ICD-11 development/comparison literature and a 2025 post-CDDR review; exact verbatim CDDR phrasing should be checked in the WHO PDF before a quotation or exact board-fact formulation is frozen.
4. **ICD-11 provisional label before one month.** High-quality final-development literature supports `Other specified primary psychotic disorder` when symptom requirements are met but duration is <1 month. Verify the exact final CDDR wording/name if this is retained as an exact board fact.
5. **Schneiderian first-rank list.** Historical source/translations operationalise the list somewhat differently (especially grouping of thought interference/passivity/'made' phenomena). The later adjudicator should choose one source-locked canonical exam list rather than synthesising a novel enumeration.
6. **First-rank diagnostic-accuracy numbers.** Cochrane estimates are supportable but arise largely from older studies with methodological limitations. If exact sensitivity/specificity numbers are kept in the board-fact block, preserve the comparator (`other psychoses` versus `all other diagnoses`) and confidence/quality caveat.
7. **Cognitive magnitude anchor.** Schaefer et al. 2013 reported a grand mean Hedges g = -1.03, processing speed g = -1.25 and episodic memory g = -1.23. If exact numbers are retained, label them as group-level meta-analytic estimates rather than an individual diagnostic threshold; newer work also confirms substantial heterogeneity.
8. **ICD qualifier names/codes.** The six symptom domains are well supported; exact extension-code strings/severity coding are low-value for this oral question and should be verified from the current ICD browser only if the adjudicator decides they merit inclusion.

## 6. Likely 2017-vs-current comparison points

These are **comparison targets for the local/exam-source agent, not claims about what Oxford 2017 says**.

- Whether the 2017 exam source still teaches an ICD-10-style privileged hierarchy for Schneiderian first-rank symptoms versus the current DSM/ICD de-emphasis of their diagnostic specificity.
- Whether classical schizophrenia subtypes are still used pedagogically or diagnostically in the exam source versus their absence from current DSM-5-TR and ICD-11.
- Whether catatonia is framed as a schizophrenia subtype versus its current separate/cross-diagnostic status.
- Whether cognition is treated only as an associated impairment versus the stronger contemporary dimensional emphasis in ICD-11 and EPA assessment guidance.
- Whether negative symptoms are organised using legacy broad lists versus the contemporary five-domain consensus and explicit primary/persistent-versus-secondary distinction.
- Whether DSM criteria are represented from DSM-IV/DSM-5-era wording; exact DSM-5-TR wording must be source-checked rather than assumed identical.
- Whether the source implies DSM/ICD convergence in duration or functioning. Current systems retain the important six-month-versus-one-month distinction and differ on mandatory functional impairment.
- Whether a 2017 discussion of mood psychosis/schizoaffective disorder uses a framework that differs materially from current ICD-11's episode-based conception or DSM's longitudinal conception; detailed resolution belongs mainly to Q13.
