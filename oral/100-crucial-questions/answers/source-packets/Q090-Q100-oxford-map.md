## 2. Reusable chapter map

### 2.1 Clinical phenomenology & core syndromes

#### Cognitive Behaviour Therapy (CBT) (Ch. 24, pp. 685–696)
- **Core Model & Collaborative Empiricism:** CBT is a structured, time-limited, goal-oriented psychotherapy based on the premise that psychological distress is maintained by maladaptive cognitive appraisals and unhelpful behavioural patterns. Therapist and patient work together as an investigative team (**collaborative empiricism**) using explicit hypotheses and empirical tests.
- **The Cognitive Formulation:** Individualized cross-sectional and longitudinal formulation linking:
  1. *Core Beliefs / Schemas:* Deep-seated, rigid, global assumptions about self, others, and the world formed in early life (e.g. "I am unlovable", "The world is dangerous").
  2. *Intermediate Beliefs / Rules:* Conditional assumptions and attitudes (e.g. "If I please everyone, I will be accepted").
  3. *Automatic Thoughts:* Situation-specific, rapid, involuntary cognitions that mediate between an external event and the patient's emotional, physiological, and behavioural response.
  4. *The Interactive Loop:* Bidirectional links between **Thoughts $\leftrightarrow$ Emotions $\leftrightarrow$ Physiology $\leftrightarrow$ Behaviour**.
- **Cognitive Distortions:** Systematic cognitive biases in information processing:
  - *All-or-nothing thinking (dichotomous):* Black-and-white evaluation.
  - *Catastrophizing:* Expecting the worst possible outcome.
  - *Overgeneralization:* Drawing sweeping conclusions from a single event.
  - *Selective abstraction (mental filter):* Focusing solely on negative details while ignoring positive evidence.
  - *Mind reading & Fortune telling:* Arbitrary negative inferences without evidence.
  - *Personalization:* Assuming personal blame for external events.
- **Key Interventions:**
  - *Cognitive Restructuring:* Socratic questioning, examining evidence for and against thoughts, generating balanced alternative thoughts.
  - *Behavioural Experiments:* Testing automatic thoughts and catastrophic predictions through planned real-world actions.
  - *Graded Exposure & Habituation / Inhibitory Learning:* Systematic, hierarchical confrontation of feared stimuli without safety behaviours in anxiety/OCD.
  - *Behavioural Activation (BA):* Structured scheduling of mastery and pleasure activities to counteract withdrawal and inertia in depression.
  - *Homework & Relapse Prevention:* Practice assignments between sessions; identifying early warning signs, relapse signatures, and creating a written maintenance plan.
- **Broad Psychiatric Indications:** Major depressive disorder, panic disorder, agoraphobia, social anxiety disorder, generalized anxiety disorder, OCD, PTSD, bulimia nervosa, insomnia (CBT-I), psychosis (CBTp for distressing delusions/hallucinations), and chronic pain.

#### Psychodynamic Concepts: Transference, Countertransference & Splitting (Ch. 24, pp. 681–685, 696–702)
- **Transference:** The unconscious displacement and redirection of feelings, attitudes, expectations, and relational templates originally experienced toward significant childhood figures (e.g. parents) onto the therapist or psychiatrist in the current clinical relationship.
- **Countertransference:** The clinician's emotional, cognitive, and behavioural reactions to the patient. Includes:
  - *Objective Countertransference:* Reactions induced in the clinician by the patient's current interpersonal behaviour and projective identification (invaluable diagnostic and formulation data).
  - *Subjective Countertransference:* Clinician's personal unresolved conflicts activated by the patient (requires personal reflection and supervision).
- **Clinical Utility & Enactment:** Recognizing transference and countertransference prevents unconscious acting out (**enactment**), maintains professional boundaries, and provides direct insight into the patient's internal object relations.
- **Splitting & Team Dynamics (Q91a):**
  - *Mechanism:* Primitive defense mechanism where self and others are perceived as entirely "all-good" (idealized) or "all-bad" (devalued), unable to integrate ambivalent qualities.
  - *Team Impact:* Manifests as staff polarisation—some team members over-identify and become protective ("good/caring staff"), while others feel angry, frustrated, and punitive ("bad/strict staff"). Leads to inconsistent care plans, boundary breaches, and severe patient dysregulation.
  - *Team Management:*
    1. *Communication:* Regular, open multidisciplinary team (MDT) meetings to recognize splitting early.
    2. *Shared Formulation:* Develop a unified bio-psycho-social formulation explaining splitting as a trauma-based defense against abandonment.
    3. *Consistent Boundaries:* Establish clear, transparent, uniform ward/clinic rules and care plans applied equally by all staff.
    4. *Avoid Punitive Responses:* Non-punitive, calm, boundaried stance without retaliatory rejection or special exceptions.
    5. *Reflective Practice & Supervision:* Dedicated team supervision to process intense countertransference anger or rescue fantasies.

#### Supportive vs Exploratory Psychotherapy (Ch. 24, pp. 681–685)
- **Supportive Psychotherapy:**
  - *Core Aim:* Restore emotional equilibrium, strengthen existing ego defenses, enhance reality testing, reduce anxiety, and improve adaptive coping without uncovering deep unconscious conflicts.
  - *Techniques:* Active listening, emotional containment, validation, reassurance, practical advice, problem-solving, environmental modification, psychoeducation.
  - *Indications:* Patients with limited ego strength, acute crisis/severe life stressors, active psychotic or severe affective illness, significant cognitive impairment, or severe personality disorganization where exploratory therapy could precipitate decompensation.
- **Exploratory (Insight-Oriented / Expressive) Psychotherapy:**
  - *Core Aim:* Uncover unconscious conflicts, analyze defenses, interpret transference patterns, and facilitate personality restructuring.
  - *Techniques:* Clarification, confrontation, interpretation of unconscious defenses and transference, dream analysis, tolerating emotional ambiguity and regression.
  - *Indications:* Patients with sufficient ego strength, psychological mindedness, capacity for emotional reflection, stable impulse control, and ability to form a collaborative therapeutic alliance.

#### Interpersonal Psychotherapy (IPT) (Ch. 24, pp. 698–700)
- **Core Framework:** Time-limited (12–16 sessions), structured, manualized psychotherapy developed by Klerman and Weissman. Posits that psychiatric symptoms (especially depression) occur in an interpersonal context and that improving interpersonal functioning relieves depressive symptoms.
- **The 4 Interpersonal Focus Areas:**
  1. *Unresolved Grief / Bereavement:* Facilitating the mourning process, emotional catharsis, and developing new relationships following the death of a significant person.
  2. *Interpersonal Role Disputes:* Resolving overt or covert conflicts with a partner, family member, or colleague through communication analysis and problem-solving.
  3. *Role Transitions:* Coping with major life changes (e.g. divorce, retirement, diagnosis of illness, job loss, parenthood) by mourning the loss of the old role and acquiring competence in the new role.
  4. *Interpersonal Deficits / Sensitivity:* Addressing chronic social isolation, lack of relationships, or pervasive interpersonal friction through social skills training and communication analysis.
- **Primary Indications:** Major depressive disorder (acute and maintenance), adolescent depression, postnatal depression. (Adapted for bulimia nervosa and bipolar disorder as IPSRT).

#### Psychoeducation (Ch. 24, pp. 702–704 & Ch. 10, 11)
- **Definition & Rationale:** Systematic, structured, evidence-based educational intervention providing patients and families with comprehensive knowledge regarding the illness, neurobiology, treatment options, prognosis, and self-management strategies.
- **Core Components:**
  1. *Illness Understanding:* De-stigmatizing medical explanation of the disorder, correcting misconceptions, and framing vulnerability within a stress-vulnerability model.
  2. *Medication Knowledge & Adherence:* Transparent discussion of mechanism, benefits, timeline of response, management of side effects, and risks of abrupt cessation.
  3. *Relapse Signatures & Early Warning Signs:* Identifying idiosyncratic prodromal symptoms (e.g. sleep reduction, irritability, social withdrawal, mild paranoia) and establishing a written crisis relapse prevention plan.
  4. *Lifestyle & Coping:* Sleep hygiene, substance avoidance, stress management, regular daily routine.
  5. *Family & Carer Involvement:* Enhancing family communication, reducing high Expressed Emotion (EE: critical comments, hostility, emotional over-involvement), and reducing caregiver burden.
  6. *Shared Decision-Making:* Empowering active patient partnership in treatment choices.
- **Exemplars:** Proven efficacy in reducing relapse rates and hospitalizations in **Bipolar Disorder** (Colom group psychoeducation model) and **Schizophrenia** (family psychoeducation).

#### Frontal-Subcortical & Limbic Circuits (Ch. 5, pp. 110–118)
- **The 3 Principal Frontal-Subcortical Circuits:**
  1. *Dorsolateral Prefrontal Circuit (DLPFC $\rightarrow$ Dorsolateral Caudate $\rightarrow$ Globus Pallidus / Substantia Nigra $\rightarrow$ VA/MD Thalamus $\rightarrow$ DLPFC):*
     - *Function:* Executive functioning, working memory, cognitive flexibility, abstract reasoning, set-shifting, planning, problem-solving.
     - *Lesion / Dysfunction:* **Executive Dysexecutive Syndrome** — cognitive rigidity, perseveration, poor working memory, distractibility, disorganization (observed in schizophrenia, major depression, vascular dementia, ADHD).
  2. *Orbitofrontal Circuit (OFC / Ventromedial $\rightarrow$ Ventromedial Caudate $\rightarrow$ Globus Pallidus $\rightarrow$ MD Thalamus $\rightarrow$ OFC):*
     - *Function:* Social judgment, impulse control, emotional regulation, evaluation of reward and punishment, moral reasoning.
     - *Lesion / Dysfunction:* **Disinhibition Syndrome** — emotional lability, impulsivity, tactlessness, socially inappropriate behaviour, hyperorality, moral agnosia (observed in behavioural variant FTD, brain injury, mania, psychopathy).
  3. *Anterior Cingulate Circuit (ACC $\rightarrow$ Ventral Striatum / Nucleus Accumbens $\rightarrow$ Ventral Pallidum $\rightarrow$ MD Thalamus $\rightarrow$ ACC):*
     - *Function:* Motivated behaviour, initiative, conflict monitoring, error detection, emotional salience.
     - *Lesion / Dysfunction:* **Akinetic / Apathetic Syndrome** — apathy, abulia, psychic akinesia, loss of spontaneous speech and movement, akinetic mutism (observed in severe depression, negative symptoms of schizophrenia, ACA strokes).
- **Key Limbic Structures:**
  - *Amygdala:* Processing affective salience, threat detection, fear conditioning, emotional memory. Hyperactivity linked to anxiety disorders, PTSD, and depression; hypoactivity/structural deficits linked to callous-unemotional traits.
  - *Hippocampus:* Declarative/episodic memory encoding and consolidation, spatial navigation, context conditioning, neurogenesis, and negative feedback regulation of the HPA axis. Volume reduction observed in chronic depression, PTSD, and Alzheimer's disease.
  - *Ventral Striatum (Nucleus Accumbens):* Core hub of the brain reward system; evaluates reward anticipation and incentive salience.

#### Major Dopamine Pathways & Clinical Significance (Ch. 5, pp. 110–114 & Ch. 25)
- **The 4 Major Dopamine Pathways:**
  1. *Mesolimbic Pathway (VTA $\rightarrow$ Nucleus Accumbens / Limbic Cortex):*
     - *Function:* Mediates reward, pleasure, motivation, incentive salience, and positive reinforcement.
     - *Psychiatric Role:* Hyperactivity of D2 neurotransmission mediates **positive psychotic symptoms** (delusions, auditory hallucinations, thought disorder). Antipsychotic D2 blockade in this pathway resolves positive symptoms. Also mediates the rewarding effects of addictive substances.
  2. *Mesocortical Pathway (VTA $\rightarrow$ Dorsolateral & Ventromedial Prefrontal Cortex):*
     - *Function:* Mediates cognitive functioning, working memory, executive function, motivation, and emotional expression.
     - *Psychiatric Role:* Hypoactivity / dopamine deficiency mediates **negative symptoms** (avolition, alogia, affective flattening) and **cognitive deficits** of schizophrenia. (Explains why non-selective high-potency D2 blockers can worsen cognitive and negative symptoms).
  3. *Nigrostriatal Pathway (Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta $\rightarrow$ Dorsal Striatum [Caudate & Putamen]):*
     - *Function:* Controls motor coordination, voluntary movement initiation, and habit formation.
     - *Psychiatric Role:* Part of the extrapyramidal system. Antipsychotic D2 blockade (>80% occupancy) leads to **Extrapyramidal Side Effects (EPS)** (acute dystonia, parkinsonism, akathisia). Chronic blockade leads to D2 receptor up-regulation/supersensitivity causing **Tardive Dyskinesia (TD)**. Degeneration in Parkinson's disease causes motor parkinsonism.
  4. *Tuberoinfundibular Pathway (Arcuate Nucleus of Hypothalamus $\rightarrow$ Anterior Pituitary Gland):*
     - *Function:* Dopamine acts as Prolactin-Inhibiting Factor (PIF), tonically inhibiting prolactin release.
     - *Psychiatric Role:* Antipsychotic D2 blockade disinhibits prolactin release, causing **Hyperprolactinemia** (galactorrhea, amenorrhea, sexual dysfunction, gynecomastia, long-term osteoporosis).
- *Schizophrenia Balanced Formulation:* Schizophrenia is NOT "too much dopamine everywhere", but rather a regional imbalance: **subcortical mesolimbic hyperdopaminergia** (positive symptoms) co-occurring with **prefrontal mesocortical hypodopaminergia** (negative/cognitive symptoms).

#### Heritability, Familial Risk & Polygenic Architecture (Ch. 5, pp. 101–110)
- **Heritability ($h^2$):** The proportion of total phenotypic variance of a trait or disorder within a specific population that is attributable to genetic variance ($V_G / V_P$).
  - *Crucial Board Distinctions:* Heritability is a **population-level statistic**, NOT an individual's genetic destiny or risk. It does NOT mean the proportion of a single individual's illness caused by genes. It varies depending on environmental diversity in the population studied.
- **Methodologies:**
  - *Familial Aggregation:* Demonstrates that disorders cluster in families; calculates Relative Risk to First-Degree Relatives ($\lambda_R$). Does not separate shared genes from shared environment.
  - *Twin Studies:* Compares concordance rates between Monozygotic (MZ, 100% shared genes) and Dizygotic (DZ, 50% shared genes) twins. Excess MZ concordance indicates genetic influence.
  - *Adoption Studies:* Separates biological parents (genetic influence) from adoptive environment (environmental influence).
- **Representative Heritability Estimates in Psychiatry (Oxford 7th ed):**
  - Autism Spectrum Disorder: ~80%–90%
  - Bipolar Disorder: ~70%–80%
  - Schizophrenia: ~70%–80%
  - ADHD: ~70%–80%
  - Alzheimer's Disease: ~60%–70%
  - Major Depressive Disorder: ~35%–40%
  - Generalized Anxiety Disorder: ~30%–35%
- **Polygenic Architecture & Variants:**
  - Most psychiatric disorders are highly polygenic, resulting from the cumulative effect of **hundreds to thousands of common Single Nucleotide Polymorphisms (SNPs)**, each conferring a tiny individual risk (Odds Ratios typically 1.05–1.20).
  - *Rare Variants & Copy Number Variations (CNVs):* High-penetrance structural microdeletions/duplications (e.g. 22q11.2 deletion, 16p11.2, 1q21.1) confer large individual risk for schizophrenia, autism, and intellectual disability, but account for only a small fraction of total cases.
  - *Genetic Pleiotropy / Correlation:* Significant genetic overlap exists across major diagnostic categories (e.g. schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, major depression, ADHD, ASD share substantial polygenic vulnerability).
  - *Polygenic Risk Scores (PRS):* Calculate aggregate genetic burden. Currently valuable for population-level research, but lack sufficient sensitivity and specificity for individual clinical diagnosis.

#### Critical Appraisal of Psychiatric Studies (Ch. 6, pp. 119–134)
- **Core Appraisal Framework (PICO):**
  - **P**opulation: Definition, diagnostic criteria, inclusion/exclusion criteria, representative sample.
  - **I**ntervention: Specificity of drug dose/regimen, manualized psychotherapy, duration.
  - **C**omparator: Placebo vs active comparator, gold-standard dosing, blinding integrity.
  - **O**utcomes: Clinically meaningful primary endpoints, validated rating scales (e.g. MADRS, PANSS), secondary outcomes, adverse effects.
- **Key Methodological Quality Elements in RCTs:**
  - *Randomisation:* Method of sequence generation (computer-generated random numbers) to prevent selection bias.
  - *Allocation Concealment:* Strict protection of the randomization sequence before assignment (e.g. centralized pharmacy, opaque sealed sequentially numbered envelopes). Prevents clinicians from influencing patient enrollment based on knowing the next treatment assignment. (Distinct from blinding!).
  - *Blinding (Masking):* Single (patient), Double (patient and investigator/outcome assessor), Triple (statistician also blinded). Evaluates whether treatment code could be broken by recognizable side effects (e.g. dry mouth, sedation).
  - *Statistical Power & Sample Size:* Pre-study power calculation ($1 - \beta$, usually $\ge 80\%$) based on expected effect size ($\alpha = 0.05$) to avoid Type II error (false negative).
  - *Attrition & Handling of Dropouts:* Attrition rate and differential dropout between arms.
  - *Intention-to-Treat (ITT) Analysis:* All randomized patients are analyzed in the group to which they were originally assigned, regardless of whether they completed the treatment or deviated from protocol. Preserves randomization, reflects real-world clinical effectiveness, and prevents attrition bias. (Contrast with *Per-Protocol* analysis, which overestimates efficacy).
- **Statistical vs Clinical Significance & Metrics:**
  - *Effect Sizes:* Standardized Mean Difference (Cohen's $d$: 0.2 small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large); Odds Ratio (OR); Relative Risk (RR).
  - *Confidence Intervals (95% CI):* Quantify precision of the estimate. If the 95% CI for RR/OR includes 1.0, or for mean difference includes 0, the finding is not statistically significant at $p < 0.05$. Wide CIs denote small sample sizes.
  - *Absolute Risk Reduction (ARR):* $ARR = \text{Control Event Rate} - \text{Experimental Event Rate}$.
  - *Number Needed to Treat (NNT):* Reciprocal of ARR ($NNT = 1 / ARR$). Number of patients who must receive the intervention for one additional patient to achieve a positive outcome compared with control.
  - *Number Needed to Harm (NNH):* Reciprocal of Absolute Risk Increase ($NNH = 1 / ARI$).
  - *Statistical vs Clinical Significance:* A statistically significant result ($p < 0.05$) in a massive sample may reflect a clinically trivial difference (e.g. 1-point change on a 60-point scale).
- **Sources of Bias & Confounding:** Selection bias, performance bias, detection bias, attrition bias, reporting/publication bias, sponsorship/funding conflicts of interest.
- **Practice Change Criteria:** Does the study address an important clinical question? Is the effect size clinically meaningful? Do the benefits outweigh the harms (NNT vs NNH)? Are the results generalizable to my specific patient population?

#### Medical Confidentiality & Ethics in Greece (Ch. 4, pp. 73–76)
- **Core Ethical Duty:** Confidentiality is the cornerstone of the doctor-patient relationship, fostering trust, open disclosure of sensitive symptoms, and protection of patient autonomy and dignity.
- **General Principles:**
  - All information obtained during psychiatric assessment and treatment (written records, verbal disclosures, laboratory results) is strictly confidential.
  - *Informed Consent for Disclosure:* Disclosure to third parties (employers, insurance, relatives) requires explicit, informed, voluntary consent from the patient.
  - *Minimum Necessary Disclosure:* When disclosure is authorized or required, only the minimum relevant information necessary to achieve the specific purpose should be shared.
  - *Multidisciplinary Team Sharing:* Clinical information may be shared among treating MDT members on a "need-to-know" basis for direct patient care, with patient aware of team-based care.
  - *Deceased Patients:* The duty of confidentiality extends beyond the patient's death.
- **Exceptions to Confidentiality:**
  - *Statutory Legal Requirements:* Court orders, statutory reporting of communicable diseases, child abuse, or elder abuse.
  - *Emergency Prevention of Severe Harm / Public Interest:* When failure to disclose would result in immediate, severe, life-threatening harm to the patient or an identifiable third party.
- **Greek Legal Framework Flags:**
  - Regulated under the **Greek Code of Medical Ethics (Law 3418/2005, Article 13)** and the **Greek Penal Code (Article 371 — Breach of Professional Secrecy)**.
  - GDPR (EU Regulation 2016/679) and Law 4624/2019 govern health data protection.

#### Imminent Violence & Third-Party Protection (Q98a) (Ch. 4, pp. 73–76 & Ch. 18)
- **Clinical Risk Assessment of Violent Threats:**
  - Evaluate threat characteristics: *Specificity* (vague vs highly detailed plan), *Intent* (ambivalent vs determined), *Means & Feasibility* (access to weapons/lethal means), *Target* (identifiable victim vs general hostility), *Imminence* (immediate execution vs distant thoughts), *History* (prior violence, impulse control), *Substance Use* (acute intoxication).
- **Clinical Harm-Reduction Hierarchy:**
  1. Deepen clinical assessment and establish therapeutic containment.
  2. Optimize psychiatric treatment (voluntary admission, medication adjustment, crisis intervention).
  3. Explore voluntary involving of support systems or potential victim.
  4. Initiate emergency involuntary psychiatric assessment / hospitalisation if civil commitment criteria are met.
- **Emergency Disclosure to Prevent Harm:**
  - If a patient makes a credible, specific, imminent threat of serious bodily harm or murder against an identifiable victim, and clinical interventions cannot contain the risk, breaching confidentiality to notify law enforcement or warn the victim may be legally and ethically justified under the legal defense of necessity (**justified emergency state**).
  - *Information Minimization:* Disclose only the minimum necessary information to avert the danger (identity of threat, target, nature of danger), document detailed clinical reasoning and senior consultation.
- **Legal Distinction Warning:** The US "Tarasoff Duty to Warn/Protect" is a California common-law doctrine. **Do NOT import Tarasoff as Greek statutory law.** In Greece, disclosure is governed by Greek Penal Code provisions on necessity (Articles 25 and 371) and Law 3418/2005.

#### Social Media & Professional Boundaries (Q98b) (Ch. 4, pp. 73–76)
- **Professional Boundaries & Online Ethics:**
  - *Confidentiality:* Absolute prohibition against posting identifiable patient clinical scenarios, images, or details online, even in closed professional forums or with superficial modifications.
  - *Dual Relationships & Friend Requests:* Psychiatrists should decline "friend" or "follow" requests from current or former patients on personal social media accounts to maintain clear therapeutic boundaries and prevent role confusion.
  - *Online Searching of Patients:* Routine unprompted internet/social media searches of patients without clinical justification or consent violate patient trust and privacy (except in rare emergency risk assessment where safety is at stake).
  - *Personal-Professional Separation:* Maintain strict separation between personal social media presence and professional practice; ensure high privacy settings.

#### Criminal Responsibility: Clinical Assessment vs Legal Determinations (Q99) (Ch. 18, pp. 528–534)
- **Core Conceptual Distinction:**
  - *Psychiatric Diagnosis $\ne$ Legal Irresponsibility:* Having a severe mental illness (e.g. schizophrenia, bipolar disorder) does NOT automatically render a defendant legally insane or criminally non-responsible.
  - *Legal Responsibility:* A legal and normative judgment made exclusively by the **Court / Judge**, based on statutory legal tests, assessing whether the defendant's mental disorder impaired their legal capacity at the precise time of the alleged offence.
- **The Core Components of Criminal Capacity:**
  1. *Cognitive Limb:* Capacity to appreciate the nature, quality, and unlawfulness/wrongfulness of the act (understanding that the act is morally and legally wrong).
  2. *Volitional Limb:* Ability to conform one's conduct to that understanding / control one's actions according to that realization.
- **Forensic Psychiatric Evaluation Methodology:**
  - *Retrospective Assessment:* Reconstructing the defendant's exact mental state at the time of the offence.
  - *Comprehensive Collateral:* Police records, witness statements, crime scene photographs, prior psychiatric records, toxicology reports.
  - *Malingering Detection:* Systematic screening for feigned psychosis or exaggerated symptoms (atypical hallucinations, sudden onset without prodrome, inconsistency).
  - *Substance Intoxication Rules:* Voluntary acute intoxication typically does NOT exculpate criminal responsibility.
- **Greek Legal Framework Flags:**
  - Governed by **Greek Penal Code Article 34** (Non-Imputability / Insanity: lack of capacity to perceive the wrongful nature of the act or act in accordance with this perception due to morbid disturbance of mental activity or consciousness) and **Article 36** (Diminished Responsibility / Reduced Imputability).
  - Separate from UK M'Naghten Rules or US ALI tests.

#### Principles of Community Care for Severe Mental Illness (SMI) (Ch. 26, pp. 777–800)
- **Core Principles:**
  1. *Multidisciplinary Team (MDT) Base:* Care delivered by integrated generic Community Mental Health Teams (CMHTs: psychiatrists, community psychiatric nurses, social workers, clinical psychologists, occupational therapists).
  2. *Continuity of Care & Key Working:* Named care coordinator / key worker providing continuous therapeutic relationship and coordinating all health and social interventions.
  3. *Assertive Outreach / Assertive Community Treatment (ACT):* Low caseloads (e.g. 1:10), frequent home visits, proactive engagement, delivering care in natural environments for hard-to-engage patients with high hospital utilization.
  4. *Crisis Resolution and Home Treatment (CRHT):* 24/7 rapid assessment and intensive home-based treatment as an alternative to acute hospital admission.
  5. *Psychiatric Rehabilitation & Recovery Orientation:* Fostering personal autonomy, hope, social inclusion, and meaningful life goals beyond symptom remission.
  6. *Supported Housing & Employment:* Graded residential support (group homes, supported flats) and Evidence-Based Supported Employment (e.g. **Individual Placement and Support [IPS]** model).
  7. *Family & Carer Interventions:* Family psychoeducation, coping skills, and support networks to reduce family stress.
  8. *Physical Health Monitoring:* Systematic screening for cardiovascular disease, diabetes, obesity, and smoking in SMI patients receiving antipsychotics.
  9. *Dual Diagnosis Care:* Integrated treatment for co-occurring severe mental illness and substance use disorders.
  10. *Least Restrictive Care:* Providing treatment in the least restrictive setting possible, close to the patient's home and social network.
- **Greek Mental Health System Flags:**
  - Transition from asylum-based care to Community Mental Health Centres (Κέντρα Ψυχικής Υγείας - ΚΨΥ), Mobile Psychiatric Units (Κινητές Μονάδες), Day Centres, and Supported Living Structures (Οικοτροφεία, Ξενώνες).
  - Flag recent system reform (**Law 5129/2024**) for current administrative verification.

### 2.2 Diagnostic & classification distinctions

| Concept A | Concept B | Core Distinguishing Feature in Oxford / Psychiatry |
|---|---|---|
| **Transference** | **Countertransference** | Transference is the patient's unconscious projection onto the therapist; Countertransference is the therapist's emotional reaction to the patient. |
| **Supportive Psychotherapy** | **Exploratory Psychotherapy** | Supportive aims to strengthen ego defenses and restore stability; Exploratory aims to uncover unconscious conflicts and analyze defenses. |
| **Randomisation** | **Allocation Concealment** | Randomisation generates an unpredictable sequence; Allocation concealment protects that sequence until assignment occurs to prevent selection bias. |
| **Intention-to-Treat (ITT)** | **Per-Protocol Analysis** | ITT analyzes all patients as randomized (reflects real-world effectiveness); Per-protocol analyzes only completers (overestimates efficacy). |
| **Statistical Significance** | **Clinical Significance** | Statistical ($p < 0.05$) indicates an effect is unlikely due to chance; Clinical indicates the magnitude of effect (NNT, Cohen's $d$) is meaningful in patient care. |
| **Medical Confidentiality** | **Legal Duty to Disclose** | Confidentiality is the primary ethical duty; statutory legal disclosure or emergency prevention of imminent life-threatening harm are narrow exceptions. |
| **Psychiatric Diagnosis** | **Criminal Responsibility** | Diagnosis is a medical finding; Criminal responsibility is a legal judgment made by the court based on cognitive and volitional capacity at the time of the offence. |

### 2.4 Aetiology, neurobiology & circuitry matrices

- **DLPFC Circuit:** Dorsolateral prefrontal cortex $\leftrightarrow$ dorsolateral caudate $\leftrightarrow$ thalamus $\rightarrow$ Executive function, working memory, set-shifting.
- **OFC Circuit:** Orbitofrontal cortex $\leftrightarrow$ ventromedial caudate $\leftrightarrow$ thalamus $\rightarrow$ Impulse control, social conduct, reward valuation.
- **ACC Circuit:** Anterior cingulate cortex $\leftrightarrow$ ventral striatum (nucleus accumbens) $\leftrightarrow$ thalamus $\rightarrow$ Motivated behaviour, error detection, emotional salience.
- **Mesolimbic Dopamine:** VTA $\rightarrow$ Nucleus Accumbens $\rightarrow$ Reward, incentive salience, positive psychotic symptoms.
- **Mesocortical Dopamine:** VTA $\rightarrow$ Prefrontal Cortex $\rightarrow$ Executive function, motivation, negative/cognitive symptoms.
- **Nigrostriatal Dopamine:** Substantia Nigra $\rightarrow$ Dorsal Striatum $\rightarrow$ Motor control, EPS, tardive dyskinesia.
- **Tuberoinfundibular Dopamine:** Hypothalamus $\rightarrow$ Pituitary $\rightarrow$ Tonic prolactin inhibition.

### 2.5 Course & prognosis

- **CBT Efficacy:** First-line evidence-based psychotherapy for unipolar depression, panic disorder, SAD, OCD, PTSD, and bulimia nervosa. Relapse rates following successful CBT are lower than after pharmacotherapy discontinuation.
- **Heritability of Severe Mental Illness:** Bipolar disorder and Schizophrenia exhibit high heritability (~70%–80%), yet polygenic risk scores remain probabilistic, not deterministic.
- **Community Care Impact:** Assertive outreach and crisis resolution reduce hospital bed days, improve treatment engagement, and enhance patient satisfaction compared to standard hospital-centric care.

### 2.6 Psychotherapeutic & clinical protocols

#### CBT Session Structure & Formulation Protocol (Q90)
1. Set collaborative agenda.
2. Review mood and homework from prior session.
3. Address main agenda topic using cognitive restructuring / behavioural experiments.
4. Assign collaborative homework for between-session practice.
5. Summarize session and solicit mutual feedback.

#### Multidisciplinary Team Splitting Management Protocol (Q91a)
1. Recognize splitting early when staff members express sharply polarized attitudes (pity/rescue vs anger/punishment).
2. Convene a dedicated multidisciplinary team meeting.
3. Formulate the splitting behavior as a trauma-based defense against abandonment.
4. Establish clear, agreed-upon, written care boundaries and consistent staff responses.
5. Ensure ongoing peer supervision and reflective practice.

#### Forensic Criminal Responsibility Assessment Protocol (Q99)
1. Comprehensive clinical psychiatric examination and mental state examination.
2. Detailed retrospective reconstruction of symptoms, cognition, and behaviour at the exact time of the offence.
3. Review of extensive collateral data (police reports, witness testimonies, interrogation transcripts, toxicology).
4. Formal evaluation of cognitive capacity (understanding wrongfulness) and volitional capacity (ability to act according to understanding).
5. Screening for malingering, substance intoxication, and personality factors.

## 3. Question map

### Q90 — Cognitive Behaviour Therapy (CBT)
- **Spine:** (1) Core model: emotional distress maintained by cognitive appraisals and unhelpful behaviour; (2) Collaborative empiricism (therapist-patient investigative team); (3) Cognitive formulation: schemas, intermediate beliefs, automatic thoughts; (4) Interactive loop: Thoughts $\leftrightarrow$ Emotions $\leftrightarrow$ Physiology $\leftrightarrow$ Behaviour; (5) Cognitive distortions (all-or-nothing, catastrophizing, mind reading, mental filter); (6) Key techniques: cognitive restructuring, Socratic questioning, behavioural experiments, graded exposure, behavioural activation, homework, relapse prevention; (7) Broad indications across depression, anxiety, OCD, PTSD, bulimia, insomnia, psychosis.
- **Keepers:** Collaborative empiricism concept; Cognitive distortion types; Interactive 4-domain loop; Socratic dialogue and behavioural experiments.
- **Verify currently:** Specific modern third-wave CBT variants (ACT, MBCT) in guideline hierarchies.
- **Boundaries:** Individual disorders in respective clinical chapters.

### Q91 — Transference & Countertransference
- **Spine:** (1) Transference: unconscious redirection of childhood relational feelings/expectations onto the therapist; (2) Countertransference: clinician's emotional/behavioural reactions to the patient (objective diagnostic vs subjective personal); (3) Clinical importance: diagnostic tool, reveals internal object relations, prevents acting out / enactment; (4) Maintaining boundaries and using supervision.
- **Keepers:** Transference vs countertransference definitions; Objective countertransference as diagnostic tool; Supervision and boundary maintenance.
- **Verify currently:** Modern psychodynamic psychotherapy evidence base.
- **Boundaries:** Splitting in Q91a; Supportive therapy in Q91b.

### Q91a — Idealisation, Devaluation & Team Splitting
- **Spine:** (1) Splitting mechanism: primitive defense viewing others as all-good or all-bad; (2) Team impact: staff polarisation, inconsistent care plans, boundary breaches, anger vs rescue fantasies; (3) Management: urgent MDT communication, shared formulation, uniform consistent boundaries, non-punitive stance, team supervision/reflection.
- **Keepers:** Team polarisation mechanism; Shared formulation necessity; Consistent uniform boundaries.
- **Verify currently:** Personality disorder team-based management guidelines.
- **Boundaries:** General transference in Q91; BPD clinical management in Q69.

### Q91b — Supportive vs Exploratory Psychotherapy
- **Spine:** (1) Supportive therapy: aims to strengthen ego defenses, restore stability, enhance reality testing; techniques: listening, reassurance, advice, problem-solving; indications: acute crisis, severe psychosis, cognitive deficit, low ego strength; (2) Exploratory therapy: aims to uncover unconscious conflicts and analyze defenses; techniques: interpretation, confrontation, dream analysis; indications: good ego strength, psychological mindedness, stable alliance.
- **Keepers:** Ego defense strengthening vs uncovering unconscious conflict; Ego strength indication criteria.
- **Verify currently:** Criteria for psychodynamic psychotherapy selection.
- **Boundaries:** CBT in Q90; IPT in Q92.

### Q92 — Interpersonal Psychotherapy (IPT)
- **Spine:** (1) Framework: time-limited (12–16 sessions), structured, manualized therapy linking symptoms to interpersonal context; (2) The 4 Problem Areas: Unresolved Grief, Interpersonal Role Disputes, Role Transitions, Interpersonal Deficits/Sensitivity; (3) Techniques: communication analysis, role play, problem-solving; (4) Primary indication: Major Depressive Disorder (acute and maintenance).
- **Keepers:** 4 core interpersonal problem areas; Time-limited structured format; Depression primary indication.
- **Verify currently:** IPT indications in perinatal depression and eating disorders.
- **Boundaries:** CBT in Q90; Psychodynamic therapy in Q91.

### Q93 — Psychoeducation
- **Spine:** (1) Definition: structured educational intervention on illness, biology, treatments, and coping; (2) Core components: illness understanding, medication mechanism/adherence, relapse signatures / early warning signs, crisis planning, lifestyle regularisation, family/carer involvement (reducing Expressed Emotion), shared decision-making; (3) Proven efficacy in Bipolar Disorder (Colom model) and Schizophrenia (family psychoeducation).
- **Keepers:** Relapse signatures / early warning signs identification; Expressed Emotion reduction; Bipolar and Schizophrenia evidence.
- **Verify currently:** Digital and group psychoeducation trial evidence.
- **Boundaries:** General treatment planning in clinical chapters.

### Q94 — Frontal-Subcortical & Limbic Circuits
- **Spine:** (1) Frontal-subcortical architecture: cortex $\rightarrow$ striatum $\rightarrow$ pallidum/substantia nigra $\rightarrow$ thalamus $\rightarrow$ cortex; (2) DLPFC circuit: executive function, working memory, set-shifting $\rightarrow$ Dysexecutive syndrome; (3) OFC circuit: impulse control, social judgment, reward valuation $\rightarrow$ Disinhibition syndrome; (4) ACC circuit: motivation, error detection, emotional salience $\rightarrow$ Apathetic/akinetic syndrome; (5) Limbic structures: Amygdala (threat/salience), Hippocampus (episodic memory, HPA regulation), Ventral Striatum (reward).
- **Keepers:** The 3 distinct frontal-subcortical circuits and their clinical syndromes; Amygdala vs Hippocampus roles.
- **Verify currently:** Modern functional neuroimaging connectivity networks (Default Mode, Salience, Central Executive).
- **Boundaries:** Neuroanatomy textbook details beyond psychiatric syndromes.

### Q95 — Major Dopamine Pathways
- **Spine:** (1) Mesolimbic: VTA $\rightarrow$ Nucleus Accumbens (reward, incentive salience, positive psychotic symptoms; D2 blockade treats psychosis); (2) Mesocortical: VTA $\rightarrow$ Prefrontal cortex (cognition, motivation, negative symptoms; hypodopaminergia); (3) Nigrostriatal: Substantia Nigra $\rightarrow$ Dorsal Striatum (motor control, EPS, tardive dyskinesia); (4) Tuberoinfundibular: Hypothalamus $\rightarrow$ Pituitary (tonic prolactin inhibition; D2 blockade causes hyperprolactinemia); (5) Regional dopamine imbalance in schizophrenia.
- **Keepers:** The 4 pathways and their distinct clinical consequences; Avoidance of simplistic "too much dopamine everywhere" view.
- **Verify currently:** D1, D2, D3 receptor subtype distribution.
- **Boundaries:** Antipsychotic pharmacology in Q76–Q78.

### Q96 — Heritability, Familial Risk & Polygenic Architecture
- **Spine:** (1) Heritability ($h^2$): population-level variance attributable to genetic variance ($V_G/V_P$), NOT individual risk or destiny; (2) Methodologies: familial aggregation ($\lambda_R$), twin studies (MZ vs DZ concordance), adoption studies; (3) Polygenic architecture: thousands of common SNPs of tiny effect (OR 1.05–1.20); (4) Rare variants & CNVs (22q11.2, 16p11.2) of high penetrance; (5) Genetic pleiotropy across disorders; (6) Gene-environment interplay; (7) Polygenic Risk Scores (PRS) lack individual diagnostic utility.
- **Keepers:** Heritability as population variance definition; Polygenic common SNPs vs rare CNVs; Genetic pleiotropy concept.
- **Verify currently:** Clinical utility status of Polygenic Risk Scores (PRS).
- **Boundaries:** Specific genetic syndromes in child/genetics chapters.

### Q97 — Critical Appraisal of a Psychiatric Study
- **Spine:** (1) PICO framework (Population, Intervention, Comparator, Outcomes); (2) RCT Quality: true randomisation, allocation concealment (opaque sealed envelopes), blinding (single/double/triple), power calculation/sample size ($1-\beta \ge 80\%$), attrition; (3) Intention-to-Treat (ITT) analysis vs per-protocol; (4) Metrics: Cohen's $d$, OR, RR, 95% Confidence Intervals, ARR, NNT/NNH; (5) Statistical ($p < 0.05$) vs Clinical significance; (6) Biases, confounding, multiplicity, conflicts of interest; (7) Practice change criteria.
- **Keepers:** Allocation concealment vs blinding distinction; Intention-to-Treat (ITT) definition; NNT formula ($1/ARR$); Statistical vs clinical significance.
- **Verify currently:** GRADE methodology and modern reporting standards (CONSORT, PRISMA).
- **Boundaries:** Mathematical derivations beyond clinical application.

### Q98 — Psychiatrist's Duties of Confidentiality in Greece
- **Spine:** (1) Core ethical duty: foundation of trust, autonomy, and open disclosure; (2) General rules: all clinical data confidential, informed consent for disclosure, minimum necessary info, MDT need-to-know sharing, duty extends post-mortem; (3) Exceptions: statutory reporting (abuse, court orders), emergency prevention of imminent life-threatening harm; (4) Greek Legal Framework: Greek Medical Code (Law 3418/2005 Art. 13), Greek Penal Code (Art. 371), GDPR & Law 4624/2019.
- **Keepers:** Core duty and consent rules; Minimum necessary disclosure; Separate UK law from Greek statutory framework.
- **Verify currently:** Greek Code of Medical Ethics (Law 3418/2005 Art. 13) and GDPR health data compliance.
- **Boundaries:** Violence threats in Q98a; Social media in Q98b.

### Q98a — Credible Imminent Violence Threats & Third-Party Protection
- **Spine:** (1) Risk assessment: specificity, intent, means, access, identifiable target, imminence, history, intoxication; (2) Clinical harm reduction: hospitalisation, treatment adjustment, crisis intervention; (3) Emergency disclosure: justified under state of necessity (Greek Penal Code Art. 25/371) to prevent imminent severe harm; (4) Disclose minimum necessary information to police/target; document reasoning; (5) Do NOT import US Tarasoff doctrine as Greek law.
- **Keepers:** Specificity/intent/means risk assessment; State of necessity emergency disclosure; Exclusion of Tarasoff as Greek statute.
- **Verify currently:** Greek jurisprudence regarding medical breach of secrecy under emergency necessity.
- **Boundaries:** General confidentiality in Q98.

### Q98b — Professional Boundaries & Social Media
- **Spine:** (1) Confidentiality: absolute prohibition against posting identifiable patient material online; (2) Boundaries: decline patient friend/follow requests on personal accounts; (3) Privacy: avoid unconsented online searching of patients; (4) Separate personal and professional online profiles; maintain high privacy settings.
- **Keepers:** Patient friend request boundary rule; No unconsented searching of patients; Absolute online confidentiality.
- **Verify currently:** Modern medical board / Greek Medical Association social media guidelines.
- **Boundaries:** General medical ethics in Q98.

### Q99 — Criminal Responsibility: Clinical Assessment vs Greek Law
- **Spine:** (1) Conceptual difference: psychiatric diagnosis is a medical finding; criminal responsibility is a legal judgment made by the court; (2) Legal capacity components: Cognitive limb (understanding wrongfulness) and Volitional limb (acting according to understanding); (3) Forensic evaluation: retrospective assessment at time of offence, collateral (police, witnesses, toxicology), malingering screening; (4) Substance intoxication rules; (5) Greek Legal Framework: Greek Penal Code Article 34 (Non-Imputability / Insanity) and Article 36 (Diminished Responsibility).
- **Keepers:** Diagnosis $\ne$ legal insanity; Cognitive vs volitional limbs; Retrospective assessment; Greek Penal Code Articles 34 & 36 (separate from UK M'Naghten).
- **Verify currently:** Greek Penal Code Article 34/36 judicial interpretation and psychiatric expert witness procedure.
- **Boundaries:** Civil capacity in general law chapters.

### Q100 — Principles of Community Care for Severe Mental Illness
- **Spine:** (1) Multidisciplinary CMHT base; (2) Continuity of care & named care coordinator; (3) Assertive Outreach / ACT (low caseloads, home visits, engaging hard-to-reach SMI); (4) Crisis Resolution & Home Treatment (CRHT 24/7 admission avoidance); (5) Psychiatric rehabilitation & recovery model; (6) Supported housing & employment (IPS model); (7) Family work (reducing EE); (8) Physical health monitoring; (9) Integrated dual diagnosis care; (10) Least restrictive environment; (11) Greek structure: CMHCs (ΚΨΥ), Mobile Units, Day Centres, Supported Living, Law 5129/2024 reform.
- **Keepers:** Multidisciplinary CMHT core; Assertive outreach and CRHT roles; IPS supported employment; Recovery orientation.
- **Verify currently:** Greek Mental Health Reform (Law 5129/2024) and Regional Mental Health Networks structure.
- **Boundaries:** Inpatient ward management in acute chapters.

## 4. 11 Most Important Claims Requiring Current Verification

1. **Greek Medical Code of Ethics (Law 3418/2005 Article 13):** Oxford outlines general medical confidentiality and UK GMC rules. Specific Greek statutory confidentiality rules, exceptions, and professional disciplinary consequences require verification under Law 3418/2005.
2. **Greek Penal Code Article 34 & 36 on Criminal Responsibility:** Oxford describes UK legal tests (M'Naghten rules, diminished responsibility). Greek legal standards for non-imputability (Article 34) and reduced imputability (Article 36) require verification.
3. **Emergency Breach of Confidentiality under Greek Law:** Oxford discusses public interest exceptions. The legal defense of state of necessity (**Greek Penal Code Article 25 and Article 371**) for disclosing imminent violent threats requires verification.
4. **Greek Community Mental Health Structure & Law 5129/2024:** Oxford describes the UK NHS service model (CMHTs, AOTs, CRHTs). The reorganization of Greek community mental health services under **Law 5129/2024** (National Mental Health Network) requires verification.
5. **Polygenic Risk Score (PRS) Clinical Utility:** Oxford discusses polygenic architecture and GWAS. The current consensus regarding whether PRS should be used in individual psychiatric risk prediction requires verification.
6. **Individual Placement and Support (IPS) Evidence:** Oxford covers psychiatric rehabilitation and employment. International Cochrane/NICE evidence establishing IPS as the superior model for supported employment in SMI requires verification.
7. **Third-Wave CBT and ACT / MBCT Indications:** Oxford covers standard Beckian CBT. The formal positioning of Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy (MBCT) and Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) in major clinical guidelines requires verification.
8. **Digital CBT and Internet-Delivered Psychological Interventions:** Oxford mentions computer-based CBT. Current evidence and guideline approval of guided digital CBT applications require verification.
9. **GDPR Compliance in Psychiatric Records & Data Sharing:** Oxford covers medical records confidentiality. EU GDPR and Greek Law 4624/2019 requirements regarding special category health data and electronic health records require verification.
10. **Social Media Guidelines from Professional Bodies:** Oxford briefly touches upon doctor-patient boundaries. Specific guidelines from the World Psychiatric Association (WPA) and national medical councils regarding online interactions require verification.
11. **Family Psychoeducation in Schizophrenia & Bipolar Disorder:** Oxford highlights psychoeducation. Modern NICE and Cochrane recommendations regarding mandatory family interventions for psychosis require verification.
