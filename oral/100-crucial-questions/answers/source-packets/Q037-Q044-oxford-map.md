## Q37. Substance-use-disorder assessment and formulation

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Characterise substances, quantity/frequency, route, duration and context.
- Identify intoxication, withdrawal, tolerance/craving/loss of control and continued use despite harm.
- Assess psychiatric, physical, social, occupational/legal and safeguarding consequences.
- Ask about prescribed/OTC substances and polysubstance use.
- Review previous abstinence/treatment, motivation/readiness and goals.
- Assess overdose, seizure, self-harm/suicide, violence, driving/occupational and neglect risks.
- Use collateral information and relevant investigations where indicated.
- Formulate the substance syndrome together with comorbidity, maintaining factors and stage/readiness for change.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Structured substance history and consequence/risk assessment.
- Dependence concepts: tolerance, withdrawal, craving/loss of control and salience/continued use despite harm.
- Polysubstance and medical/psychiatric comorbidity assessment.

### Requires current verification / update
- ICD-10 harmful-use/dependence-syndrome architecture versus ICD-11.
- Any exact diagnostic checklist presented as current classification.

## Q37a. Tobacco cessation in schizophrenia

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Smoking is clinically important because tobacco smoke alters CYP1A2 metabolism.
- Clozapine and olanzapine exposure may rise after smoking cessation and fall again if smoking restarts.
- Nicotine replacement is not equivalent to tobacco smoke for this metabolic interaction.
- Offer standard smoking-cessation pharmacotherapy plus behavioural support.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons in smoke, not nicotine itself, drive CYP1A2 induction.
- Clozapine interaction is the highest-yield safety point; olanzapine also affected.
- Smoking-status changes require active medication review.

### Requires current verification / update
- Exact magnitude/timing of clozapine concentration change.
- Whether any fixed percentage dose reduction should be taught.
- Current varenicline/bupropion/NRT safety and efficacy in schizophrenia.

## Q38. Alcohol withdrawal

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Recognise tremor, autonomic hyperactivity, anxiety, insomnia, nausea and perceptual disturbance.
- Identify severe complications: withdrawal seizures and delirium tremens.
- Assess severity, prior complicated withdrawal, medical/psychiatric comorbidity and need for inpatient care.
- Benzodiazepines are the core treatment.
- Monitor hydration/electrolytes, mental state and vitals; give thiamine in at-risk patients.
- Use greater caution in liver disease.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Benzodiazepine treatment principle.
- Withdrawal seizures/DT as emergency complications.
- Thiamine and medical monitoring.
- Lower threshold for inpatient care in high-risk patients.

### Requires current verification / update
- Fixed-dose versus symptom-triggered regimens.
- CIWA-Ar role.
- Exact benzodiazepine schedules.
- Liver-disease benzodiazepine choice.
- Thiamine route/dose.

## Q38a. Wernicke encephalopathy

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Suspect in alcohol misuse/malnutrition with confusion, ocular signs, gait/ataxia or compatible neurological change.
- Treat urgently with parenteral thiamine rather than waiting for diagnostic certainty.
- Give thiamine before/with carbohydrate where possible.
- Recognise risk of progression to Korsakoff syndrome.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Wernicke encephalopathy is an emergency.
- Complete triad is not necessary for suspicion.
- Parenteral thiamine is required in suspected cases.

### Requires current verification / update
- Exact parenteral thiamine dose, route and duration.
- Carbohydrate/glucose sequencing.
- Magnesium correction.

## Q39. Alcohol-use disorder after withdrawal

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Withdrawal management is only the beginning; relapse prevention follows.
- Combine psychosocial treatment with medication where indicated.
- Oxford-era core relapse-prevention medicines: acamprosate, naltrexone and disulfiram; nalmefene as a reduction-oriented option.
- Match treatment to abstinence/reduction goals, contraindications and adherence.
- Include mutual-help/family/social support and physical-health follow-up.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Psychosocial relapse-prevention treatment.
- Acamprosate/naltrexone/disulfiram as classic pharmacological options.
- Goal-setting and adherence/support.

### Requires current verification / update
- Current first-line hierarchy.
- Nalmefene status/role.
- Main contraindication/selection principles.
- Treatment duration anchors.

## Q40. Opioid use disorder

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Recognise overdose: depressed consciousness, respiratory depression and often miosis.
- Treat overdose with naloxone plus airway/ventilatory support.
- Recognise opioid withdrawal clinically.
- Long-term management includes methadone or buprenorphine maintenance/substitution treatment plus psychosocial care.
- Detoxification is one pathway but should be weighed against relapse/overdose risk.
- Address injecting harms, blood-borne viruses and overdose prevention.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Methadone and buprenorphine as established agonist/substitution treatments.
- Buprenorphine can precipitate withdrawal if started too early.
- Naloxone for overdose.
- Harm-reduction and infectious-disease care.

### Requires current verification / update
- Maintenance versus detoxification current hierarchy.
- Methadone induction safety and QT considerations.
- Buprenorphine induction timing/withdrawal requirement.
- Take-home naloxone.
- Current Greek service/regulatory framework.

## Q41. Cannabis-related disorders

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Recognise intoxication, anxiety/panic, psychotic symptoms and withdrawal.
- Distinguish cannabis-induced psychosis from a primary psychotic disorder through temporal relationship and longitudinal course.
- Cannabis may worsen relapse/course in established psychosis.
- Treat cannabis use disorder mainly with psychosocial approaches.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Intoxication/anxiety/psychosis/withdrawal syndromes.
- Cannabis-induced versus primary psychosis differential.
- CBT/motivational-style treatment principles.

### Requires current verification / update
- ICD-11 cannabis architecture.
- Current psychosis-risk framing.
- Synthetic cannabinoid relevance.

## Q42. Stimulant-related disorders

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Cocaine and amphetamine-type stimulants can produce agitation, autonomic activation, paranoia/psychosis, seizures, hyperthermia, MI and stroke.
- Acute management emphasises benzodiazepines and supportive/medical treatment of complications.
- Withdrawal/crash may include fatigue, sleep disturbance, dysphoria/depression, craving and suicide risk.
- Longer-term treatment is primarily psychosocial.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Sympathomimetic toxidrome and severe medical red flags.
- Stimulant-induced psychosis.
- Benzodiazepines as core acute behavioural/toxicity treatment.
- Crash/withdrawal with mood and suicide-risk assessment.

### Requires current verification / update
- Old blanket beta-blocker contraindication in cocaine-associated chest pain.
- Current longer-term psychosocial hierarchy.
- Any exact acute-drug algorithms.

## Q43. Benzodiazepine dependence and withdrawal

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Long-term benzodiazepine use can produce tolerance and physical dependence.
- Withdrawal can cause rebound anxiety/insomnia, autonomic symptoms and severe complications including seizures/delirium.
- Avoid abrupt cessation.
- Gradual tapering is the central management principle.
- Oxford describes diazepam substitution and fixed reduction schedules as practical methods.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Dependence and severe-withdrawal risks.
- Gradual taper rather than abrupt stop.
- Treat underlying anxiety/insomnia and provide psychosocial support.

### Requires current verification / update
- Whether diazepam substitution is mandatory or optional.
- Current taper-rate principles.
- Whether fixed Ashton-style or one-eighth schedules should be retained.
- Inpatient/high-risk indications.

## Q44. Gambling disorder

### Base-source answer skeleton
- Identify impaired control, preoccupation, chasing losses and continuation despite mounting harm.
- Assess financial, occupational, relational/legal and suicide-related consequences.
- Consider comorbidity and distinguish persistent gambling disorder from gambling occurring only during mania.
- Psychological treatment is central; mutual-help may support recovery.

### Stable Oxford material to preserve
- Chasing losses and cognitive distortions such as gambler's fallacy.
- Financial/interpersonal harms and suicide-risk relevance.
- Mania differential.
- Psychological/mutual-help treatment principle.

### Requires current verification / update
- ICD-11 placement under addictive behaviours.
- Current CBT/motivational treatment recommendations.
- Whether pharmacotherapy now has a defined role.
