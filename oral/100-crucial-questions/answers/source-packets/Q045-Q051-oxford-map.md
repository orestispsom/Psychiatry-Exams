## 2. Reusable chapter map

### 2.1 Clinical phenomenology & core syndromes

#### Delirium (Ch. 14, pp. 351–356 & Box 14.2)
- **Core Phenomenology:** Acute or subacute onset (developing over hours to days) characterized by a fluctuating diurnal course (symptoms worsen in the evening/night — "sundowning"). Central features:
  1. *Impaired Attention & Awareness:* Reduced ability to direct, focus, sustain, and shift attention, accompanied by reduced orientation to the environment.
  2. *Global Cognitive Disturbance:* Memory deficits, disorientation in time, place, and person, language impairment, visuospatial dysfunction.
  3. *Perceptual Disturbances:* Misinterpretations, illusions, and vivid, frightening visual hallucinations (often featuring animals, microptic figures, or intruders).
  4. *Psychomotor & Sleep-Wake Disturbance:* Disrupted sleep-wake cycle (daytime somnolence, nocturnal agitation) and rapid alterations in psychomotor activity.
- **Motor Subtypes:**
  - *Hyperactive:* Restlessness, psychomotor agitation, hypervigilance, rapid loud speech, emotional lability, active hallucinations/delusions.
  - *Hypoactive:* Lethargy, somnolence, reduced motor activity, slowed speech, apathy (frequently undetected or misdiagnosed as depression/dementia).
  - *Mixed:* Fluctuates rapidly between hyperactive and hypoactive states.

#### Major Neurocognitive Disorder / Dementia Assessment (Ch. 14, pp. 356–362)
- **Core Definition:** Acquired, persistent decline in one or more cognitive domains from a previously higher baseline level, of sufficient severity to interfere significantly with independence in daily activities (ADLs/IADLs).
- **The 6 Cognitive Domains (DSM-5):** Complex attention, executive function, learning and memory, language, perceptual-motor, social cognition.
- **ADLs vs IADLs:**
  - *Basic ADLs:* Personal hygiene, dressing, eating, toileting, ambulation.
  - *Instrumental ADLs (IADLs):* Managing finances, shopping, meal preparation, medication management, driving, telephone use. *Key diagnostic distinction:* Impairment in IADLs distinguishes Major Neurocognitive Disorder (Dementia) from Mild Neurocognitive Disorder (Mild Cognitive Impairment [MCI]), where IADLs remain independent despite cognitive decline.

#### Alzheimer's Disease (AD) (Ch. 14, pp. 362–366)
- **Clinical Progression:** Insidious onset with early prominent impairment of short-term episodic memory (misplacing objects, repeating questions, difficulty retaining new events), while remote long-term memory remains intact initially.
- **Cortical Deficit Progression:** As the disease advances, cortical cognitive deficits emerge:
  - *Aphasia:* Word-finding difficulty (anomia), impaired comprehension, semantic paraphasias.
  - *Executive Dysfunction:* Impaired planning, poor judgment, inability to perform multi-step tasks.
  - *Visuospatial Dysfunction:* Spatial disorientation, getting lost in familiar surroundings, constructional apraxia.
  - *Apraxia:* Ideomotor and ideational apraxia (inability to carry out purposeful motor tasks despite intact motor function, e.g. dressing apraxia).
  - *Agnosia:* Failure to recognize objects or familiar faces (prosopagnosia).
- **End-Stage AD:** Severe rigidity, mutism, incontinence, complete loss of mobility, bedbound state.

#### Dementia with Lewy Bodies (DLB) vs Parkinson's Disease Dementia (PDD) (Ch. 14, pp. 368–369)
- **DLB Core Features:**
  1. *Fluctuating Cognition:* Marked, unpredictable, spontaneous variations in attention, alertness, and cognitive performance (hour-to-hour or day-to-day fluctuations, daytime staring spells).
  2. *Recurrent Visual Hallucinations:* Detailed, vivid, well-formed, non-bizarre visual hallucinations (people, children, animals), occurring early in the disease.
  3. *Spontaneous Parkinsonism:* Extrapyramidal motor signs (bradykinesia, resting tremor, rigidity, gait instability) present concurrently or early.
- **Supportive Features:** REM Sleep Behaviour Disorder (RBD — acting out dreams, flailing, punching during sleep; often precedes cognitive decline by years), Autonomic dysfunction (orthostatic hypotension, constipation, urinary incontinence, syncope), repeated falls, hypersomnia, transient loss of consciousness, severe neuroleptic sensitivity.
- **Severe Neuroleptic Sensitivity:** Life-threatening adverse reaction to D2-receptor antagonist antipsychotics (e.g. haloperidol), precipitating sudden acute severe parkinsonism, catatonia, hyperthermia, rapid cognitive collapse, neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS)-like states, and increased mortality.
- **The "One-Year Rule":**
  - *DLB:* Cognitive impairment/dementia develops **before** or **concurrently with** parkinsonism (or within **1 year** of the onset of motor parkinsonian symptoms).
  - *PDD:* Cognitive impairment/dementia develops in the context of established, long-standing Parkinson's disease (dementia onset occurs **more than 1 year** after motor parkinsonian symptoms are established).

#### Visual Hallucinations & Impulse Control Disorders in Parkinson's Disease (Ch. 14, pp. 373–375 & Ch. 18, pp. 519–520)
- **Visual Hallucinations in PD:** Common complication of long-standing PD and dopaminergic therapy. Ranges from illusions / passage hallucinations to vivid, formed visual hallucinations. Often heralded by loss of insight or acute infection/delirium.
- **Dopamine Agonist-Induced Impulse-Control Disorders (ICDs):** Failure to resist impulses to engage in repetitive, reward-seeking, harmful behaviours. Strongly associated with D2/D3 **dopamine agonists** (pramipexole, ropinirole, rotigotine):
  1. *Pathological Gambling:* Compulsive betting, loss chasing, financial collapse.
  2. *Hypersexuality:* Excessive, uncharacteristic sexual drive, inappropriate approaches, pornography overuse.
  3. *Compulsive Shopping / Spending:* Uncontrolled purchasing of unneeded items.
  4. *Binge Eating:* Consuming large amounts of food rapidly without hunger.
  5. *Punding & Dopamine Dysregulation:* Purposeless, repetitive mechanical tasks (sorting, disassembling equipment, endless filing); compulsive overuse of levodopa/dopamine agonists beyond motor needs.

#### Frontotemporal Dementia (FTD) (Ch. 14, pp. 369–371 & Box 14.18)
- **Behavioral Variant FTD (bvFTD) — Core Presentation:**
  - *Early Behavioral Disinhibition:* Inappropriate social conduct, loss of decorum, impulsive/reckless financial or personal actions.
  - *Early Apathy / Inertia:* Loss of drive, passive withdrawal from work and hobbies.
  - *Loss of Empathy / Sympathy:* Emotional blunting, indifference to family needs, lack of warmth.
  - *Perseverative / Compulsive / Stereotyped Behavior:* Repetitive mannerisms, ritualistic behaviors, rigid daily routines, compulsive counting/sorting.
  - *Hyperorality & Dietary Changes:* Carbohydrate/sweet cravings, binge eating, excessive alcohol/tobacco consumption, putting inedible objects in mouth.
  - *Executive Dysfunction:* Severe impairment in planning, organization, abstract reasoning, set-shifting.
- **Language Variants (Primary Progressive Aphasia [PPA]):** Non-fluent/agrammatic PPA (hesitant, effortful speech, speech apraxia) and Semantic variant PPA (loss of word meaning, impaired confrontation naming, fluent empty speech).
- **Early Memory Preservation:** Memory and visuospatial functions are characteristically **spared in early bvFTD** (unlike AD).

#### Vascular Cognitive Impairment (VCI) / Vascular Dementia (VaD) (Ch. 14, pp. 366–368)
- **Clinical Course:** Cognitive decline temporally linked to cerebrovascular events. Classic presentation is a **stepwise course** (abrupt drop in cognitive function following stroke, followed by periods of relative stability), though subcortical ischaemic vascular dementia presents with a **gradual progressive course**.
- **Cognitive & Physical Profile:** Prominent **executive dysfunction** (impaired planning, set-shifting, mental speed), slowed processing speed, emotional lability, apathy, and mood changes. Short-term memory is less severely impaired early than in AD. Focal neurological signs: gait disturbance (small-stepped / apraxic gait), focal weakness, spasticity, pseudobulbar palsy (dysarthria, dysphagia, pathological laughing/crying), urinary incontinence.

#### Behavioural and Psychological Symptoms of Dementia (BPSD) (Ch. 19, pp. 556–560 & Box 19.4)
- **BPSD Spectrum (Box 19.4):**
  - *Psychotic:* Delusions (persecutory, theft, capgras/imposter, "house is not my home") and visual/auditory hallucinations.
  - *Affective:* Depression, anxiety, dysphoria, apathy (most common, highly distressing to carers).
  - *Hyperactivity / Agitation:* Pacing, restlessness, shouting, verbal aggression, physical aggression, disinhibition.
  - *Behavioral:* Wandering, sleep-wake reversal, shadowing carers, repetitive vocalizations, hoarding.

### 2.2 Diagnostic & classification distinctions

#### Delirium vs Dementia vs Depression (Ch. 14, pp. 351, 360)
- **Delirium:** Acute onset, fluctuating course, **impaired attention/consciousness**, prominent visual hallucinations, reversible.
- **Dementia:** Insidious onset, slow progressive course, **clear consciousness**, attention preserved early, irreversible/progressive.
- **Depression (Pseudodementia):** Subacute onset, compliant with testing ("don't know" responses vs near-miss answers in dementia), distress over memory failures, history of mood disorder, absence of fluctuating clouding of consciousness.

#### Dementia Classification Systems (Ch. 14, pp. 346, 356 & Table 14.1)
- **ICD-10:** Classifies under *Organic, including symptomatic, mental disorders* (F00–F09), including F00 (Dementia in Alzheimer's disease), F01 (Vascular dementia), F02 (Dementia in other diseases classified elsewhere: F02.0 Pick's/FTD, F02.3 Parkinson's, F02.8 Lewy body). Requires memory impairment + impairment of other cognitive abilities causing functional decline, duration ≥6 months.
- **DSM-5:** Replaced *Dementia* with **Major Neurocognitive Disorder** (requires significant decline in ≥1 cognitive domain interfering with independence in ADLs) and **Mild Neurocognitive Disorder** (modest decline, ADLs independent). Subclassified by etiology (Due to Alzheimer's disease, Frontotemporal lobar degeneration, Lewy body disease, Vascular disease, etc.).

### 2.4 Aetiology, neurobiology & biomarkers

#### Delirium Aetiology & Triggers (Ch. 14, pp. 351–353 & Box 14.1)
- **Predisposing Factors:** Age >65, pre-existing dementia/brain pathology, severe physical illness, sensory impairment (blindness/deafness), alcohol misuse, frailty.
- **Precipitating Factors (Box 14.1 / PINCH ME):**
  - *Infection:* UTI, chest infection, sepsis.
  - *Metabolic / Endocrine:* Hyponatremia, hypercalcemia, hypoglycemia, liver/renal failure, hypoxia.
  - *Drugs / Polypharmacy:* Anticholinergic drugs, sedatives/benzodiazepines, opioids, corticosteroids, anticonvulsants, drug withdrawal (alcohol, sedatives).
  - *Physical:* Pain, constipation/fecal impaction, urinary retention, stroke, head trauma, environmental transfer/ICU stay.

#### Neuropathology & Genetics of Dementia Subtypes (Ch. 14, pp. 362–375)
- **Alzheimer's Disease:**
  - *Pathology:* Extracellular **amyloid-beta plaques** (A-beta 42) and intracellular **neurofibrillary tangles** (hyperphosphorylated tau protein). Loss of cholinergic neurons in the basal forebrain (nucleus basalis of Meynert).
  - *Genetics:* Early-onset familial AD (<65 yr): autosomal dominant mutations in *APP* (chr 21), *PSEN1* (chr 14), *PSEN2* (chr 1). Late-onset AD risk gene: **ApoE epsilon-4** (chromosome 19; ApoE4 increases risk, ApoE2 is protective). Down's syndrome (Trisomy 21) universally develops AD pathology by age 40 due to APP gene triplication.
- **Dementia with Lewy Bodies / PDD:**
  - *Pathology:* Widespread neuronal cytoplasmic inclusions of **alpha-synuclein** (Lewy bodies) and Lewy neurites in neocortex, limbic system, and substantia nigra.
- **Frontotemporal Dementia:**
  - *Pathology:* Frontotemporal lobar degeneration (FTLD) with Tau protein inclusions (Pick bodies / Pick's disease), TDP-44 inclusions, or FUS inclusions.
- **Dopamine Agonist Impulse Control Disorders:**
  - *Neurobiology:* Dopamine agonists (pramipexole, ropinirole) stimulate **D2/D3 receptors** in the mesolimbic reward system (nucleus accumbens), hypersensitizing reward circuitry and impairing impulse inhibition.

### 2.5 Course & prognosis

- **Delirium:** Acute, reversible condition; however, episodes of delirium increase 1-year mortality, accelerate underlying cognitive decline, and prolong hospital stays.
- **Alzheimer's Disease:** Gradual, relentless progression. Mean survival from diagnosis is **7 to 10 years**.
- **Dementia with Lewy Bodies:** Progressive course, often deteriorating faster than AD due to falls, autonomic failure, and antipsychotic sensitivity. Mean survival **5 to 8 years**.
- **Frontotemporal Dementia:** Early onset (45–65 years). Rapidly progressive; mean survival from symptom onset is **6 to 8 years**.
- **Vascular Dementia:** Variable; stepwise decline with stroke events. Survival depends on cardiovascular risk control.

### 2.6 Pharmacological & treatment principles

#### Delirium Management (Ch. 14, pp. 353–356)
1. **Identify & Treat Underlying Cause:** Primary medical urgency (antibiotics, pain relief, hydration, catheterization, bowel care, drug review/deprescribing).
2. **Environmental & Non-Pharmacological:** Reassurance, quiet well-lit room, clear clock/calendar, orientation aids, family presence, sensory aids (glasses/hearing aids), early mobilization. Avoid physical restraints.
3. **Pharmacotherapy Rules:** Antipsychotics are reserved **ONLY** for severe distress, intractable hallucinations/delusions, or severe agitation threatening safety.
   - Use lowest effective dose for shortest possible duration (e.g. **Haloperidol 0.5–1 mg** oral/IM, or olanzapine/quetiapine).
   - **DLB / Parkinson's Contraindication:** Haloperidol and standard D2 antagonists are CONTRAINDICATED in suspected DLB/PDD due to severe neuroleptic sensitivity; use low-dose quetiapine if sedation is urgently required.
   - Benzodiazepines are second-line, used ONLY for alcohol/sedative withdrawal delirium or when antipsychotics are contraindicated.

#### Assessment & Diagnostic Workup of Dementia (Ch. 14, pp. 358–362)
- History from patient and informant (onset, course, IADLs).
- Exclude delirium and depression.
- Cognitive screening: MMSE, MoCA, ACE-III.
- Laboratory panel: FBC, ESR/CRP, U&Es, LFTs, TFTs (hypothyroidism), Vitamin B12 and folate, calcium, blood glucose.
- **Structural Neuroimaging (CT or MRI head):** Recommended for all newly diagnosed patients to rule out surgical/reversible causes (subdural hematoma, normal pressure hydrocephalus [NPH], tumor) and confirm structural subtype pattern.

#### Pharmacotherapy of Alzheimer's Disease (Ch. 19, pp. 558–560 & Ch. 25, pp. 760–765)
- **Cholinesterase Inhibitors (ChEIs):** **Donepezil, Rivastigmine, Galantamine**. First-line for mild-to-moderate AD. Mechanism: enhance synaptic acetylcholine. Adverse effects: GI distress (nausea, diarrhea), bradycardia, syncope, insomnia, vivid dreams.
- **Memantine:** Uncompetitive NMDA receptor antagonist. First-line for moderate-to-severe AD (or added to ChEIs in moderate-to-severe AD, or if ChEIs contraindicated). Mechanism: protects against pathological glutamate excitotoxicity. Adverse effects: dizziness, headache, constipation, hypertension.

#### DLB, PDD & Visual Hallucination Management (Ch. 14, pp. 373–375 & Ch. 19, pp. 556–560)
- **DLB / PDD Cognitive & Behavioral Treatment:** **Cholinesterase inhibitors (Donepezil, Rivastigmine)** are highly effective for cognitive deficits, apathy, visual hallucinations, and daytime somnolence.
- **Stepwise Reduction of Antiparkinsonian Drugs for Visual Hallucinations:**
  1. Taper and stop **anticholinergics** (trihexyphenidyl);
  2. Taper and stop **selegiline / rasagiline / amantadine**;
  3. Taper and stop **dopamine agonists** (pramipexole, ropinirole);
  4. Taper **COMT inhibitors** (entacapone);
  5. Reduce **Levodopa** to minimum dose required for basic motor function.
- **Antipsychotic Selection in PD / DLB:**
  - Avoid standard D2 antagonists (haloperidol).
  - Low-dose **Quetiapine** (12.5–50 mg) widely used in practice.
  - Low-dose **Clozapine** (6.25–50 mg) proven effective in RCTs without worsening motor function (requires blood monitoring).

#### Dopamine Agonist Impulse Control Disorder Management (Ch. 14, p. 374)
- Screen actively for gambling, hypersexuality, shopping, binge eating.
- Mainstay: **Gradual tapering and discontinuation of the dopamine agonist** under neurological supervision.
- **DAWS Warning:** Rapid cessation causes Dopamine Agonist Withdrawal Syndrome (severe anxiety, panic, depression, dysphoria, craving, pain). Increase levodopa if motor function deteriorates.

#### FTD & Vascular Dementia Management (Ch. 14, pp. 368, 371)
- **FTD:** ChEIs and memantine are **ineffective and may worsen agitation/disinhibition**. Use behavioral strategies, environmental support, and cautious SSRIs (for compulsive symptoms).
- **Vascular Cognitive Impairment:** Secondary prevention (BP control, statins, antiplatelet therapy, AF anticoagulation). ChEIs/memantine beneficial if **Mixed Dementia** (AD + VaD) is present.

#### BPSD Management & Safety Warnings (Ch. 19, pp. 556–560 & Box 19.4)
- **Assess Triggers:** Pain, UTI, constipation, urinary retention, sensory deficits, environmental distress.
- **Non-Pharmacological First:** Person-centered care, Dementia Care Mapping, CST, aromatherapy, music therapy, carer education.
- **Antipsychotic Warnings & Rules:**
  - Reserved for severe, distressing symptoms or safety risk refractory to non-pharmacological care.
  - **Black-Box Warning:** **3-fold increased risk of stroke/TIA** and **1.5-fold increased risk of all-cause mortality** in elderly dementia patients.
  - Use lowest effective dose for shortest duration (target review at 6–12 weeks with planned deprescribing). **Risperidone** is the only licensed antipsychotic for short-term (up to 6 weeks) persistent aggression in moderate-to-severe AD.
  - SSRIs (sertraline, citalopram) preferred for depressive/anxiety symptoms and non-psychotic agitation.

### Q45 — Delirium
- **Spine:** (1) Acute onset and fluctuating diurnal course; (2) Inattention and reduced awareness as central feature; (3) Cognitive/perceptual disturbances (visual hallucinations); (4) Subtypes: hyperactive, hypoactive, mixed; (5) Differential: dementia, depression, psychosis, catatonia; (6) Precipitants (PINCH ME) and predisposing factors (Box 14.1); (7) Physical exam and lab panel; (8) Treat underlying cause; (9) Non-pharmacological environmental care; (10) Low-dose haloperidol/antipsychotic ONLY for severe distress/agitation; DLB contraindication.
- **Keepers:** Box 14.1 precipitants; Box 14.2 features; hypoactive delirium under-recognition; PINCH ME mnemonic; haloperidol DLB contraindication.
- **Verify currently:** 4AT / CAM delirium screening tools; modern antipsychotic prescribing restrictions in delirium.
- **Boundaries:** Dementia assessment belongs in Q46; BPSD in Q51.

### Q46 — Suspected Major Neurocognitive Disorder / Dementia Assessment
- **Spine:** (1) History and informant collateral (onset, course, IADL decline); (2) Assessment across 6 cognitive domains; (3) Exclusion of delirium and depression (pseudodementia); (4) Physical & neurological exam; (5) Standardized cognitive scales (MMSE, MoCA, ACE-III); (6) Blood panel (TFTs, B12, folate, calcium); (7) Structural CT/MRI head; (8) Reversible causes (hypothyroidism, B12, NPH, subdural); (9) Medico-legal: capacity, driving regulations, safeguarding, carer support.
- **Keepers:** IADL impairment defining Major vs Mild NCD; MoCA sensitivity for subcortical/executive deficits; NPH triad; CT/MRI mandate.
- **Verify currently:** DSM-5-TR / ICD-11 diagnostic criteria; plasma amyloid/tau biomarkers (p-tau181, A-beta 42/40 ratio).
- **Boundaries:** AD specific features belong in Q47; DLB in Q48; FTD in Q49; VaD in Q50.

### Q47 — Alzheimer Disease (AD)
- **Spine:** (1) Insidious onset with early short-term episodic memory loss; (2) Cortical cognitive decline (aphasia, apraxia, agnosia, executive dysfunction); (3) Pathology: amyloid-beta plaques and neurofibrillary tau tangles; (4) Genetics: APP, PSEN1, PSEN2 early-onset mutations; ApoE4 late-onset risk allele; Down's syndrome link; (5) Differential: DLB, FTD, VaD, depression; (6) Pharmacotherapy: ChEIs (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine) for mild-moderate AD; Memantine for moderate-severe AD; (7) Psychosocial and carer support.
- **Keepers:** Cholinergic nucleus basalis loss; ApoE4 risk vs ApoE2 protection; Down's syndrome chromosome 21 APP link; donepezil vs memantine indications.
- **Verify currently:** Monoclonal anti-amyloid antibodies (lecanemab, donanemab) licensing status; CSF and plasma AD biomarkers.
- **Boundaries:** Assessment belongs in Q46; BPSD in Q51.

### Q48 — Dementia with Lewy Bodies (DLB) vs Parkinson Disease Dementia (PDD)
- **Spine:** (1) Core triad: cognitive fluctuations, recurrent vivid visual hallucinations, spontaneous parkinsonism; (2) Supportive features: REM sleep behavior disorder (RBD), autonomic dysfunction, severe neuroleptic sensitivity; (3) **The "One-Year Rule":** DLB (dementia before/with parkinsonism or within 1 year) vs PDD (dementia >1 year after established PD); (4) Alpha-synuclein pathology; (5) Pharmacotherapy: ChEIs (donepezil/rivastigmine) highly effective; avoid typical antipsychotics (haloperidol).
- **Keepers:** 1-year rule; severe neuroleptic sensitivity warning; alpha-synuclein pathology; ChEI efficacy in DLB/PDD.
- **Verify currently:** 4th consensus criteria for DLB (McKeith 2017); DaTscan / MIBG cardiac scintigraphy imaging.
- **Boundaries:** Visual hallucinations in PD belong in Q48a; Impulse control disorders in Q48b.

### Q48a — Visual Hallucinations in Parkinson Disease
- **Spine:** (1) Screen for acute delirium, infection (UTI), visual loss, or PDD/DLB progression; (2) Driven by antiparkinsonian dopaminergic therapy; (3) Stepwise drug reduction protocol: anticholinergics -> MAO-B/amantadine -> dopamine agonists -> COMT inhibitors -> reduce levodopa; (4) Antipsychotic principles: avoid D2 antagonists; low-dose quetiapine widely used; low-dose clozapine proven in RCTs (requires blood monitoring).
- **Keepers:** Stepwise antiparkinsonian reduction order; clozapine RCT evidence; D2 antagonist contraindication.
- **Verify currently:** Pimavanserin (5-HT2A inverse agonist) FDA/EMA/UK licensing status.
- **Boundaries:** Core DLB/PDD diagnosis belongs in Q48; Impulse control disorders in Q48b.

### Q48b — Dopamine Agonist Impulse-Control Disorders (ICDs)
- **Spine:** (1) Core domains: pathological gambling, hypersexuality, compulsive shopping, binge eating, punding/DDS; (2) Driven by D2/D3 dopamine agonists (pramipexole, ropinirole, rotigotine); (3) Active screening and collateral history required; (4) Mainstay management: gradual dopamine agonist tapering; (5) **DAWS Warning:** Rapid withdrawal causes severe anxiety, dysphoria, and craving; increase levodopa if motor function deteriorates.
- **Keepers:** 4 main ICD domains + punding; dopamine agonist D2/D3 association; DAWS withdrawal warning.
- **Verify currently:** Current Movement Disorder Society (MDS) guidelines on ICD screening and management.
- **Boundaries:** Visual hallucinations belong in Q48a; Gambling disorder in Q44.

### Q49 — Frontotemporal Dementia (FTD)
- **Spine:** (1) Early onset (45–65 years); (2) Behavioral variant (bvFTD): early disinhibition, apathy, loss of empathy, stereotypies/compulsions, hyperorality/dietary changes, executive dysfunction; (3) Language variants (PPA); (4) Early relative memory and visuospatial preservation; (5) Frontotemporal atrophy on CT/MRI/PET; (6) Treatment: ChEIs and memantine are INEFFECTIVE/HARMFUL; non-pharmacological care, SSRIs for compulsions.
- **Keepers:** Box 14.18 bvFTD criteria; early memory sparing; hyperorality/sweet cravings; ChEI contraindication in FTD.
- **Verify currently:** International consensus criteria for bvFTD (Rascovsky 2011); genetic testing (C9orf72, MAPT, GRN).
- **Boundaries:** Alzheimer's disease belongs in Q47.

### Q50 — Vascular Cognitive Impairment (VCI) / Vascular Dementia (VaD)
- **Spine:** (1) Temporal link to cerebrovascular events; (2) Stepwise course (cortical VaD) vs gradual subcortical progression; (3) Cognitive profile: prominent executive dysfunction, slowed processing speed, emotional lability; (4) Physical features: gait apraxia, focal weakness, spasticity, pseudobulbar palsy; (5) Imaging: cortical/subcortical infarcts, white matter hyperintensities (leukoaraiosis); (6) Mixed AD/VaD pathology frequency; (7) Management: secondary vascular prevention (BP, statins, antiplatelets, AF control); ChEIs in mixed dementia.
- **Keepers:** Stepwise course concept; executive dysfunction predominance; pseudobulbar palsy; secondary prevention focus.
- **Verify currently:** VICCCS / NINDS-AIREN vascular dementia criteria updates.
- **Boundaries:** Alzheimer's disease belongs in Q47; BPSD in Q51.

### Q51 — Behavioural and Psychological Symptoms of Dementia (BPSD)
- **Spine:** (1) BPSD spectrum (Box 19.4): agitation, aggression, psychosis, depression, apathy, wandering; (2) Systematic trigger assessment: pain, UTI, constipation, retention, medication, environmental distress; (3) Non-pharmacological interventions FIRST-LINE (person-centered care, DCM, CST, music therapy); (4) Pharmacotherapy rules & risks: reserved for severe distress/safety risk; (5) **Black-Box Warning:** 3x stroke risk and 1.5x mortality risk with antipsychotics; (6) Risperidone short-term (up to 6 weeks) licensing in AD; 6–12 week trial and planned deprescribing.
- **Keepers:** Box 19.4 spectrum; trigger search mandate; 3x stroke & 1.5x mortality risks; risperidone short-term AD licensing.
- **Verify currently:** Current NICE NG97 guidelines on BPSD management and deprescribing algorithms.
- **Boundaries:** Delirium belongs in Q45.

## 4. 12 Most Important Claims Requiring Current Verification

1. **Anti-Amyloid Monoclonal Antibodies in Alzheimer's Disease:** Oxford 7th ed (2017) lists no disease-modifying therapies for AD. Recent FDA/EMA approvals of monoclonal antibodies (lecanemab, donanemab) targeting amyloid-beta require explicit verification.
2. **Fluid and Plasma Biomarkers in Dementia Diagnosis:** Oxford covers structural CT/MRI and clinical criteria. Modern diagnostic guidelines incorporate CSF and plasma biomarkers (p-tau181, p-tau217, A-beta 42/40 ratio, neurofilament light chain [NfL]).
3. **Black-Box Antipsychotic Risks in BPSD:** Oxford emphasizes the 3-fold stroke risk and 1.5-fold mortality risk of antipsychotics in dementia. Current NICE NG97 guidelines and prescribing restrictions require verification.
4. **Pimavanserin Licensing for Parkinson's Disease Psychosis:** Oxford notes quetiapine and clozapine for PD visual hallucinations. FDA approval of pimavanserin (selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist) and its UK/EU availability require verification.
5. **Dopamine Agonist Withdrawal Syndrome (DAWS):** Oxford describes dopamine agonist-induced impulse control disorders. DAWS features, risk factors, and slow tapering protocols require current verification.
6. **DLB Consensus Diagnostic Criteria (McKeith 2017):** Oxford 7th ed covers standard DLB features. The updated 4th consensus criteria (incorporating REM sleep behavior disorder as a core feature and DaTscan / MIBG scintigraphy as indicative biomarkers) require verification.
7. **Cholinesterase Inhibitor Contraindication in FTD:** Oxford notes ChEIs and memantine are not effective in FTD and may worsen agitation. Current consensus recommendations against ChEI use in bvFTD require confirmation.
8. **Delirium Screening Instruments (4AT / CAM):** Oxford outlines clinical features of delirium (Box 14.2). Standard validated screening tools (4AT, Confusion Assessment Method [CAM]) require verification.
9. **DSM-5-TR & ICD-11 Neurocognitive Terminology:** Oxford contrasts ICD-10 *Organic Mental Disorders* with DSM-5 *Major/Mild Neurocognitive Disorders*. ICD-11 structural changes (*Neurocognitive disorders*) require verification.
10. **Risperidone Licensing Limits in BPSD:** Oxford notes risperidone is used for BPSD. Modern UK/EU specific licensing restrictions (short-term treatment up to 6 weeks for severe persistent aggression in AD) require verification.
11. **Subcortical Vascular Dementia vs Alzheimer Progression:** Oxford describes stepwise vs progressive course. Current VICCCS (Vascular Impairment of Cognition Classification Consensus Study) criteria require verification.
12. **Delirium Antipsychotic Prescribing Restrictions:** Oxford recommends low-dose haloperidol for severe delirium. Recent guidelines (NICE CG103) restricting antipsychotic use to extremely brief periods (1–7 days) require verification.
