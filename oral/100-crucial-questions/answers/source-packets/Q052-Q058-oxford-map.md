## 2. Reusable chapter map

### 2.1 Clinical phenomenology & core syndromes

#### Severe Self-Neglect, Squalor & Diogenes Syndrome (Ch. 8, p. 191; Ch. 19, pp. 546–552)
- **Core Phenomenology:** Extreme self-neglect, domestic squalor, social withdrawal, hoarding of rubbish and worthless garbage (syllogomania), indifference to filthy living conditions, and active refusal of external medical or social assistance.
- **Diogenes Syndrome Framing:** Named after Diogenes of Sinope (who rejected luxury for ascetic philosophy). Oxford notes the analogy is imperfect because Diogenes chose poverty as a conscious philosophy, whereas senile squalor syndrome usually reflects underlying neuropathology, severe psychiatric illness, or executive failure.
- **Distinction from Eccentric Lifestyle Choice:** Lifestyle choice involves autonomous, un-impaired reasoning without objective incapacity or severe cognitive/psychiatric collapse. Self-neglect requiring intervention is driven by executive dysfunction, severe depression, active psychosis, cognitive decline, or frontal lobe damage.
- **Physical & Environmental Risks:** Severe malnutrition, dehydration, hypothermia, untreated physical illness (leg ulcers, skin infections), medication neglect, severe fire hazards, pest and rodent infestation, structural decay, risk of collapse, and risks to dependants or neighbors (fire, gas leaks, pest spread).

#### Autoimmune Encephalitis & Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis (Ch. 11, p. 283; Ch. 14, pp. 387–388)
- **Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis Prototype:** Severe autoimmune encephalitis caused by antibodies directed against the NR1 subunit of the NMDA receptor, predominantly affecting young females.
- **Clinical Red Flag Progression:**
  1. *Prodrome:* Flu-like illness (fever, headache, fatigue, nausea).
  2. *Psychiatric Stage:* Rapid onset of severe psychiatric disturbance (bizarre persecutory delusions, visual/auditory hallucinations, acute agitation, mania, severe insomnia, emotional lability, catatonia).
  3. *Neurological Stage:* Progression over days/weeks to seizures (focal/generalized, status epilepticus), abnormal involuntary movements (orofacial dyskinesias, choreoathetosis, limb dystonia), cognitive collapse, mutism, and fluctuating consciousness/stupor.
  4. *Autonomic Instability:* Central hypoventilation (requiring mechanical ventilation), blood pressure lability, marked tachycardia, central hyperthermia.
- **Neoplastic Association:** Strong association with **ovarian teratoma** in approximately 50% of young female cases.

#### Epilepsy & Psychiatric Phenomena (Ch. 14, pp. 381–386)
- **Temporal Classification of Psychiatric Symptoms:**
  - *Ictal (During Seizure):* Aura (epigastric rising sensation, déjà vu, jamais vu, intense fear/anxiety, gustatory/olfactory hallucinations in Temporal Lobe Epilepsy [TLE]), ictal automatisms, absence stupor, ictal psychosis.
  - *Pre-Ictal:* Dysphoria, irritability, anxiety, or headache building up hours to days prior to a seizure.
  - *Post-Ictal:* Post-ictal confusion, amnesia, agitation, delirium, or **Post-Ictal Psychosis (PIP)** (characteristically occurs after a 24–48 hour "lucid interval" following a cluster of seizures, presenting with florid paranoia, mania, or hallucinations lasting days to 2 weeks).
  - *Interictal (Between Seizures):*
    - **Depression & Suicide:** Most common interictal disorder (20%–50% prevalence). Suicide risk is elevated **5- to 10-fold** compared to general population (even higher in TLE).
    - **Schizophrenia-like Psychosis of Epilepsy (SLPE):** Chronic interictal paranoid psychosis developing years after epilepsy onset (median 10–15 years), characterized by persecutory delusions and preserved affect, with fewer negative symptoms than primary schizophrenia.
    - **Gastaut-Geschwind Syndrome:** Historical/controversial interictal personality pattern in TLE (hypergraphia, hyperreligiosity, circumstantiality, deepened emotionality, hyposexuality).

#### Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) Psychiatry (Ch. 14, pp. 378–381)
- **Acute Index of Severity — Post-Traumatic Amnesia (PTA):** The duration of PTA (period post-injury before continuous memory for ongoing events returns) is the best retrospective and prospective index of TBI severity:
  - *<1 hour:* Mild; *1–24 hours:* Moderate; *1–7 days:* Severe; *>7 days:* Very severe.
- **Organic Personality Change Syndromes:**
  - *Orbitofrontal Syndrome:* Disinhibition, impulsivity, emotional lability, irritability, aggressive outbursts, tactlessness, inappropriate euphoria.
  - *Dorsolateral Prefrontal Syndrome:* Apathy, inertia, psychomotor slowing, lack of initiation, loss of drive (often misdiagnosed as depression).
- **Comorbidities:** Major depression (30%–50%), **3- to 4-fold increased suicide risk**, substance misuse, post-traumatic headache/dizziness (Post-Concussional Syndrome).

#### Liaison Psychiatry in the Medically Ill (Ch. 22, pp. 631–640, 658–668)
- **Consultation Task & Referral Clarification:** Primary task is to clarify the specific referral question from medical/surgical teams:
  1. Distinguishing physical disease symptoms from psychiatric/functional presentations;
  2. Diagnosing psychiatric comorbidity (delirium, depression, anxiety, substance withdrawal) in medical inpatients;
  3. Assessing decision-making capacity to consent to or refuse medical treatment;
  4. Evaluating suicide/self-harm risk following deliberate self-harm admission.
- **Biopsychosocial Formulation:** Integrates underlying physical pathology, medical treatments (steroids, chemotherapy, ICU stay), pre-existing psychiatric illness, personality traits, coping mechanisms, and social support.

#### Apathy vs Depression (Ch. 1, p. 5; Ch. 14, pp. 370, 378; Ch. 19, p. 556)
- **Apathy Definition:** Primary quantitative reduction in voluntary, goal-directed behaviour, cognitive activity, and emotional expression ("loss of motivation, drive, and initiation").
- **Depression Definition:** Primary mood disturbance featuring pervasive sadness, gloom, hopelessness, self-blame, worthlessness, and suicidal ideation.
- **Anhedonia Overlap:** Apathy involves passive lack of interest/engagement without emotional distress; depression features anhedonia accompanied by painful distress over loss of enjoyment.

#### Charles Bonnet Syndrome (CBS) (Ch. 19, pp. 555–556)
- **Core Phenomenology:** Complex, vivid, well-formed visual hallucinations occurring in individuals with severe visual impairment (eye disease/blindness), with **intact insight** and **absence of psychosis, delirium, or neurodegenerative dementia**.
- **Hallucination Features:** Exclusively visual (vividly detailed people, children, faces, animals, landscapes), appearing suddenly, lasting minutes to hours. Insight is preserved (patient realizes images are not real).

### 2.2 Diagnostic & classification distinctions

#### Capacity & Self-Neglect Legal Distinctions (Ch. 19, pp. 546–552)
- **Decision-Making Capacity:** Decision-specific and time-specific. Requires demonstrating that impairment in brain/mind function prevents the person from: (1) Understanding relevant information; (2) Retaining information; (3) Weighing information to make a decision; or (4) Communicating the decision.
- **Self-Neglect & Involuntary Admission:** *Critical exam/legal distinction:* Severe self-neglect or squalor alone does NOT automatically justify involuntary psychiatric admission or forced medical treatment. Involuntary intervention requires an underlying mental disorder meeting statutory legal criteria (Mental Health Act).

#### Postictal vs Interictal Psychosis in Epilepsy (Ch. 14, pp. 383–384)
- **Postictal Psychosis (PIP):** Occurs within 1 week of a seizure cluster, preceded by a brief **lucid interval of 24–48 hours**. Self-limiting (lasts days to 2 weeks).
- **Schizophrenia-like Psychosis of Epilepsy (SLPE):** Chronic interictal psychosis independent of recent seizures, developing years after epilepsy onset.

### 2.4 Aetiology, neurobiology & biomarkers

#### Autoimmune Encephalitis Neurobiology & Biomarkers (Ch. 11, p. 283; Ch. 14, pp. 387–388)
- Anti-NMDA receptor antibodies bind to extracellular NR1/NR2 heteromers, causing antibody-mediated receptor internalization and glutamatergic hypofunction.
- Diagnostic Workup: CSF pleocytosis, oligoclonal bands, specific serum/CSF autoantibody panel (anti-NMDAR, anti-LGI1, anti-CASPR2). EEG shows "extreme delta brush" pattern. MRI head shows T2/FLAIR limbic hyperintensities. Pelvic US/CT screening for ovarian teratoma.

#### Neurobiology of Epilepsy & TBI (Ch. 14, pp. 378–386)
- **Epilepsy Focus:** Temporal Lobe Epilepsy (medial temporal sclerosis, amygdalohippocampal sclerosis) carries highest rate of interictal depression, psychosis, and suicide.
- **TBI Neuroanatomy:** Frontal lobe contusions and diffuse axonal injury (DAI). Orbitofrontal damage -> disinhibition; Dorsolateral prefrontal damage -> apathy.

#### Neurobiology of Apathy & Charles Bonnet (Ch. 1, p. 5; Ch. 14; Ch. 19)
- **Apathy:** Disruption of frontal-subcortical circuits (anterior cingulate cortex, striatum, basal ganglia).
- **Charles Bonnet Syndrome:** Visual deafferentation (sensory deprivation) leading to cortical hyperexcitability and spontaneous firing of visual association cortex (Brodmann areas 18/19).

### 2.5 Course & prognosis

- **Autoimmune Encephalitis:** Early recognition and immunotherapy yield good recovery in ~75%–80% of cases; delayed treatment increases mortality and chronic cognitive deficits.
- **Epilepsy Suicide Risk:** Suicide risk is elevated **5- to 10-fold** in epilepsy patients (up to 25-fold in TLE).
- **TBI Recovery:** Post-concussional syndrome symptoms resolve within weeks to months in 70%–85% of mild TBI cases; severe TBI with PTA >7 days results in permanent cognitive/executive deficits.
- **Charles Bonnet Syndrome:** Chronic course; hallucinations fluctuate but may persist for years until vision stabilizes or complete blindness ensues.

### 2.6 Pharmacological & treatment principles

#### Severe Self-Neglect Management (Ch. 19, pp. 546–552)
- Multi-agency approach (social services, occupational therapy, environmental health, community nursing).
- Build therapeutic rapport slowly; non-confrontational engagement; least-restrictive interventions.
- Formal capacity assessment. Involuntary admission reserved ONLY for underlying severe mental disorder under statutory mental health law.

#### Autoimmune Encephalitis Management (Ch. 14, pp. 387–388)
- Urgent ICU / neurology admission. First-line immunotherapy: high-dose IV methylprednisolone, IVIG (intravenous immunoglobulin), or plasmapheresis.
- Surgical resection of ovarian teratoma if identified. Low-dose atypical antipsychotics/benzos for severe agitation (caution with NMS-like states).

#### Antiseizure Drugs & Psychotropics (Ch. 14, pp. 381–386)
- **Antiseizure Drug (ASM) Adverse Effects:** Levetiracetam (aggression, depression, psychosis), Topiramate (cognitive slowing, depression), Vigabatrin (psychosis), Phenobarbital (depression). Mood-stabilizing ASMs: Valproate, Lamotrigine, Carbamazepine.
- **Psychotropic Seizure Threshold Effects:**
  - *High Risk:* **Clozapine**, **Bupropion**, Chlorpromazine, Maprotiline.
  - *Low Risk:* SSRIs, SNRIs, Aripiprazole, Risperidone, Quetiapine.
- **Enzyme Induction Interactions:** Carbamazepine, Phenytoin, and Phenobarbital induce CYP3A4/2D6, significantly lowering plasma levels of antipsychotics, antidepressants, and oral contraceptives. Valproate inhibits lamotrigine metabolism (halve lamotrigine dose).

#### TBI Prescribing Principles (Ch. 14, pp. 378–381)
- Heightened brain sensitivity to psychotropics. "Start low, go slow."
- Avoid anticholinergic and sedating drugs (worsen cognition and confusion).
- Post-traumatic depression: SSRIs preferred. Post-traumatic agitation/aggression: SSRIs, beta-blockers (propranolol), valproate, or low-dose atypical antipsychotics.

#### Liaison Psychiatry Approach (Ch. 22, pp. 631–640)
- Directly contact referring medical/surgical team to clarify referral goal.
- Conduct comprehensive biopsychosocial evaluation integrating physical disease factors.
- Provide clear, direct written formulation and actionable management recommendations in medical notes.

#### Charles Bonnet Syndrome Management (Ch. 19, pp. 555–556)
- **Reassurance:** Explain that hallucinations stem from visual deafferentation (eye disease), NOT mental illness or "going mad" (brings immense patient relief).
- **Ophthalmological Referral:** Optimize vision (cataract surgery, macular degeneration treatment, glasses, improved room lighting).
- **Behavioral Techniques:** Rapid eye movements, blinking, changing lighting. Avoid unnecessary antipsychotics!

## 3. Question map

### Q52 — Severe Self-Neglect / Squalor / Refusing Help
- **Spine:** (1) Clinical picture of Diogenes syndrome / senile squalor; (2) Distinguish self-neglect from autonomous lifestyle choice; (3) Assess underlying causes (bvFTD, AD, schizophrenia defect, severe depression, alcohol misuse); (4) Evaluate physical/environmental risks (malnutrition, infection, fire, collapse, dependants); (5) Capacity assessment specifically for care/living conditions; (6) Multidisciplinary social/medical engagement; (7) Involuntary admission restricted to statutory mental health criteria.
- **Keepers:** Diogenes syndrome features; capacity decision-specific rule; MHA restriction rule (self-neglect alone does not justify involuntary admission).
- **Verify currently:** Greek mental health legal framework regarding involuntary admission and adult safeguarding.
- **Boundaries:** Hoarding disorder belongs in Q31a; FTD in Q49.

### Q53 — Autoimmune Encephalitis Red Flags
- **Spine:** (1) Prototype: Anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis (young females); (2) Psychiatric onset (acute psychosis, agitation, mania, catatonia); (3) Neurological red flags (new seizures, orofacial dyskinesias, choreoathetosis); (4) Autonomic instability and central hypoventilation; (5) Association with ovarian teratoma (50%); (6) Workup: CSF pleocytosis/antibodies, EEG extreme delta brush, pelvic imaging; (7) Treatment: urgent ICU/neurology, first-line IV steroids/IVIG/plasmapheresis, teratoma resection.
- **Keepers:** Anti-NMDAR antibody target; orofacial dyskinesia red flag; ovarian teratoma association; extreme delta brush EEG pattern.
- **Verify currently:** Modern consensus autoantibody panels (anti-LGI1, anti-CASPR2) and second-line immunotherapy (rituximab).
- **Boundaries:** Primary schizophrenia belongs in Q11/Q12; Delirium in Q45.

### Q54 — Epilepsy and Psychiatry
- **Spine:** (1) Temporal classification: ictal (aura, automatisms), peri-ictal (pre-ictal dysphoria, post-ictal psychosis after 24–48h lucid interval), interictal (depression, SLPE); (2) Depression and 5–10x elevated suicide risk (highest in TLE); (3) Gastaut-Geschwind interictal personality concepts; (4) ASM psychiatric side effects (levetiracetam aggression, topiramate slowing); (5) Psychotropic seizure threshold (clozapine/bupropion high risk; SSRIs low risk); (6) Enzyme-inducing ASM drug interactions; (7) Functional non-epileptic seizures (PNES) differential.
- **Keepers:** 24–48h lucid interval in post-ictal psychosis; 5–10x suicide risk in epilepsy; Levetiracetam behavioral side effects; Clozapine/Bupropion seizure risk.
- **Verify currently:** Modern antiseizure medication interaction tables and PNES video-EEG diagnostic protocols.
- **Boundaries:** Functional neurological disorder belongs in Q36.

### Q55 — Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) Psychiatry
- **Spine:** (1) Severity index: Post-Traumatic Amnesia (PTA) duration (<1h mild to >7d very severe); (2) Acute post-traumatic delirium; (3) Organic personality change: orbitofrontal (disinhibition/aggression) vs dorsolateral prefrontal (apathy/inertia); (4) Depression (30%–50%) and 3-4x elevated suicide risk; (5) Post-concussional syndrome; (6) Medication sensitivity ("start low, go slow", avoid anticholinergics); (7) Management of post-traumatic agitation (SSRIs, beta-blockers, valproate).
- **Keepers:** PTA duration severity index; orbitofrontal vs dorsolateral syndromes; 3-4x suicide risk; psychotropic sensitivity rule.
- **Verify currently:** Current neuro-rehabilitation guidelines and pharmacotherapy evidence for TBI agitation.
- **Boundaries:** General delirium belongs in Q45; Apathy differential in Q57.

### Q56 — Liaison Psychiatry in the Medically Ill Inpatient
- **Spine:** (1) Clarify specific referral question with medical/surgical team; (2) Common consultation tasks: delirium (~30%), depression vs adjustment, capacity assessment, self-harm evaluation, functional symptoms; (3) Biopsychosocial formulation integrating physical disease, treatments (steroids, ICU), personality, and social context; (4) Direct written and verbal team communication; (5) Medico-legal capacity rules.
- **Keepers:** Delirium as #1 referral reason; biopsychosocial liaison formulation model; capacity decision-specific framework.
- **Verify currently:** Legal/capacity frameworks in general hospital liaison settings.
- **Boundaries:** General psychiatric history taking belongs in foundational modules; Delirium in Q45.

### Q57 — Apathy vs Depression
- **Spine:** (1) Definitions: Apathy (loss of motivation/drive/initiation) vs Depression (mood misery, worthlessness, guilt, suicidal ideation); (2) Clinical matrix: Apathy features neutral affect, low patient distress, unconcerned thought content; Depression features sad affect, high patient distress, negative cognitions; (3) Anhedonia overlap (passive lack of interest vs painful distress over loss of enjoyment); (4) Neuroanatomy: frontal-subcortical circuit dysfunction; (5) Clinical contexts: bvFTD, Parkinson's, TBI, PSP, stroke, SSRI apathy.
- **Keepers:** Neutral affect & low patient distress in apathy; frontal-subcortical neuroanatomy; bvFTD / Parkinson's context.
- **Verify currently:** Apathy Evaluation Scale (AES) and modern diagnostic criteria for apathy.
- **Boundaries:** FTD diagnosis belongs in Q49; Depression in Q20.

### Q58 — Charles Bonnet Syndrome (CBS)
- **Spine:** (1) Core phenomenology: vivid, complex visual hallucinations in visually impaired individuals; (2) Intact insight (patient recognizes images are not real); (3) Absence of delusions, hallucinations in other modalities, or dementia; (4) Underlying eye disease (macular degeneration, cataracts, glaucoma); (5) Differential: delirium, DLB, PD psychosis, primary psychosis; (6) Management: reassurance (explaining visual deafferentation), ophthalmological optimization, blinking/lighting maneuvers, avoiding antipsychotics.
- **Keepers:** Visual deafferentation mechanism; intact insight mandate; visual loss context; reassurance as primary treatment.
- **Verify currently:** Current ophthalmology-psychiatry referral guidelines for CBS.
- **Boundaries:** DLB visual hallucinations belong in Q48; PD psychosis in Q48a.

## 4. 12 Most Important Claims Requiring Current Verification

1. **Autoimmune Encephalitis Autoantibody Panels & Treatment:** Oxford 7th ed mentions anti-NMDA receptor antibodies. Modern autoantibody panels (anti-LGI1, anti-CASPR2, anti-AMPAR) and second-line immunotherapy (rituximab, cyclophosphamide) require verification.
2. **Greek Legal Framework for Self-Neglect and Involuntary Admission:** Oxford outlines generic UK Mental Health Act principles. Current Greek mental health legislation (Law 2071/1992 and updates) regarding involuntary admission and adult safeguarding requires verification.
3. **Levemitiracetam and Modern Antiseizure Drug Behavioral Effects:** Oxford notes antiseizure drug adverse effects. Detailed behavioral profiles of newer ASMs (brivaracetam, perampanel) require verification.
4. **Clozapine and Bupropion Seizure Threshold Risks:** Oxford identifies clozapine and bupropion as carrying high seizure risk. Exact dose-dependent seizure incidence rates and titration safety limits require current verification.
5. **Post-Traumatic Amnesia (PTA) Severity Classification:** Oxford cites PTA duration thresholds (<1h to >7d). Current neuro-trauma classification guidelines (e.g. Mayo/VA TBI severity scale) require verification.
6. **Parenteral Immunotherapy vs Antipsychotics in Encephalitis:** Oxford notes antipsychotics for agitation. Current guidelines prioritizing early high-dose IV steroids/IVIG over antipsychotics in autoimmune encephalitis require verification.
7. **Pimavanserin and Modern PD Psychosis Pharmacotherapy:** Oxford covers low-dose quetiapine and clozapine for PD visual hallucinations. Pimavanserin licensing status and current BAP/EAN guidelines require verification.
8. **Gastaut-Geschwind Syndrome Validity:** Oxford references TLE personality traits. Current neuropsychiatric consensus regarding the validity and framing of Gastaut-Geschwind syndrome requires verification.
9. **Post-Ictal Psychosis Lucid Interval Rule:** Oxford notes a 24–48 hour lucid interval following seizure clusters before post-ictal psychosis. Clinical variation and treatment protocols require verification.
10. **Liaison Capacity Assessment Frameworks:** Oxford outlines decision-making capacity. Current statutory legal standards for capacity assessment in general medical hospitals require regional verification.
11. **Diagnostic Criteria for Apathy:** Oxford contrasts apathy with depression clinically. Formal validated diagnostic criteria for apathy in neurodegenerative disease (e.g., Robert et al. criteria) require verification.
12. **Charles Bonnet Syndrome Pharmacotherapy Evidence:** Oxford notes reassurance and ophthalmological care, mentioning low-dose medication for refractory cases. Modern evidence for anticonvulsants/SSRIs in CBS requires verification.
