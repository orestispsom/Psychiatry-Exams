## Q90. What is CBT and how is it used in psychiatry?

Must cover:
- Structured, collaborative, formulation-based, goal-oriented psychotherapy.
- Link situations/appraisals, emotions, physiology and behaviour.
- Collaborative empiricism, agreed goals, between-session practice/homework and relapse prevention.
- Core techniques: behavioural activation, exposure, cognitive restructuring and behavioural experiments.
- Technique choice is formulation/disorder-specific.
- Main psychiatric uses may include depression, anxiety disorders, OCD and psychosis where clinically appropriate.

Board facts / updates:
- MBCT is a legitimate contemporary extension, especially for relapse prevention in recurrent/higher-relapse-risk depression.
- Supported digital CBT/guided self-help can be evidence-based for selected disorders/settings, but is not automatically equivalent to full therapist-delivered CBT for every patient.

Trap:
- Do not turn Q90 into a generic “third-wave therapies” answer.

## Q91. What are transference and countertransference, and why do they matter clinically?

Must cover:
- Transference: expectations, affects and relational templates from earlier relationships re-enacted in the therapeutic relationship.
- Countertransference: clinician emotional/cognitive responses within the therapeutic relationship.
- Both can inform formulation but are hypothesis-generating, not objective truth.
- Countertransference must be reflected on in relation to the clinician’s own vulnerabilities and the wider formulation.
- Use supervision, boundaries and reflective practice to prevent enactment.

### Q91a. Idealisation/devaluation and splitting within a team
- Recognise staff polarisation/rescuing versus rejecting positions.
- Use shared formulation, regular communication, consistent boundaries/care plans and supervision.
- Avoid blaming the patient for “splitting the team”, retaliatory responses or special exceptions.

### Q91b. Supportive vs exploratory psychotherapy
- Supportive: stabilisation, containment, coping, reality testing, problem-solving and preservation/improvement of functioning; often more active therapist stance.
- Exploratory: interpretation/reflection on underlying patterns/conflicts; requires sufficient stability, reflective capacity, impulse control, alliance and tolerance of affect/ambiguity.
- Supportive and exploratory elements can shift over time; not rigid mutually exclusive schools.

## Q92. What is IPT, and when is it useful?

Must cover:
- Structured, time-limited, manualised psychotherapy linking symptoms to current interpersonal context.
- Classic problem areas: grief/bereavement, role transitions, role disputes and interpersonal difficulties/deficits.
- Depression is the central board-level indication.

Board facts / updates:
- IPT is also a recognised option in perinatal depression.
- IPSRT is a bipolar adaptation and should not be confused with plain IPT.
- Do not imply IPT displaces disorder-specific first-line psychotherapies in eating disorders.

## Q93. What is psychoeducation, and how does it improve psychiatric treatment?

Must cover:
- More than simply naming the diagnosis.
- Illness/treatment understanding, collaborative treatment decisions, adherence/concordance, self-management, coping, personal relapse signatures/early warning signs and crisis/relapse planning.
- Family/carer involvement where appropriate.

Board facts:
- Family intervention is evidence-based in schizophrenia and bipolar disorder and should be offered where relevant.
- “Offered/recommended” does not mean compulsory family participation.

## Q94. Which frontal-subcortical and limbic circuits are most relevant to psychiatry?

Must cover:
- DLPFC/frontostriatal: executive function, working memory, planning, set-shifting, organisation, cognitive flexibility; dysfunction → dysexecutive picture.
- Orbitofrontal/ventromedial: social judgement, reward/punishment evaluation, impulse control; dysfunction → disinhibition.
- Anterior cingulate/medial frontal: initiation, motivation, effort allocation/action selection; dysfunction → apathy/abulia.
- Amygdala: affective salience, threat/fear learning, emotional processing.
- Hippocampus: episodic/declarative memory and contextual representation.
- Ventral striatum/nucleus accumbens: reward, incentive salience, reinforcement.

Trap:
- Present as high-yield functional associations, not deterministic one-region/one-symptom maps.

## Q95. What are the major dopamine pathways and their clinical significance?

Must cover:
- Mesolimbic: reward/incentive salience; excessive presynaptic striatal dopamine signalling implicated in psychosis/positive symptoms.
- Mesocortical: cognition/motivation; classic relative cortical hypodopaminergia model for negative/cognitive symptoms is useful but incomplete.
- Nigrostriatal: motor function; D2 blockade → EPS.
- Tuberoinfundibular: dopamine suppresses prolactin; D2 blockade → hyperprolactinaemia.

Trap:
- Do not say schizophrenia is simply “too much dopamine everywhere”.

## Q96. What do heritability, familial risk and polygenic risk mean in psychiatry?

Must cover:
- Heritability = proportion of variation in a population under specified environmental conditions attributable to genetic variation; not “X% of this person’s illness is genetic”.
- Familial aggregation reflects shared genetic liability plus shared/developmental environmental factors.
- Major psychiatric disorders are usually highly polygenic: many common variants of very small effect.
- Rare variants/CNVs may have larger effects in selected presentations but explain only a minority of ordinary adult psychiatric illness.
- Pleiotropy/genetic overlap exists across diagnostic boundaries.

Board fact / current practice:
- PRS are not routine stand-alone diagnostic or prognostic tests in ordinary psychiatric practice.

Trap:
- High heritability/polygenicity does not equal individual genetic destiny.

## Q97. How do you critically appraise a psychiatric study and decide whether it should change practice?

Use a viva framework:
1. Define the question/PICO and study design.
2. Assess population/comparator and whether design fits the question.
3. Internal validity: randomisation, allocation concealment, blinding, attrition/missing data, intention-to-treat, confounding/bias as applicable.
4. Results: effect size, confidence intervals/precision, absolute vs relative effect, NNT/NNH where interpretable.
5. Distinguish statistical from clinical significance.
6. Examine harms, treatment burden, multiplicity, external validity/generalisability and funding/conflicts.
7. Ask whether protocol/trial was prospectively registered and outcomes/analyses prespecified; check for selective reporting.
8. Decide whether the result is large, precise, applicable, safe and consistent enough with the total evidence to change practice.

Trap:
- p<0.05 alone does not establish clinical importance.

## Q98. What are the psychiatrist’s duties of confidentiality in Greece?

Lead with current Greek law.

Must cover:
- Core authority: Greek Law 3418/2005 (Code of Medical Ethics), Article 13.
- Strict confidentiality for information learned from the patient or third parties in professional practice concerning the patient/close persons.
- Duty continues after stopping practice and after the patient’s death.
- Physician must supervise collaborators/assistants and protect confidentiality.
- Disclosure can be lawful with valid consent.
- Disclosure may also be permitted/required where fulfilling a legal duty or protecting a justified substantial interest that cannot otherwise be protected, including necessity/defence conditions.
- Penal Code Article 371 gives professional secrecy criminal-law protection; disclosure therefore needs a defensible legal basis.
- Relatives do not automatically have a right to a competent living adult’s confidential psychiatric information.
- Necessary information can be shared within the legitimate treating system on a need-to-know/purpose-limited basis.
- Health data are special-category personal data; GDPR/Law 4624/2019 govern lawful processing/security but do not replace substantive medical confidentiality.
- If disclosure is justified, use necessity/proportionality/data minimisation: disclose only what is needed, to the appropriate recipient, and document reasoning.

Exam/current distinction:
- Replace generic UK/common-law confidentiality answers with Article 13 Greek law.

### Q98a. Credible imminent threat of violence

Clinical structure:
- Assess target, specificity, intent, planning, means/access, imminence, past violence, intoxication/psychosis, escalation and protective factors.
- Reduce opportunity/access and intensify treatment/support where possible.
- Document formulation and response.

Greek legal points:
- Do NOT teach a general Greek Tarasoff duty to warn.
- Article 13 confidentiality exceptions/necessity can justify proportionate protective disclosure in sufficiently serious circumstances.
- Penal Code Article 232 creates a specific reporting duty to the authorities when one reliably learns of a planned or ongoing felony while it can still be prevented, subject to statutory conditions.
- The precise circumstances requiring direct warning of the threatened individual, rather than reporting to authorities or another protective disclosure, were not cleanly established.
- Therefore say: there is no verified general Greek Tarasoff-style duty to warn; action depends on the seriousness/imminence of risk and the applicable legal basis, with proportionate disclosure/reporting and careful documentation.

### Q98b. Social media

Must cover:
- Confidentiality applies online exactly as offline.
- Do not post identifiable patient material without proper lawful basis/consent.
- Avoid boundary-blurring private interactions and dual-role dynamics.
- Patient friend/follow requests should usually be handled through professional boundaries.
- Curiosity-driven online searching of patients is not routine good practice.
- Professional/personal account separation can help but does not remove confidentiality obligations.

Do not claim a special Greek social-media statute was identified.

## Q99. How does Greek law approach criminal responsibility, and how does that differ from clinical psychiatric assessment?

Lead with Greek Penal Code Articles 34 and 36.

Must cover:
- Article 34: at the time of the offence, because of qualifying mental/intellectual disorder or disturbance of consciousness, the person lacked either:
  - capacity to understand the wrongfulness of the act; or
  - capacity to act according to that understanding.
- This is offence-time, retrospective and legal, not simply diagnostic.
- Diagnosis alone does not establish non-imputability.
- Article 36 covers substantially reduced, rather than abolished, capacity and reduced punishment.
- Assessment requires reconstruction of mental state at the offence time using interview, collateral, records, behaviour before/during/after offence and consideration of malingering/substances where relevant.

Distinguish criminal responsibility from:
- diagnosis;
- current decision-making capacity;
- current dangerousness/risk;
- fitness/ability to participate in proceedings.

Exam/current distinction:
- Do not use M’Naghten/UK formulations as Greek law.

## Q100. What are the principles of community care for adults with severe mental illness?

Core principles:
- Multidisciplinary community care.
- Continuity and coordinated case management.
- Crisis intervention/home treatment where feasible.
- Rehabilitation.
- Supported housing and supported employment.
- Family/carer work.
- Physical-health care and substance-use treatment.
- Early intervention.
- Recovery orientation.
- Least restrictive care.
- Social inclusion and relapse prevention.
- Coordination with primary care and social services.

Greek current system:
- Since Law 5129/2024, the top-level structure is the Εθνικό Δίκτυο Υπηρεσιών Ψυχικής Υγείας (Ε.Δ.Υ.Ψ.Υ.).
- It is organised into seven Πε.Δ.Υ.Ψ.Υ. under the corresponding Υ.Πε.
- Community Mental Health Centres (ΚΨΥ), Mobile Mental Health Units and early-intervention/community services continue within this network.
- Current 2026 implementation confirms the new network is operational.
- Do not overstate that every legacy sector/ΤοΨΥ term has disappeared from every local/regulatory document.

Exam/current distinction:
- Replace a legacy UK-style CMHT/AOT/CRHT or old Greek sector-only answer with current Greek network architecture while keeping the universal principles of community psychiatry central.
