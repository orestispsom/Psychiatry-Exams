# Q95. What are the major dopamine pathways and their clinical significance?

## Recall spine

1. **Mesolimbic = reward/incentive salience**
2. **Mesocortical = cognition/motivation**
3. **Nigrostriatal = movement**
4. **Tuberoinfundibular = prolactin control**
5. **D2 blockade links pathways to adverse effects**
6. **Avoid a global dopamine-excess model**

## Model oral answer

The four dopamine pathways most relevant to psychiatry are the **mesolimbic, mesocortical, nigrostriatal and tuberoinfundibular pathways**. They matter because dopamine signalling contributes to reward, salience, cognition and movement, while dopamine-receptor blockade explains several important antipsychotic effects and adverse effects. Schizophrenia should not be reduced to “too much dopamine everywhere.”

The **mesolimbic pathway** is closely involved in reward and incentive salience. In psychosis, excessive **presynaptic striatal dopamine signalling** is implicated particularly in positive psychotic symptoms. The clinically useful model is therefore one of dysregulated salience-related dopamine signalling rather than a nonspecific excess throughout the brain.

The **mesocortical pathway** contributes to cognition and motivation. The classic teaching is that relatively reduced cortical dopaminergic function may contribute to negative and cognitive symptoms in schizophrenia. That model remains useful as a heuristic, but cortical dopamine findings are more complex and it should not be presented as a complete mechanism of negative symptoms.

The **nigrostriatal pathway** is central to motor function. Dopamine D2 blockade in this pathway is the key pharmacological link to extrapyramidal adverse effects such as parkinsonism and other movement syndromes.

The **tuberoinfundibular pathway** regulates prolactin secretion. Dopamine normally suppresses prolactin release, so D2 blockade can remove that inhibitory influence and produce hyperprolactinaemia, with clinically relevant endocrine and sexual consequences.

A classic PET teaching point is that antipsychotic response and adverse effects relate to striatal D2 occupancy: therapeutic response often occurs in a mid-range occupancy zone, while EPS and prolactin risk rise at higher occupancy. However, the commonly quoted numerical thresholds are **heuristics rather than routine treatment targets** and vary by drug, patient and partial-agonist pharmacology.

A concise oral formulation is therefore: mesolimbic dopamine relates to reward, incentive salience and positive psychosis; mesocortical dopamine is linked to cognition and motivation; nigrostriatal dopamine governs motor function and explains EPS with D2 blockade; and tuberoinfundibular dopamine suppresses prolactin, so blockade can cause hyperprolactinaemia.

## Must-know board facts

- **Mesolimbic:** reward/incentive salience; presynaptic striatal dopamine dysregulation is strongly implicated in psychosis.
- **Mesocortical:** cognition/motivation; classic hypodopaminergic teaching is a heuristic, not a complete mechanism.
- **Nigrostriatal:** movement; D2 blockade → EPS.
- **Tuberoinfundibular:** dopamine inhibits prolactin; D2 blockade → hyperprolactinaemia.
- D2-occupancy numbers belong to **classic PET heuristics**, not routine measured prescribing targets.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Saying schizophrenia is simply caused by **too much dopamine everywhere**.
- Treating the classic mesocortical hypodopaminergia model as a complete explanation of negative and cognitive symptoms.
- Treating one D2-occupancy percentage as a universal efficacy or EPS threshold across all drugs, especially partial agonists.
