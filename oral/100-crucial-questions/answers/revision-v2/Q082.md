# Q82. What do you need to know about valproate in psychiatry?

## Recall spine

1. **Bipolar indication where appropriate**
2. **Weight and metabolic effects**
3. **Tremor and haematological toxicity**
4. **Hepatic, pancreatic and hyperammonaemic toxicity**
5. **Baseline and ongoing monitoring**
6. **Pregnancy Prevention Programme**
7. **Current male reproductive precautions**

## Model oral answer

Valproate can be used in bipolar disorder in appropriate patients, but prescribing is dominated by its **metabolic, haematological, hepatic, pancreatic and reproductive risks**. I would use it only after considering alternatives, obtain the required baseline and ongoing monitoring, and apply the current reproductive restrictions to both women and men. The reproductive framework must be stated accurately rather than reduced to either an outdated permissive approach or an unconditional ban in every patient who could conceive.

Important adverse effects include **weight gain and broader metabolic burden**, tremor and haematological abnormalities including thrombocytopenia. More serious toxicities include **hepatotoxicity and pancreatitis**. Hyperammonaemic encephalopathy is another important safety diagnosis and can occur **even when transaminases are normal**, so unexplained encephalopathy on valproate should prompt consideration of ammonia rather than reliance on liver enzymes alone.

Baseline and ongoing monitoring should follow the current product information and guideline. A practical NICE stable-treatment anchor is **weight/BMI, FBC and liver-function testing at 6 months and then annually** after the initial treatment phase, with additional testing when symptoms or clinical risk require it.

The most important modern change is reproductive safety. For **bipolar disorder, valproate is contraindicated during pregnancy**. In women and girls able to become pregnant, it should not be described as an unconditional absolute prohibition in every circumstance: use is contraindicated **unless the stringent Pregnancy Prevention Programme conditions are met** and alternative treatments are unsuitable, ineffective or not tolerated under specialist management.

Current European precautions also extend to **men taking valproate**. Men should receive counselling about the potential paternal neurodevelopmental risk. Contraception considerations apply during treatment and for **3 months after stopping valproate**, and sperm donation should be avoided during that same interval.

Mechanistically, valproate has multiple molecular effects, including increased **GABAergic signalling** and ion-channel effects. I would not present HDAC inhibition or one molecular action as the established explanation of its antimanic efficacy.

These reproductive issues should be integrated into ordinary prescribing rather than raised only after pregnancy occurs. The prescriber should establish whether pregnancy is relevant, discuss alternatives and reproductive plans, and document that the patient understands the applicable restrictions and precautions.

The concise viva answer is therefore: valproate is an effective mood-stabilising option in selected bipolar patients but requires attention to metabolic, haematological, hepatic, pancreatic and hyperammonaemic toxicity, appropriate laboratory monitoring, and stringent modern reproductive regulation.

## Must-know board facts

- For bipolar disorder, valproate is **contraindicated during pregnancy**.
- In women/girls able to become pregnant, use is contraindicated unless **Pregnancy Prevention Programme** conditions are met and alternatives are unsuitable, ineffective or not tolerated under specialist care.
- Men require counselling and contraception considerations during treatment and for **3 months after stopping**; no sperm donation during that interval.
- Stable NICE monitoring anchor: **weight/BMI, FBC and LFTs at 6 months, then annually**.
- Hyperammonaemic encephalopathy can occur with **normal transaminases**.
- Valproate has multiple pharmacological effects; do not teach one research mechanism such as HDAC inhibition as the clinical antimanic mechanism.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Describing valproate as an unconditional absolute ban in every woman of childbearing potential.
- Forgetting the current **male reproductive precautions**.
- Missing hyperammonaemia because liver enzymes are normal.
- Quoting old teratogenicity percentages instead of giving the current prescribing framework.
- Turning a complex mechanism into one simplistic receptor/epigenetic explanation.
