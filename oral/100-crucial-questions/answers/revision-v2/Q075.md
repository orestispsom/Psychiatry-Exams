# Q75. What are the most important psychotropic drug interactions?

## Recall spine

1. **CYP induction and inhibition**
2. **Serotonergic combinations and MAOI washout**
3. **Additive QT prolongation**
4. **CNS and respiratory depression**
5. **Lithium accumulation**
6. **Valproate, lamotrigine and enzyme-inducing ASMs**
7. **Bleeding risk**

## Model oral answer

The safest way to approach psychotropic interactions is by **mechanism and clinical consequence**, not by memorising isolated pairs. The main high-yield groups are metabolic interactions through CYP enzymes, serotonergic toxicity, additive QT risk, additive CNS depression, lithium accumulation, important antiseizure-drug interactions and increased bleeding risk.

For metabolic interactions, smoking is a classic example. **Tobacco-smoke polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons induce CYP1A2; nicotine does not.** Smoking can therefore lower clozapine or olanzapine exposure, while smoking cessation can increase it. Nicotine replacement does not preserve the smoke-induced enzyme effect, so medication review is needed when smoking status changes.

CYP inhibitors can raise susceptible drug concentrations, but fixed fold-change tables are misleading because magnitude depends on the substrate, dose, genotype, smoking and co-medication. Clinically, fluoxetine and paroxetine are important CYP2D6 inhibitors and fluvoxamine is an important CYP1A2 inhibitor, but the viva answer should focus on recognising the mechanism and checking the actual product interaction rather than quoting universal multipliers.

A second major category is **serotonergic toxicity**. Irreversible MAOIs should not be combined with serotonergic antidepressants without the appropriate washout. A high-yield product-specific safety anchor is **at least 5 weeks after stopping fluoxetine before starting an irreversible MAOI**, because of fluoxetine/norfluoxetine’s long persistence. In the opposite direction, the current fluoxetine product information requires **at least 2 weeks after stopping an irreversible MAOI before starting fluoxetine**. Other switches are product-specific and should not be inferred from these two intervals.

**QT risk is cumulative.** Combining QT-prolonging drugs, particularly in a patient with additional cardiac or electrolyte risk, may produce substantially more concern than either drug in isolation. The response is to review the complete medication list and the patient’s risk factors rather than treating QT risk as a property of one drug alone.

Additive sedation and respiratory depression are particularly important with **benzodiazepines plus opioids or alcohol**. This is a clinically significant interaction because CNS depressant effects can combine even when each substance is familiar to the patient.

**Lithium** has several important pharmacokinetic interactions. NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors or ARBs and diuretics can raise lithium exposure, with thiazides being a particularly important interaction. Dehydration can further increase risk, so intercurrent illness and fluid loss must be included in the medication history.

With mood stabilisers and antiseizure drugs, **valproate inhibits lamotrigine glucuronidation and raises lamotrigine exposure**, which is why the exact SmPC titration schedule changes when the two are combined. Conversely, enzyme-inducing antiseizure medicines can lower concentrations of susceptible psychotropics and may cause apparent nonresponse if the interaction is missed.

Finally, SSRIs and SNRIs can increase **bleeding risk**, especially when combined with NSAIDs, antiplatelet drugs or anticoagulants. The clinical response depends on individual gastrointestinal and bleeding risk; it is not correct to say that every such combination automatically requires a proton-pump inhibitor.

The practical rule is to review interactions whenever a psychotropic is started, stopped or changed, and whenever smoking, physical illness or co-prescribing changes. Interaction risk is cumulative and patient-specific.

## Must-know board facts

- **Nicotine replacement does not reproduce tobacco-smoke CYP1A2 induction.**
- Fluoxetine → irreversible MAOI: wait **at least 5 weeks**.
- Irreversible MAOI → fluoxetine: wait **at least 2 weeks**.
- These exact intervals are **product-specific**, not a universal rule for every antidepressant.
- **Valproate inhibits lamotrigine glucuronidation**, increasing exposure and rash risk; follow the SmPC titration rather than applying a simple percentage rule.
- Benzodiazepine + opioid/alcohol can cause dangerous **additive respiratory depression**.
- Lithium exposure can rise with **NSAIDs, ACEi/ARBs, diuretics and dehydration**.
- Psychotropic interaction risk is often **cumulative and patient-specific**, especially for QT prolongation, sedation and bleeding.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Saying nicotine induces CYP1A2.
- Quoting fixed CYP interaction multipliers as though they apply to every patient.
- Blind cross-tapering through an irreversible MAOI.
- Applying the fluoxetine washout interval to all antidepressants without checking product information.
- Ignoring non-psychiatric medicines such as NSAIDs, antihypertensives, opioids or anticoagulants.
- Assuming a PPI is always required whenever an SSRI is combined with an NSAID or anticoagulant.
