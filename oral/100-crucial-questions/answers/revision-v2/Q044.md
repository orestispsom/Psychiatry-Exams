# Q44. How do you diagnose and manage gambling disorder?

## Recall spine

1. **Identify impaired control, priority and persistence despite harm**
2. **Define financial, social and suicide-related consequences**
3. **Distinguish persistent gambling disorder from gambling during mania**
4. **Use gambling-specific CBT as first-line treatment**
5. **Add practical financial harm reduction**
6. **Reserve naltrexone for selected specialist second-line use**

## Model oral answer

Gambling disorder is a **behavioural addiction**, not simply frequent gambling or an impulse-control problem. The core pattern is impaired control over gambling, increasing priority given to it over other activities, and continuation or escalation despite significant negative consequences. Assessment must include financial and safeguarding harm, suicide risk and the possibility that gambling is occurring only within a manic or hypomanic episode.

I would first establish the **pattern and loss of control**: how often the person gambles, whether attempts to cut down repeatedly fail, how much time and attention gambling occupies, and whether it has displaced work, relationships or other responsibilities. Classic phenomenology such as **chasing losses**, the **gambler’s fallacy** and near-miss cognitions can be useful, but the diagnosis rests on the persistent addictive pattern and resulting impairment rather than on one cognitive distortion alone.

The harms can be severe and should be assessed directly. I would ask about **debt, access to money, borrowing, occupational consequences, relationship breakdown, legal problems, risks to dependants and safeguarding concerns**. Suicide risk requires explicit assessment, particularly when the patient is facing acute financial or interpersonal crisis.

The bipolar differential is important. Gambling that occurs only during a clear **manic or hypomanic episode** should prompt assessment and treatment of bipolar disorder rather than automatic diagnosis of gambling disorder. Conversely, persistent pathological gambling outside mood episodes may represent a coexisting gambling disorder, so the two conditions are not mutually exclusive.

The first-line treatment is **gambling-specific CBT**. Group CBT is a standard option when acceptable and feasible; individual CBT is appropriate when group treatment is unsuitable or declined. Motivational work and relapse-prevention strategies can support engagement, especially where ambivalence or repeated relapse is prominent.

Treatment should also reduce immediate access to harm. Practical measures can include **self-exclusion, limit-setting, restricting access to funds**, and involving financial, social or legal support where appropriate. These interventions are not merely administrative; they reduce environmental opportunities that maintain the addictive behaviour and help protect the patient and dependants from further loss.

Pharmacotherapy is not routine first-line treatment. However, **off-label naltrexone may be considered in specialist care** after adequate psychological treatment has failed or when repeated relapse persists. It should be presented as a selected second-line option rather than as standard medication for all patients.

## Must-know board facts

- **ICD-11 classifies gambling disorder under disorders due to addictive behaviours.**
- **DSM-5-TR gambling disorder:** at least **4 of 9 criteria over 12 months**. This is a DSM-specific board scaffold, not the ICD-11 architecture.
- **Gambler’s fallacy and near-miss effects** are clinically relevant cognitive distortions but are not themselves diagnostic criteria.
- **Naltrexone** has a selected specialist second-line off-label role after adequate psychological treatment failure or repeated relapse; it is not routine first-line pharmacotherapy.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Diagnosing gambling disorder from gambling that occurs only during mania or hypomania.
- Ignoring suicide risk, debt crisis and safeguarding of dependants.
- Treating gambling disorder simply as an impulse-control disorder.
- Treating a cognitive distortion such as the gambler’s fallacy as the diagnosis itself.
- Presenting naltrexone as routine first-line medication.

## Exam answer vs current practice

**Classification:** Older teaching often placed pathological gambling primarily within impulse-control disorders. Current ICD-11 places gambling disorder within disorders due to addictive behaviours.

**Medication:** Older answers may state that medication has no role at all. Current guidance allows selected specialist off-label naltrexone after psychological treatment failure or repeated relapse, while CBT remains first-line.
