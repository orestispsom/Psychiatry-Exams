# Q19. Tell me about clozapine: indications, major risks and monitoring.

## Recall spine

1. **Indication**
2. **Haematological** — ANC thresholds and schedule
3. **Cardiac** — myocarditis/cardiomyopathy
4. **Gastrointestinal** — hypomotility/constipation
5. **Neurological/other** — seizures, metabolic effects, sedation, hypersalivation
6. **Exposure/TDM** — smoking, infection, interacting medicines
7. **Interruption/restart** — >2 days means re-titration

## Model oral answer

Clozapine is the key treatment for **treatment-resistant schizophrenia after two adequate antipsychotic failures**, but it requires active safety management because several adverse effects can be life-threatening. Its main clinical indication is treatment-resistant schizophrenia. European product information also includes schizophrenia with **severe untreatable neurological adverse reactions** to other antipsychotics and psychosis in **Parkinson disease after failure of standard treatment**. Guidelines also support clozapine in persistent substantial suicide risk despite other treatment, and persistent substantial aggression may justify its use; these guideline-supported uses should be distinguished from formal product indications.

The major safety issues can be organised into six practical groups.

**Haematological:** the current European framework uses **ANC rather than combined WBC and ANC monitoring**. Treatment can be initiated with ANC at least **1.5 ×10^9/L**, or at least **1.0 ×10^9/L** in confirmed benign ethnic or Duffy-null neutropenia. In the general population, ANC below **1.0 ×10^9/L** requires immediate discontinuation with no re-exposure; for confirmed BEN/Duffy-null neutropenia the corresponding threshold is below **0.5 ×10^9/L**. Mild neutropenia between **1.0 and 1.5 ×10^9/L** can be managed with enhanced monitoring within the revised framework. Routine ANC monitoring is **weekly for the first 18 weeks, monthly for the next 34 weeks to one year, every 12 weeks after the first year if there has been no neutropenia, and annually after two years if there was no neutropenia in the first two years**. New infection symptoms require prompt assessment and immediate ANC testing.

**Cardiac:** myocarditis is especially important early in treatment, with greatest risk in the first two months. Persistent unexplained tachycardia, chest pain, dyspnoea, heart-failure symptoms or a flu-like illness should raise suspicion; suspected myocarditis requires prompt cessation and urgent cardiology assessment. Baseline and weekly CRP and troponin for roughly the first four weeks is a useful expert-consensus screening strategy, but not one universally mandated European laboratory schedule.

**Gastrointestinal:** gastrointestinal hypomotility and constipation can progress to faecal impaction, ileus, megacolon or ischaemia. Bowel function must be asked about actively and constipation treated, particularly when other bowel-risk factors or anticholinergic or opioid medication are present.

**Neurological and other major adverse effects:** seizure risk is dose- or exposure-related. Metabolic effects and weight gain, sedation and hypersalivation are also important practical burdens.

**Exposure-changing interactions and TDM:** cigarette smoke induces **CYP1A2**, so abrupt smoking cessation can raise clozapine concentrations, while resumption can lower exposure. Serious infection, especially pneumonia, can suppress CYP1A2 through inflammation and markedly increase exposure; infection plus smoking cessation can compound the effect. Therapeutic drug monitoring is particularly useful in apparent non-response, adherence uncertainty, toxicity, smoking change, interacting medication and serious infection.

**Interruption and restart:** if more than **2 days** have passed since the last dose, clozapine should be restarted at **12.5 mg once or twice on day 1** and then re-titrated according to tolerability. The previous full dose must not simply be resumed because of hypotension and collapse risk. Haematological restart rules are separate from pharmacological re-titration.

## Must-know board facts

- **ANC to start:** ≥1.5 ×10^9/L; confirmed BEN/Duffy-null neutropenia ≥1.0 ×10^9/L.
- **ANC stop/no re-exposure:** general population <1.0 ×10^9/L; confirmed BEN/Duffy-null neutropenia <0.5 ×10^9/L.
- **Routine ANC schedule:** weekly 18 weeks → monthly to 1 year → every 12 weeks after year 1 if no neutropenia → annually after 2 years if no neutropenia in the first 2 years.
- **Restart after >2 days off:** 12.5 mg once or twice on day 1, then re-titrate according to tolerability.
- **Before declaring clozapine non-response:** a TRRIP optimum-exposure anchor is trough concentration **≥350 ng/mL**; this is not a universal toxicity threshold.

## Examiner follow-ups

### Q19a. The patient develops pneumonia and abruptly stops smoking. What happens to clozapine?

Both changes can **increase clozapine exposure at the same time**: inflammation suppresses CYP1A2 and smoking cessation removes smoke-induced CYP1A2 induction. Toxicity may emerge quickly, with sedation, confusion or delirium, hypersalivation and aspiration risk, hypotension or seizures, potentially worsening the pneumonia.

The priorities are urgent physical assessment and treatment of the infection, an ANC, a clozapine level where possible, and review of interacting antibiotics and other medication. Clozapine should be reduced promptly or temporarily held according to clinical severity rather than continuing the pre-illness smoking dose unchanged. Close monitoring is required, with later dose readjustment or re-titration as inflammation resolves and smoking status stabilises.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Using obsolete combined WBC+ANC or lifelong-monthly European monitoring rules.
- Restarting the previous full dose after more than 2 days off.
- Remembering agranulocytosis but forgetting myocarditis or gastrointestinal hypomotility.
- Ignoring smoking cessation and serious infection as major causes of acute clozapine toxicity.
- Applying consensus percentage dose reductions mechanically without clinical assessment or TDM context.

## Exam answer vs current practice

**Haematological monitoring update:** Older European teaching used different haematological thresholds and lifelong monthly monitoring. The current European framework uses ANC-only monitoring with the revised thresholds and reduced monitoring frequency after sustained treatment without neutropenia.
