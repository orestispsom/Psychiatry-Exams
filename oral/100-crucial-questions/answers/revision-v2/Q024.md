# Q24. How do you treat acute mania?

## Recall spine

1. **Risk, setting and medical differential**
2. **Consider stopping destabilising antidepressants**
3. **Use a guideline-specific antimanic sequence**
4. **Escalate according to response and severity**
5. **Short-term benzodiazepine adjunct if needed**
6. **ECT for severe or refractory mania**
7. **Apply current valproate reproductive restrictions**

## Model oral answer

Acute mania requires simultaneous assessment of **risk, cause and severity**, while antimanic treatment is started promptly. I would assess danger to self or others, behavioural control, psychosis, severe agitation, sleep deprivation, substance use, capacity, medical or neurological causes, current medication and the patient’s ability to adhere. Severe mania often requires urgent specialist or inpatient management, with containment and physical assessment proceeding in parallel where possible.

If mania or hypomania has emerged while the patient is taking an **antidepressant**, I would consider stopping it as part of acute management. That should not be translated into an unqualified rule to stop every antidepressant abruptly, because withdrawal risk varies by drug. Substance intoxication or withdrawal and relevant medical causes also need active treatment rather than assuming the syndrome is purely psychiatric.

The pharmacological sequence should be stated with its **guideline context**. In the NICE pathway, if the patient is not already taking an antipsychotic or mood stabiliser, an antipsychotic is offered first. If the first antipsychotic is ineffective or poorly tolerated, another antipsychotic is offered. If response remains inadequate, **lithium is added**; if lithium is ineffective or unsuitable, **valproate is then considered**, subject to its major reproductive restrictions. Other international guidelines use somewhat different first-line hierarchies, so I would not merge all systems into one universal ranking.

For severe behavioural disturbance, short-term **benzodiazepines** may be used adjunctively for agitation or insomnia while definitive antimanic treatment takes effect, but they are not the core treatment of mania. Supportive management also matters: reduce stimulation, restore sleep, hydration and nutrition, manage substances and involve carers where appropriate.

**ECT** remains an important option when mania is prolonged, severe, refractory or life-threatening. It should not be excluded simply because the syndrome is manic rather than depressive.

Whenever **valproate** is considered, current reproductive safety restrictions are essential. It is contraindicated for bipolar disorder during pregnancy. In girls and women able to become pregnant, it is contraindicated unless Pregnancy Prevention Programme conditions are fulfilled and alternative treatments are ineffective or not tolerated. For men, current European precautions require specialist initiation or supervision, counselling about a potential paternal-exposure neurodevelopmental signal, effective contraception for the man and female partner during treatment and for at least **3 months after stopping**, avoidance of sperm donation during that period, and regular review.

The overall approach is therefore: secure safety and an appropriate setting, correct destabilising factors, use a clearly identified guideline sequence for antimanic treatment, escalate promptly when response is inadequate, and keep reproductive safety integral to any valproate decision.

## Must-know board facts

- **NICE acute-mania sequence:** offer an antipsychotic → if needed try another antipsychotic → add lithium if response remains inadequate → consider valproate if lithium is unsuitable or ineffective. Other guidelines may sequence these treatments differently.
- **Antidepressants:** NICE says to **consider stopping** an antidepressant during mania/hypomania; do not turn this into an automatic abrupt-discontinuation rule for every drug.
- **Valproate in pregnancy:** contraindicated for bipolar disorder.
- **Women able to become pregnant:** use is contraindicated unless Pregnancy Prevention Programme conditions are met and alternatives are ineffective or not tolerated.
- **Regulatory counselling anchor:** official European information describes major congenital-malformation risk of roughly **10–11%** and developmental problems in up to roughly **30–40%** of exposed children. These are regulatory counselling figures, not general epidemiological rules.
- **Men taking valproate:** discuss effective contraception for the patient and female partner during treatment and for at least **3 months after stopping**; no sperm donation for at least 3 months after stopping. This is a precaution based on a potential risk signal.
- Do **not** memorise valproate loading doses or serum ranges as a universal acute-mania rule; dosing follows current product/guideline context.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Treating agitation alone and forgetting the manic syndrome or medical differential.
- Saying antidepressants must always be stopped abruptly.
- Presenting lithium, valproate and antipsychotics as one universal co-equal first-line hierarchy without naming the guideline.
- Calling valproate “first line” without current reproductive restrictions.
- Using benzodiazepines as definitive antimanic treatment.

## Exam answer vs current practice

**Treatment hierarchy:** Major guidelines differ in sequencing. A current viva answer should identify the framework being used rather than blend NICE and international algorithms.

**Valproate:** Older routine use without reproductive qualification is outdated. Current European reproductive restrictions must accompany any recommendation of valproate in bipolar disorder.
