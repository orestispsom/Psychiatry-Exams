# Q26. How do you manage bipolar disorder long term?

## Recall spine

1. **Shared relapse-prevention and recovery plan**
2. **Lithium as a central maintenance option**
3. **Choose alternatives by polarity and previous response**
4. **Psychoeducation, adherence, sleep and substances**
5. **Physical-health monitoring**
6. **Suicide prevention and functional recovery**
7. **Individualise duration and withdraw gradually if stopping**
8. **Apply valproate reproductive restrictions**

## Model oral answer

Long-term management of bipolar disorder should be a **shared relapse-prevention and recovery plan**, not simply continuation of whatever controlled the last episode. I would review the pattern and polarity of previous episodes, individual relapse signatures and triggers, adherence, sleep and circadian regularity, substance use, suicide risk, social and occupational recovery, and family or carer needs.

Bipolar disorder is generally recurrent, so maintenance treatment is commonly long term and may be indefinite after recurrent or severe episodes. There is no single universal stopping date. The choice of maintenance treatment should be guided by previous response, predominant relapse polarity, tolerability, comorbidity and patient preference.

**Lithium** remains a central first-line maintenance treatment and is often the reference treatment for long-term bipolar disorder. It is particularly valuable when it has previously been effective and is suitable for the individual patient. Other established maintenance options include **quetiapine, lamotrigine, valproate/divalproex and selected antipsychotics**, with the choice determined by previous response and the polarity of relapses rather than assuming that every agent prevents mania and depression equally well.

Phase specificity is important. **Lamotrigine is stronger for prevention of depressive relapse and is not an acute antimanic drug. Aripiprazole has stronger evidence for prevention of manic relapse, while lithium and quetiapine have broader maintenance roles.** These are useful directional tendencies rather than a rigid ranking for every patient.

Medication is only one part of maintenance care. **Psychoeducation, adherence work, recognition of early warning signs, family-focused or CBT-informed interventions where appropriate, regular sleep and daily routine, and substance-use management** all contribute to relapse prevention. Psychoeducation and family or rhythm-based interventions can reduce relapse and improve self-management in selected populations, but fixed percentage reductions should not be memorised as universal effects.

Physical health requires active attention, including weight and metabolic or cardiovascular risk and the adverse effects specific to the treatment being used. Lithium also has evidence for reducing suicide and self-harm risk in mood disorders, which is an additional reason to favour it in suitable patients, although the size of that effect varies across studies and it does not eliminate suicide risk.

If discontinuation is considered, the decision should be individualised according to the number and severity of previous episodes, psychosis, consequences of relapse, residual symptoms, previous relapse pattern, adverse effects and patient preference. Withdrawal should be **gradual**, with an explicit relapse-monitoring plan.

Whenever **valproate** is considered for maintenance, the current reproductive restrictions remain essential. It is contraindicated for bipolar disorder during pregnancy. In girls and women able to become pregnant, it is contraindicated unless Pregnancy Prevention Programme conditions are met and alternatives are ineffective or not tolerated. In men, current European precautions require specialist counselling about a potential paternal-exposure neurodevelopmental signal, effective contraception for the man and female partner during treatment and for at least **3 months after stopping**, and no sperm donation during that period.

## Must-know board facts

- **Lithium maintenance target:** **0.6–0.8 mmol/L** under NICE.
- **Selected higher maintenance range:** consider **0.8–1.0 mmol/L** when there has been relapse while taking lithium or persistent subthreshold symptoms with functional impairment.
- **Lithium level timing:** sample at about **12 hours post-dose**.
- **Lithium and suicide:** evidence supports reduction in suicide/self-harm risk in mood disorders, but do not quote a fixed percentage reduction as universal.
- **Pole-specific maintenance:** lamotrigine is stronger for depressive-relapse prevention; aripiprazole is stronger for manic-relapse prevention; lithium and quetiapine have broader roles.
- **Long-term care is not medication-only:** psychoeducation, relapse-signature work, adherence, sleep/rhythm management, physical health and functional recovery are core components.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Simply continuing the acute drug indefinitely without considering polarity or previous response.
- Forgetting psychoeducation, adherence and physical health.
- Quoting a fixed lithium suicide-prevention percentage.
- Treating every maintenance drug as equally effective against both poles.
- Listing valproate without current reproductive restrictions.

## Exam answer vs current practice

**Lithium monitoring anchor:** A high-level maintenance answer should be able to state **0.6–0.8 mmol/L**, selected **0.8–1.0 mmol/L**, and a **12-hour** sample without turning the page into the full lithium-monitoring question.

**Valproate:** Older maintenance answers may list valproate without qualification. Current European practice requires the reproductive restrictions whenever valproate is materially considered.
