# Q09. When can a patient be admitted involuntarily in Greece, and what is the procedure?

## Recall spine

1. **Know the two alternative statutory routes**
2. **Identify who may initiate**
3. **Obtain the required medical opinions**
4. **Use the 48-hour examination route when prior examination is impossible**
5. **Prosecutor and court deadlines**
6. **Patient rights, duration and review**
7. **Healthcare-led transport and admission ≠ blanket treatment consent**

## Model oral answer

In Greece, involuntary psychiatric hospitalisation remains governed substantively by the statutory framework of Law 2071/1992, as subsequently amended. There are **two alternative legal routes**, so dangerousness is not the only criterion. The procedure also contains medical, prosecutorial and judicial safeguards, and current transport policy is healthcare-led rather than treating police conveyance as the default.

The first route requires **all three elements**: the person has a mental disorder; because of that disorder they are unable to judge the interest of their own health; and without hospitalisation either treatment would be excluded or their health would deteriorate. The second route is alternative: there is a mental disorder and hospitalisation is necessary to prevent acts of violence against the patient or others. Mere social, moral or political nonconformity is explicitly not a mental disorder.

The procedure may be initiated by the **statutorily specified spouse, relatives or caregiver**. If none is available, the competent Public Prosecutor may act ex officio in an emergency.

Ordinarily, **two reasoned written medical opinions** are required. These are generally from two psychiatrists or, if two cannot be found, from one psychiatrist and a physician of a related specialty. The opinions must address the statutory criteria, and the examining doctors must not be relatives of the applicant or patient.

If prior examination cannot be completed because the patient refuses assessment, or in an ex officio process, the prosecutor may order transfer to a **public psychiatric clinic for examination**. Detention for this examination may not exceed **48 hours**. This 48-hour period is an examination safeguard; it is not the duration limit of involuntary hospitalisation.

With concordant supporting opinions, the prosecutor orders transfer and admission. If the opinions conflict, transfer may still occur with the matter determined judicially. The prosecutor must submit the case to the court within **3 days** of ordering transfer, and the court sits within **10 days**. The patient must be summoned at least **48 hours before the hearing** and may appear with a lawyer and a psychiatrist as technical adviser. Proceedings are held in camera and the decision must be specifically reasoned. If the application is rejected, the patient is discharged immediately.

Current first-instance jurisdiction is the **Μονομελές Πρωτοδικείο**. Older statutory wording may still refer to the Πολυμελές Πρωτοδικείο, but that is not the current procedural jurisdiction.

Hospitalisation should end once the statutory criteria no longer apply. The ordinary statutory maximum is **6 months**, with review and reporting safeguards, including a report after the first **3 months** and exceptional conditions for extension.

Transport has also been modernised. The current statutory model is **healthcare-led and dignity-preserving**. Police involvement in onward transfer is exceptional and tied to statutory conditions, including a psychiatrist’s written assessment where imminent harm cannot be prevented by de-escalation. Actual service availability may vary, but police transport should not be presented as the default model.

Finally, involuntary hospitalisation does **not** automatically authorise every treatment. Admission status and consent to a particular intervention remain distinct legal questions.

## Must-know board facts

- **Route I:** mental disorder + impaired judgement of the interest of one’s own health + treatment would be excluded or health would deteriorate without hospitalisation.
- **Route II:** mental disorder + hospitalisation necessary to prevent violence to self or others.
- **Key deadlines:** examination detention ≤48 hours when prior examination is impossible; prosecutor to court within 3 days; court within 10 days; summons ≥48 hours before hearing.
- **Current first-instance court:** Μονομελές Πρωτοδικείο.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Treating dangerousness as the only legal criterion.
- Confusing the 48-hour examination window with the duration of involuntary hospitalisation.
- Saying police transport is the default current model.
- Conflating involuntary admission with unrestricted involuntary treatment.

## Exam answer vs current practice

**Court and transport update:** Older formulations may still name the Πολυμελές Πρωτοδικείο and describe police-centred transfer. Current first-instance jurisdiction is the **Μονομελές Πρωτοδικείο**, and the current statutory transport model is healthcare-led, with police involvement exceptional under the specified conditions. An unenacted reform proposal should not be presented as law.
