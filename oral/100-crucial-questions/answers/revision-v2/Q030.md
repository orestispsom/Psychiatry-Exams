# Q30. How do you diagnose and manage social anxiety disorder?

## Recall spine

1. **Fear of scrutiny and negative evaluation**
2. **Avoidance or endurance with marked anxiety and impairment**
3. **Distinguish normal shyness from disorder**
4. **Separate from avoidant personality disorder and autism**
5. **Use individual disorder-specific CBT first**
6. **Use an SSRI if CBT is declined and medication is preferred**

## Model oral answer

Social anxiety disorder is characterised by **marked fear or anxiety in social situations because of anticipated scrutiny, embarrassment or negative evaluation**, leading to avoidance or endurance with intense anxiety and clinically significant impairment. The diagnosis is not simply severe shyness; the fear has to organise behaviour and interfere meaningfully with functioning.

I would clarify which social situations are feared, what the patient predicts will happen, how much avoidance has developed and what this costs socially, educationally or occupationally. It is also useful to identify **safety behaviours** and excessive self-focused attention, because these can maintain the disorder even when overt avoidance is limited.

The differential needs care. **Avoidant personality disorder** is a broader and more enduring personality pattern involving social inhibition, feelings of inadequacy and hypersensitivity across contexts. Social anxiety disorder is more specifically organised around feared scrutiny and negative evaluation. The two can coexist, so one should not be diagnosed away merely because the other is present.

**Autism** is different again: it is neurodevelopmental and involves persistent social-communication differences together with restricted, repetitive or sensory features. A person with autism may avoid social situations because communication or sensory demands are difficult rather than because negative evaluation is the primary fear. Autism and social anxiety can nevertheless coexist, so the clinical task is to establish the mechanism of avoidance rather than infer diagnosis from social withdrawal alone.

Under NICE, the preferred first-line psychological treatment is **individual, disorder-specific CBT**. It should directly target the maintaining mechanisms through exposure or behavioural experiments, reduction of safety behaviours and self-focused attention, and cognitive restructuring of feared social predictions. In Clark-and-Wells-style treatment, dropping safety behaviours and shifting attention away from internal self-monitoring are central procedures; video feedback may be used to test distorted self-imagery. Generic supportive therapy is not equivalent to this disorder-specific approach.

If the person declines CBT and prefers medication, NICE recommends an **SSRI**, with sertraline or escitalopram as examples. Other medication strategies can be considered after nonresponse or intolerance, but this should not be presented as though individual CBT and medication are automatically co-equal first steps in the NICE sequence.

**Beta-blockers are not established core treatment for social anxiety disorder.** They may reduce peripheral autonomic symptoms in selected performance situations, but a fixed propranolol dose or an “only for performance subtype” rule should not become the board-treatment framework.

The practical answer is therefore to diagnose a persistent fear of social evaluation with avoidance and impairment, distinguish it from broader personality and neurodevelopmental patterns, and use disorder-specific individual CBT as the preferred NICE first treatment, with an SSRI when CBT is declined and medication is preferred.

## Must-know board facts

- **NICE sequence:** offer **individual disorder-specific CBT first**; if CBT is declined and medication is preferred, offer an **SSRI** such as sertraline or escitalopram.
- **CBT mechanism/procedure:** reduce self-focused attention and safety behaviours, test feared social predictions through exposure/behavioural experiments, and use corrective feedback where appropriate.
- **DSM-5-TR:** uses a **performance-only specifier**; the old formal “generalized” subtype is obsolete.
- **Beta-blockers:** may help selected peripheral autonomic symptoms in performance situations but are not a core evidence-based treatment for the disorder; do not memorise a fixed dose as the main board fact.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Calling severe shyness a disorder without functional impairment.
- Confusing avoidant personality disorder or autism with social anxiety from one shared feature.
- Presenting CBT and medication as an undifferentiated first-line pair when using the NICE framework.
- Recommending beta-blockers as standard treatment for social anxiety disorder.

## Exam answer vs current practice

**Treatment hierarchy:** If giving a NICE answer, disorder-specific individual CBT comes first; medication is offered when CBT is declined and the patient prefers pharmacotherapy.

**Classification distinction:** DSM uses a performance-only specifier; ICD-11 does not use that specifier as its organising architecture.
