# Q73. How do you approach gender dysphoria or gender incongruence in an adult?

## Recall spine

1. **Gender diversity is not psychopathology**
2. **Clarify the person’s experience, distress and goals**
3. **Take a respectful biopsychosocial history**
4. **Assess comorbidity and risk on their own merits**
5. **Assess decision-making ability for proposed interventions**
6. **Support autonomy and appropriate specialist referral**

## Model oral answer

Gender diversity itself is **not a psychiatric disorder**. My role is not to diagnose mental illness from identity, expression or gender nonconformity, but to understand the person’s experience of gender incongruence or dysphoria, assess distress, safety and comorbidity where relevant, support informed autonomous decision-making and facilitate appropriate specialist gender-health care when requested.

I would begin with a respectful history using the person’s own language for identity, pronouns and goals. I would explore the nature and duration of gender incongruence or dysphoria, which aspects of the body or social role are distressing, what changes or supports the person is seeking, and how the experience affects relationships, work, daily functioning and quality of life.

The social context matters. I would ask about family and relationship support, discrimination, minority stress, housing or occupational difficulties and other barriers to care. Distress arising from rejection or stigma should not be misread as evidence that gender diversity is itself pathological.

Psychiatric assessment remains important but must be conducted **on its own merits**. Depression, anxiety, neurodevelopmental conditions, trauma-related symptoms, substance use and self-harm or suicide risk should be identified and treated when present, but they should not automatically be used to explain away or invalidate the person’s gender identity. Conversely, clinically significant mental illness should not be ignored simply because the consultation concerns gender.

For any proposed intervention, I would assess the person’s **decision-making ability and capacity in the usual clinically relevant way**, ensuring that the individual understands the nature, potential benefits, limitations and risks of the decision under consideration and is able to participate meaningfully in informed consent. The aim is to support autonomous decision-making rather than make the psychiatrist a gatekeeper who must “prove” identity.

Assessment and care should be **individualised rather than forced into one universal pathway**. Not every adult requires the same multidisciplinary assessments, social-transition steps or medical interventions. Fertility counselling is relevant before treatments that may impair fertility, but it is linked to the contemplated intervention rather than a mandatory step for every gender-diverse adult.

Physical-health considerations relevant to the person’s goals should be integrated with appropriate specialist input. Where gender-health assessment or intervention is sought, I would facilitate referral or support through an appropriate specialist pathway, while avoiding unsupported assumptions about a particular Greek service, reimbursement or referral structure.

The overall approach is respectful, non-pathologising and clinically rigorous: understand the person’s goals and distress, assess safety and comorbidity without causal assumptions, support informed decision-making and connect the person with appropriate gender-health care.

## Must-know board facts

- **ICD-11 moved Gender Incongruence of Adolescence and Adulthood out of mental disorders** and into Conditions related to sexual health.
- Gender diversity itself is **not psychopathology**.
- Psychiatric comorbidity is assessed and treated on its own merits; it does not automatically invalidate gender identity or treatment goals.
- Fertility counselling is relevant before interventions that may impair fertility, not as a universal requirement for every adult.
- There is **no verified uniform Greek national referral/reimbursement pathway** that should be invented for the viva.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Treating trans or gender-diverse identity as psychopathology.
- Assuming psychiatric comorbidity invalidates gender identity.
- Making the psychiatrist a gatekeeper who must “prove” identity.
- Presenting one universal multidisciplinary/social-transition pathway as mandatory for every adult.
- Inventing unverified Greek gender-service or referral rules.

## Exam answer vs current practice

ICD-10 placed gender-related diagnoses within mental disorders. **ICD-11 moved Gender Incongruence of Adolescence and Adulthood to Conditions related to sexual health**, reflecting the principle that gender diversity itself is not mental illness.
