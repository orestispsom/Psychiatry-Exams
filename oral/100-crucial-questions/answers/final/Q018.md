# Q18. When do you call schizophrenia treatment-resistant, and what must you exclude first?

## Recall spine

1. **Define the persistent target symptoms**
2. **Confirm 2 adequate antipsychotic trials**
3. **Check dose and duration**
4. **Verify adherence and exposure**
5. **Reassess diagnosis, substances, interactions and medical causes**
6. **Address psychosocial and environmental factors**
7. **If genuine TRS, move to clozapine**

## Model oral answer

I would call schizophrenia **treatment-resistant** when clinically significant schizophrenia symptoms persist despite at least **two adequate antipsychotic trials**, provided that apparent non-response is not better explained by pseudoresistance. The important part of the definition is therefore not just counting failed drugs; it is demonstrating that the patient had adequate treatment exposure for the right target symptoms.

First, I would clarify **what is actually persisting**. Persistent positive psychotic symptoms are different from primary negative symptoms, cognitive impairment, depressive or affective pathology, and other syndromes that may not be expected to respond in the same way to further antipsychotic treatment. I would also reassess the diagnosis itself before labelling the illness resistant.

Each previous antipsychotic trial must then be examined for **dose, duration and exposure**. A useful operational benchmark is at least **6 weeks at a therapeutic dose for each antipsychotic**. Two sequential failed adequate trials remain the mainstream threshold; one current guideline formulation specifies two different antipsychotics, with at least one being a non-clozapine second-generation antipsychotic.

The next major issue is **adherence**, because non-adherence is a major cause of pseudoresistance. I would assess it systematically rather than rely only on an assumption of exposure. Where feasible, plasma levels and/or a long-acting injectable or directly observed exposure can reduce uncertainty, but they are not universal regulatory prerequisites for diagnosing treatment resistance.

I would also review pharmacokinetic factors and interacting medication, ongoing **substance use**, and relevant medical illness. The apparent lack of efficacy may reflect inadequate drug exposure, continuing substances, an interaction, or a secondary clinical problem rather than genuine treatment resistance.

Appropriate **psychosocial treatment and environmental or functional factors** should also have been addressed. Persistent symptoms or disability in an inadequately supported patient should not automatically trigger a treatment-resistance label.

Once these checks have been made and there are persistent clinically significant symptoms despite two genuinely adequate trials, the patient has genuine treatment-resistant schizophrenia and should move to the **clozapine pathway**. Serial trials of additional non-clozapine antipsychotics should not be used simply to postpone clozapine once the treatment-resistant state is established.

The practical sequence is therefore: define the target symptoms, prove two adequate trials, exclude pseudoresistance systematically, and then act on the diagnosis rather than continuing indefinite antipsychotic cycling.

## Must-know board facts

- **Core threshold:** persistent clinically significant symptoms despite **at least 2 adequate antipsychotic trials**, after excluding pseudoresistance.
- **TRRIP operational duration benchmark:** **at least 6 weeks at a therapeutic dose for each antipsychotic**.
- The tighter research framing also incorporates persistent symptoms of at least moderate severity and functional impairment over the adequate trial period; it need not replace the practical clinical definition in the viva.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Calling intolerance to medication “treatment resistance” without clarifying whether an adequate efficacy trial occurred.
- Diagnosing TRS without checking adherence and exposure.
- Mistaking primary negative or cognitive symptoms for persistent positive psychosis.
- Cycling through multiple further non-clozapine antipsychotics after genuine TRS is established.
