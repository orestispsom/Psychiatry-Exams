# Q31. How do you diagnose and treat obsessive-compulsive disorder?

## Recall spine

1. **Define obsessions and compulsions by form**
2. **Assess insight and functional impact**
3. **Separate OCD from psychosis, rumination, OCPD and repetitive behaviour**
4. **Use ERP-based CBT**
5. **Use an SSRI with an adequately long trial**
6. **Optimise within licensed range before later-line strategies**
7. **Consider clomipramine or specialist augmentation if resistant**

## Model oral answer

Obsessive-compulsive disorder is defined by **obsessions, compulsions or both**, causing distress, time burden or functional impairment. The key diagnostic task is phenomenological: obsessions are intrusive, unwanted mental events, while compulsions are repetitive behaviours or mental acts used to reduce distress or prevent a feared outcome. Insight can vary considerably, so poor insight does not automatically turn OCD into a psychotic disorder.

**Obsessions** are recurrent intrusive thoughts, images or impulses or urges that the person experiences as unwanted and distressing, and typically tries to resist, neutralise or control to some degree. **Compulsions** are repetitive behaviours or mental acts carried out in response to obsessions or according to rigid rules, usually to reduce anxiety or prevent a feared consequence, even though they are excessive or not realistically connected to that consequence.

Common themes include contamination and cleaning, doubt and checking, symmetry or ordering, taboo or repugnant thoughts and mental rituals, but the diagnosis should not be reduced to a checklist of themes. The important question is the form and function of the symptom.

The differential includes **psychosis**, depressive rumination, generalized anxiety, obsessive-compulsive personality disorder, autism-related repetitive behaviour and tic-related phenomena. In OCD, even very strongly held beliefs may still fit the obsessional structure when they are congruent with the OCD syndrome and other psychotic features are absent. **OCPD** is different: it is an enduring personality pattern, not a disorder of intrusive obsessions and neutralising compulsions.

Current classification places OCD within **obsessive-compulsive and related disorders**, rather than among anxiety disorders.

Treatment has two major pillars. The core psychotherapy is **CBT with exposure and response prevention, ERP**. The patient is helped to confront feared cues while refraining from the ritual or neutralising response so that the maintaining cycle is weakened.

For pharmacotherapy, **SSRIs are first-line**. A proper OCD trial is often longer than a depression-style trial and may require up to **12 weeks**. If a standard dose has been ineffective but tolerated after about **4–6 weeks**, gradual dose optimisation toward the upper **licensed or SmPC range** may be considered. This should not be translated into an automatic rule to use supra-licensed “high-dose SSRIs.”

If adequate SSRI and ERP pathways fail, **clomipramine** remains an evidence-based later-line option, although its anticholinergic, cardiovascular and overdose burden is greater. **Antipsychotic augmentation** is a specialist strategy for resistant OCD after substantial failure of adequate SSRI and ERP treatment; antipsychotic monotherapy is not standard OCD treatment.

## Must-know board facts

- OCD is classified within **obsessive-compulsive and related disorders**.
- **ERP** is the core psychotherapy.
- An adequate SSRI trial may require up to **12 weeks**.

## Examiner follow-ups

### Q31a. How do you distinguish hoarding disorder from OCD?

Hoarding disorder is a **separate current OCD-related diagnosis**, not simply an OCD subtype. The core feature is persistent difficulty discarding possessions regardless of objective value, leading to accumulation and clutter that compromises living spaces and causes distress or impairment. Excessive acquisition is common but is not required.

Saving beliefs and urges may be more ego-syntonic and insight may be limited or absent. The differential includes ordinary collecting, realistic retention because of material deprivation or need, and clutter secondary to another condition.

If treatment is discussed, the high-yield approach is **specialised CBT** targeting discarding, acquisition, decision-making and avoidance. Standard OCD pharmacotherapy should not simply be assumed to have equivalent evidence for hoarding disorder.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Equating OCD with liking cleanliness, symmetry or order.
- Confusing obsession with delusion or OCPD personality traits.
- Declaring SSRI failure after a short depression-style trial.
- Teaching automatic supra-label “high-dose SSRI” prescribing.
