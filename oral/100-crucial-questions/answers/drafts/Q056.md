# Q56. How do you approach a liaison psychiatry consultation in a medically ill inpatient?

## Recall spine

1. **Clarify the referral question**
2. **Delirium, capacity, suicide and withdrawal**
3. **Illness- and medication-related psychiatric syndromes**
4. **Functional or somatic presentations**
5. **Biopsychosocial formulation in the medical context**
6. **Operational recommendations to the treating team**

## Model oral answer

A liaison psychiatry consultation should begin by clarifying **exactly what the medical or surgical team needs decided now**. The psychiatrist’s role is not to repeat a generic psychiatric history, but to identify the relevant syndrome, risk or decision problem within the medical context and give concise recommendations that the treating team can act on.

The first step is therefore the **referral question**. Common high-value problems include delirium, decision-making capacity and consent or refusal, self-harm or suicide risk, substance withdrawal, depression or anxiety, medication-induced psychiatric symptoms, and functional or somatic presentations. Different questions require different depth and urgency.

I would then review the **medical context** carefully: the underlying illness, current physiological disturbance, recent procedures or intensive-care exposure, pain, sleep disruption, medications and interactions, and alcohol or substance use. A new psychiatric symptom in a medically ill patient may be caused or amplified by the illness or its treatment, so medication and physiological review are central rather than peripheral.

Pre-existing psychiatric history still matters, but it has to be integrated with the current medical state. Collateral information may be needed to establish baseline cognition, behaviour, adherence or risk. The formulation should bring together the physical illness, treatment burden, psychiatric history, coping style, current behaviour and social context.

A common consultation is **capacity or refusal of treatment**. The assessment must be specific to the decision being made. In Greece, the UK Mental Capacity Act four-part statutory test should not be presented as Greek law. The clinician should distinguish the clinical assessment of decision-making ability from the legal validity of consent or refusal under the applicable Greek framework.

Risk assessment should be equally focused. If the referral concerns self-harm, suicide, agitation or treatment refusal, the answer should identify the immediate risk, the factors driving it and what the medical team should do next. Likewise, if delirium or withdrawal is suspected, this should alter both investigation and management rather than being treated as an incidental psychiatric label.

The value of the liaison consultation is in the **recommendations**. I would communicate directly with the treating team: what syndrome I think is present, what immediate risks matter, what investigations or medications may need adjustment, how staff should approach communication or behaviour, and what psychiatric or community follow-up is required. The recommendations should be practical and linked to the reason for referral.

Liaison psychiatry does not remove responsibility from the primary medical team. It adds psychiatric reasoning to the medical formulation and helps coordinate care across specialties.

The concise viva structure is therefore: define the referral problem, assess the medical and psychiatric contributors, address delirium/capacity/risk or withdrawal where relevant, formulate biopsychosocially, and give direct operational recommendations.

## Must-know board facts

- Clarifying the **referral question** is part of the consultation itself.
- Capacity in the medically ill inpatient is **decision-specific and jurisdiction-sensitive**.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Turning liaison assessment into generic psychiatric history-taking.
- Calling every distressed medically ill patient “adjustment disorder.”
- Missing delirium, medication effects or substance withdrawal.
- Importing UK capacity law into Greek practice.

## Exam answer vs current practice

**Greek legal context:** UK-derived capacity frameworks should not be presented as Greek statute. In a Greek viva answer, distinguish clinical assessment of decision-making ability from legal validity of consent or refusal under Greek law.
