# Q54. What psychiatric problems are associated with epilepsy, and how do you approach them?

## Recall spine

1. **Ictal, peri-ictal and interictal timing**
2. **Depression, anxiety and suicide risk**
3. **Postictal psychosis with a lucid interval**
4. **Interictal psychosis**
5. **Antiseizure-medication psychiatric effects**
6. **Psychotropic seizure-threshold and interaction issues**
7. **Functional/dissociative seizures differential**

## Model oral answer

Psychiatric phenomena in epilepsy are best organised by their **temporal relationship to seizures**: ictal, peri-ictal or postictal, and interictal. I would then assess common psychiatric comorbidity, medication effects and psychotropic interactions, while keeping functional or dissociative seizures in the differential when the events themselves are uncertain.

**Ictal psychiatric phenomena** can include sudden fear, experiential symptoms, autonomic symptoms and hallucination-like experiences, particularly with temporal-lobe seizures. The main diagnostic clue is the stereotyped relationship to seizure activity rather than treating an isolated unusual experience as proof of a primary psychiatric disorder.

In the **postictal period**, confusion and agitation are common neurobehavioural states, and postictal psychosis is an important specific syndrome. A **lucid interval** after seizure activity is characteristic, but it should not be taught as a rigid 24–48-hour rule. Contemporary descriptions allow a lucid period ranging from hours to several days, with psychosis typically emerging within a week after seizure clusters.

Interictally, patients may develop **depression, anxiety or chronic psychotic syndromes**. Depression and suicide risk deserve active assessment rather than being regarded as understandable but clinically secondary consequences of epilepsy. Management should consider the psychiatric syndrome on its own merits while integrating seizure control and treatment interactions.

Medication review is essential. **Levetiracetam** can cause clinically significant irritability, aggression, depression, agitation and psychotic symptoms. Conversely, psychotropics can affect seizure threshold. **Clozapine lowers seizure threshold in a dose-related way**, and **bupropion is contraindicated in people with a current or past seizure disorder**. SSRIs are generally among the preferred antidepressants when antidepressant treatment is required. Enzyme-inducing antiseizure medications can also reduce psychotropic exposure, so interaction review is part of routine prescribing.

When the diagnosis of the events themselves is uncertain, **functional or dissociative seizures** must be considered. These episodes are not intentionally produced. Diagnosis is clinical and may be supported by video-EEG recording of typical events when necessary. The goal is to distinguish the mechanisms accurately rather than to frame the symptoms as deception.

Management is therefore collaborative between neurology and psychiatry: optimise seizure control, identify psychiatric comorbidity, review antiseizure-medication adverse effects, choose psychotropics with seizure and interaction issues in mind, and address the functional and psychosocial consequences of the illness.

The historical **Gastaut–Geschwind syndrome** should not be presented as an established personality syndrome. At most, it is historical and contested material rather than a modern diagnostic framework.

## Must-know board facts

- **Postictal psychosis:** a lucid interval is characteristic, but not rigidly 24–48 hours.
- **Levetiracetam** can produce important psychiatric adverse effects.
- **Clozapine** lowers seizure threshold in a dose-related manner; **bupropion is contraindicated** in seizure disorder.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Treating the postictal lucid interval as a fixed 24–48-hour rule.
- Assuming functional/dissociative seizures are voluntary.
- Presenting Gastaut–Geschwind syndrome as an established personality disorder.
- Ignoring depression and suicide risk in epilepsy.

## Exam answer vs current practice

**Terminology and timing:** Older teaching may rigidly specify a 24–48-hour lucid interval and use “PNES” as the dominant term. A current answer should describe the lucid interval as hours to days and use **functional/dissociative seizures** without implying intentional production.
