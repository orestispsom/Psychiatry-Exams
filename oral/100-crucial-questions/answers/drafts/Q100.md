# Q100. What are the principles of community care for adults with severe mental illness?

## Recall spine

1. **Multidisciplinary community care**
2. **Continuity and coordinated case management**
3. **Crisis care and early intervention**
4. **Rehabilitation, housing and employment**
5. **Family, physical health and substance-use care**
6. **Recovery, inclusion and least restriction**
7. **Current Greek network architecture**

## Model oral answer

Community psychiatry aims to provide **continuous, coordinated, multidisciplinary care close to the patient’s ordinary life**, with the least restrictive level of intervention compatible with safety and need. For adults with severe mental illness, the priorities are not only symptom control but also relapse prevention, rehabilitation, physical health, social inclusion and recovery.

The first principle is **continuity of care**. Patients with severe and recurrent illness often move between acute services, outpatient care, primary care and social services, so there must be clear coordination and responsibility across transitions. Case-management functions are important where needs are complex, especially when several services are involved.

A second principle is the ability to respond to deterioration **before hospital admission becomes the only option**. Community crisis intervention and home treatment should be used where feasible and safe, while early-intervention approaches aim to engage patients promptly and reduce avoidable delay in treatment. Hospital care remains necessary when community management cannot safely meet the patient’s needs, but it should not be the default organising centre of long-term care.

Community treatment must also address **function and participation**. Rehabilitation, supported housing and supported employment help patients maintain or regain ordinary social roles. Recovery orientation means working with the patient’s own goals, strengths and preferences rather than defining success only as symptom reduction.

Family or carer work is important when appropriate, as are **physical-health care and substance-use treatment**. Severe mental illness should not result in fragmented care in which psychiatric symptoms are treated while cardiovascular, metabolic, substance-related or primary-care needs are neglected. Coordination with primary care and social services is therefore a core part of the model.

The wider service philosophy should emphasise **least restrictive care, social inclusion and relapse prevention**. That includes helping the patient recognise early warning signs, maintain treatment engagement and access practical supports before crises become severe.

In Greece, the current top-level architecture has changed. Since **Law 5129/2024**, services are organised within the **Εθνικό Δίκτυο Υπηρεσιών Ψυχικής Υγείας (Ε.Δ.Υ.Ψ.Υ.)**, structured into **seven Πε.Δ.Υ.Ψ.Υ.** under the corresponding Υ.Πε. Community Mental Health Centres, Mobile Mental Health Units and early-intervention and other community services continue within this network. The important point is to know the current organising framework without implying that every older sector or ΤοΨΥ term has disappeared from all local or regulatory use.

So the oral answer should combine the universal principles—continuity, multidisciplinary care, crisis response, rehabilitation, recovery, inclusion and integration—with the current Greek service architecture rather than giving only an old sector-based or imported UK service model.

## Exam answer vs current practice

Older answers may be organised around legacy sector terminology or UK-style CMHT/AOT/CRHT structures. The current Greek answer should retain the universal principles of community psychiatry while recognising the **Ε.Δ.Υ.Ψ.Υ. → seven Πε.Δ.Υ.Ψ.Υ.** architecture and the continuing role of ΚΨΥ, Mobile Mental Health Units and other community services.
