# Q26. How do you manage bipolar disorder long term?

## Recall spine

1. **Shared relapse-prevention and recovery plan**
2. **Lithium as a central maintenance option**
3. **Choose alternatives by polarity and previous response**
4. **Psychoeducation, adherence, sleep and substances**
5. **Physical-health monitoring**
6. **Suicide prevention and functional recovery**
7. **Individualise duration and withdraw gradually if stopping**
8. **Apply valproate reproductive restrictions**

## Model oral answer

Long-term management of bipolar disorder should be a **shared relapse-prevention and recovery plan**, not simply continuation of whatever controlled the last episode. I would review the pattern and polarity of previous episodes, individual relapse signatures and triggers, adherence, sleep and circadian regularity, substance use, suicide risk, social and occupational recovery, and family or carer needs.

Bipolar disorder is generally recurrent, so maintenance treatment is commonly long term and may be indefinite after recurrent or severe episodes. There is no single universal stopping date. The choice of maintenance treatment should be guided by previous response, predominant relapse polarity, tolerability, comorbidity and patient preference.

**Lithium** remains a central first-line maintenance treatment and is often the reference treatment for long-term bipolar disorder. It is particularly valuable when it has previously been effective and is suitable for the individual patient. Other established maintenance options include **quetiapine, lamotrigine, valproate/divalproex and selected antipsychotics**, with the choice determined by previous response and the polarity of relapses rather than assuming that every agent prevents mania and depression equally well.

Phase specificity is important. **Lamotrigine is useful for prevention of depressive relapse but is not an acute antimanic drug.** More broadly, the maintenance plan should reflect whether previous illness has been predominantly manic, depressive or mixed, and whether the acute treatment is also appropriate and tolerable as a long-term strategy.

Medication is only one part of maintenance care. **Psychoeducation, adherence work, recognition of early warning signs, family or CBT-informed interventions where appropriate, regular sleep and daily routine, and substance-use management** all contribute to relapse prevention. Long-term management should also address functioning and personal recovery goals rather than measuring success only by absence of hospitalisation.

Physical health requires active attention, including weight and metabolic or cardiovascular risk and the adverse effects specific to the treatment being used. Lithium also has evidence suggesting a reduction in suicidal behaviour and deaths, which is an additional reason to favour it in suitable patients, although it does not eliminate suicide risk.

If discontinuation is considered, the decision should be individualised according to the number and severity of previous episodes, psychosis, consequences of relapse, residual symptoms, previous relapse pattern, adverse effects and patient preference. Withdrawal should be **gradual**, with an explicit relapse-monitoring plan.

Whenever **valproate** is considered for maintenance, the current reproductive restrictions remain essential. It must not be used for bipolar disorder during pregnancy. In girls and women able to become pregnant, it should not be used unless Pregnancy Prevention Programme conditions are met and alternatives are ineffective or not tolerated. In men, current European precautions require specialist counselling about a potential paternal-exposure neurodevelopmental signal, effective contraception for the man and female partner during treatment and for at least **3 months after stopping**, and no sperm donation during that period.

## Must-know board facts

- **Lithium:** a central first-line maintenance treatment for bipolar disorder.
- **Lamotrigine:** useful for prevention of depressive relapse, but not an acute antimanic treatment.
- **Long-term care:** maintenance is not medication-only; relapse prevention, adherence, psychoeducation, physical health and functional recovery are core components.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Simply continuing the acute drug indefinitely without considering polarity or previous response.
- Forgetting psychoeducation, adherence and physical health.
- Turning this answer into a detailed lithium-monitoring schedule.
- Listing valproate without the current reproductive restrictions.

## Exam answer vs current practice

**Valproate:** Older maintenance answers may list valproate without qualification. Current European practice requires the reproductive restrictions to be stated whenever valproate is materially considered.
