# Q92. What is interpersonal psychotherapy, and when is it useful?

## Recall spine

1. **Structured and time-limited**
2. **Symptoms linked to current interpersonal context**
3. **Grief/bereavement**
4. **Role transitions**
5. **Role disputes**
6. **Interpersonal difficulties**
7. **Depression is the core indication**

## Model oral answer

Interpersonal psychotherapy, or **IPT**, is a structured, time-limited and manualised psychotherapy that links psychiatric symptoms to the patient’s **current interpersonal context**. Its central board-level indication is depression. The treatment focuses less on reconstructing the whole personality or developmental history and more on identifying the interpersonal problem area most closely related to the current episode and working on it directly.

The classic IPT problem areas are **grief or bereavement, role transitions, role disputes and interpersonal difficulties or deficits**. In grief, treatment focuses on the relationship to the loss and adaptation to life after it. In role transitions, the work centres on major changes in social or personal roles and the demands associated with them. Role disputes involve important relationships in which expectations are mismatched or conflict is unresolved. Interpersonal difficulties refer to persistent problems establishing or maintaining satisfying relationships.

The therapist first develops an interpersonal formulation that connects the patient’s symptoms with one or more of these current interpersonal domains. Treatment then works actively on communication, role expectations, emotional expression, problem-solving and use of social supports as relevant to the chosen focus. The structure is deliberately focused and time-limited rather than open-ended.

For a viva answer, **depression should remain the main indication**. IPT is also a recognised option in **perinatal depression**, which is a useful contemporary extension of the core answer. It should not be described as a universal psychotherapy for every psychiatric disorder.

It is also important to distinguish standard IPT from related adaptations. **Interpersonal and social rhythm therapy, IPSRT, is a bipolar adaptation** that incorporates interpersonal work with attention to social rhythms; it should not be presented as identical to plain IPT. Likewise, although interpersonal approaches may have relevance in other clinical areas, IPT should not be claimed to displace disorder-specific first-line psychotherapies in eating disorders.

The concise answer is therefore that IPT is a focused psychotherapy for psychiatric symptoms—especially depression—organised around the interaction between the current episode and the patient’s important interpersonal relationships and role changes.

## Must-know board facts

- The four classic IPT problem areas are **grief/bereavement, role transitions, role disputes and interpersonal difficulties/deficits**.
- **Depression** is the central board-level indication.
- IPT is also a recognised option in **perinatal depression**.
- **IPSRT** is a bipolar adaptation and is not simply another name for IPT.
