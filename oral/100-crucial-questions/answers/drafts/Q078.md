# Q78. What metabolic, endocrine and cardiovascular adverse effects of antipsychotics should you monitor?

## Recall spine

1. **Weight and waist**
2. **Blood pressure and pulse**
3. **Glucose/HbA1c and lipids**
4. **Prolactin and sexual/endocrine effects**
5. **ECG and QT risk when indicated**
6. **Clozapine myocarditis vigilance**

## Model oral answer

Antipsychotic monitoring should focus on **metabolic disease, prolactin-related effects and cardiovascular risk**, while remaining drug- and patient-specific. I would obtain an appropriate baseline physical-health profile, repeat metabolic monitoring during treatment, and add ECG or specialist investigations when the drug, setting or individual risk makes them necessary. A baseline ECG is not a universal requirement for every antipsychotic outpatient.

The metabolic burden includes **weight gain, central adiposity, hyperglycaemia or diabetes and dyslipidaemia**. Baseline monitoring in schizophrenia should include weight, waist circumference, blood pressure and pulse, glucose or HbA1c and lipids. These measures need repeated follow-up, although the exact schedule varies by guideline, indication and clinical risk rather than being one universal timetable for all patients.

Weight alone is not sufficient. A patient may develop worsening glycaemic or lipid risk even when the change in weight is not dramatic, so the monitoring package should be interpreted as a whole and in the context of baseline cardiovascular risk.

**Hyperprolactinaemia** can produce clinically important sexual, reproductive and endocrine consequences. Prolactin is therefore part of the baseline monitoring framework, but interpretation should be linked to symptoms, the specific antipsychotic and the broader clinical context rather than treated as an isolated laboratory abnormality.

Cardiovascular assessment includes blood pressure, pulse and **QT risk**. ECG use should be guided by the product information, clinical setting, co-prescribed QT-prolonging drugs and individual cardiac risk. A **QTc above 500 ms** is a major high-risk finding and should prompt urgent reassessment of QT-prolonging drugs, electrolyte abnormalities and cardiac risk rather than simple continuation without review.

Clozapine requires additional vigilance. **Myocarditis is an important early treatment risk**, so unexplained tachycardia or other concerning clinical change should trigger prompt assessment. Specialist services may use structured troponin and CRP surveillance protocols, but these should not be described as a universally legislated mandatory schedule in every jurisdiction or service.

The practical viva answer is therefore to establish baseline cardiometabolic and endocrine risk, repeat metabolic monitoring, ask actively about adverse effects, use ECG according to risk rather than by rote, and maintain specific early vigilance for clozapine myocarditis.

## Must-know board facts

- Baseline monitoring includes **weight, waist, BP/pulse, glucose or HbA1c, lipids and prolactin**.
- A baseline ECG is **risk-, setting- and product-information dependent**, not universally mandatory for every outpatient.
- **QTc >500 ms** is a major high-risk threshold requiring urgent reassessment.
- Clozapine myocarditis is a high-stakes early risk; troponin/CRP surveillance protocols are **specialist protocols, not universally legislated schedules**.
