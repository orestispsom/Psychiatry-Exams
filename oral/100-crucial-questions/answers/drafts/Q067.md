# Q67. What are the main sleep stages and their EEG characteristics?

## Recall spine

1. **N1**
2. **N2**
3. **N3**
4. **REM**
5. **NREM–REM cycling**

## Model oral answer

Sleep is organised into **NREM stages N1, N2 and N3, followed by REM sleep**, with repeated NREM–REM cycles across the night.

- **N1:** light transitional sleep with predominantly **theta activity**.
- **N2:** characterised by **sleep spindles and K-complexes**.
- **N3:** slow-wave sleep with high-amplitude **delta or slow-wave activity**.
- **REM:** a **low-voltage mixed-frequency EEG**, rapid eye movements and skeletal-muscle atonia apart from expected exceptions.

For an oral exam, those are the high-yield identifying features; unnecessary numerical staging detail adds little unless specifically requested.

## Examiner follow-up

### Q67a. What sleep-architecture changes are classically associated with depression?

Classic associations are **shortened REM latency, increased REM density and reduced slow-wave sleep**. These are useful research and traditional exam findings, but they are **not diagnostic tests for depression**, and an exact REM-latency cut-off should not be treated as a universal rule.
