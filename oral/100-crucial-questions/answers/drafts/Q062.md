# Q62. How do you diagnose and treat anorexia nervosa?

## Recall spine

1. **Restriction with significantly low weight**
2. **Persistent prevention of weight restoration / fear of weight gain**
3. **Weight and shape psychopathology**
4. **Assess medical and psychiatric risk**
5. **Nutritional rehabilitation and weight restoration**
6. **Eating-disorder-focused psychotherapy**
7. **Medication has a limited adjunctive role**

## Model oral answer

Anorexia nervosa is diagnosed from the **combination of significantly low weight, persistent restriction or behaviours that prevent weight restoration, and characteristic weight/shape psychopathology**. I would not use BMI below 17.5 or amenorrhoea as universal current diagnostic requirements. Management combines medical risk assessment, nutritional rehabilitation and weight restoration, eating-disorder-focused psychotherapy and ongoing physical monitoring.

I would assess the eating-disorder syndrome directly: the extent and duration of dietary restriction, behaviours that prevent weight gain, fear of gaining weight, and disturbance or overvaluation of weight and shape. The person’s weight must be interpreted in clinical context rather than reduced to a single numerical threshold.

I would also establish the **trajectory and severity**. Important questions include rate of weight loss, current intake, exercise, bingeing or purging, vomiting or other compensatory behaviours, and whether the person can maintain hydration and nutrition. Physical and psychiatric risk should be assessed together, including functional decline, self-harm and suicide risk, and relevant comorbidity.

The physical assessment should be proportionate to severity and include vital signs, hydration and targeted investigations such as ECG and electrolytes when indicated. A patient can be medically high risk because of rapid loss, physiological instability or biochemical disturbance even if one isolated weight measure appears less extreme.

Treatment has three linked components. First is **nutritional rehabilitation and weight restoration**, with medical monitoring appropriate to risk. Second is a specialist eating-disorder psychotherapy. In adults, evidence-based options include **CBT-ED, MANTRA and SSCM**. Third is management of comorbid psychiatric and physical problems and the social or functional consequences of illness.

Medication should not be presented as a stand-alone treatment for the core disorder. It may be used for clearly diagnosed comorbidity where appropriate, but it does not replace nutritional rehabilitation or eating-disorder-focused psychological treatment.

The overall approach is therefore to recognise the modern diagnostic architecture without obsolete amenorrhoea or fixed-BMI requirements, assess medical and psychiatric risk carefully, and treat with coordinated nutritional, psychological and physical care.

## Examiner follow-up

### Q62a. How do you distinguish ARFID from anorexia nervosa?

In **avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder**, restriction may be driven by **sensory sensitivity, fear of aversive consequences, or low interest or appetite**. The consequences can include low weight or weight loss, nutritional deficiency, dependence on supplements or enteral feeding, or marked psychosocial interference.

The crucial distinction is that ARFID is **not driven by weight/shape overvaluation or fear of fatness**. Adult treatment evidence is less mature, so management should be multidisciplinary, correcting medical and nutritional consequences and using behavioural or CBT-style interventions targeted to the maintaining mechanism rather than assuming one universal branded psychotherapy.

## Must-know board facts

- **Amenorrhoea is not required** for anorexia nervosa.
- There is **no universal fixed BMI diagnostic cut-off**.
- ARFID is distinguished by the **absence of weight/shape psychopathology as the driver of restriction**.

## Exam answer vs current practice

Older ICD-10 teaching emphasised BMI thresholds and amenorrhoea. Current diagnosis should not depend on either as a universal requirement.
