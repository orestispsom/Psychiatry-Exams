# Q94. Which frontal-subcortical and limbic circuits are most relevant to psychiatry?

## Recall spine

1. **DLPFC/frontostriatal = executive function**
2. **Orbitofrontal/ventromedial = judgement and inhibition**
3. **Anterior cingulate/medial frontal = initiation and motivation**
4. **Amygdala = salience and threat**
5. **Hippocampus = episodic/contextual memory**
6. **Ventral striatum = reward and reinforcement**

## Model oral answer

The most useful psychiatric neuroanatomy is to think in **functional circuits rather than isolated brain regions**. Frontal-subcortical circuits help organise executive control, behaviour and motivation, while limbic and reward systems contribute to emotional salience, memory and reinforcement. These are high-yield associations, not deterministic one-region/one-symptom maps.

The **dorsolateral prefrontal/frontostriatal circuit** is especially important for executive function: working memory, planning, organisation, set-shifting and cognitive flexibility. Dysfunction here produces a dysexecutive picture, with difficulty organising behaviour, maintaining goals and adapting strategies.

The **orbitofrontal and ventromedial prefrontal systems** are more closely related to social judgement, evaluation of reward and punishment, and impulse control. Dysfunction can therefore produce disinhibition, poor judgement and behaviour that is inadequately modified by consequences.

The **anterior cingulate and medial frontal systems** are important for initiation, motivation, effort allocation and action selection. Dysfunction is associated with apathy or, at the more severe end, abulia rather than primarily a disorder of motor strength.

Among limbic structures, the **amygdala** is central to affective salience, threat and fear learning, and broader emotional processing. The **hippocampus** is particularly important for episodic and declarative memory and for representing contextual information.

The **ventral striatum, including the nucleus accumbens**, is important in reward, incentive salience and reinforcement. It is therefore relevant to psychiatric models involving motivation, reward learning and maladaptive reinforcement.

The practical viva point is to connect each circuit to a characteristic domain: dorsolateral systems to executive control, orbitofrontal systems to inhibition and social judgement, medial frontal/anterior cingulate systems to initiation, the amygdala to emotional salience and threat, the hippocampus to episodic/contextual memory, and the ventral striatum to reward and reinforcement.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Treating these associations as exact one-region/one-symptom localisation.
- Ignoring that psychiatric functions arise from interacting circuits rather than isolated structures.
