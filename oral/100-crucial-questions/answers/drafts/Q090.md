# Q90. What is CBT and how is it used in psychiatry?

## Recall spine

1. **Structured and collaborative**
2. **Formulation links situation, appraisal, emotion and behaviour**
3. **Shared goals and collaborative empiricism**
4. **Behavioural and cognitive techniques**
5. **Between-session practice**
6. **Disorder-specific application**
7. **Relapse prevention**

## Model oral answer

CBT is a **structured, collaborative, formulation-based and goal-oriented psychotherapy**. It links situations and appraisals with emotional, physiological and behavioural responses, then uses that formulation to identify maintaining processes that can be tested and changed. The therapist and patient work through collaborative empiricism rather than the therapist simply telling the patient what to think.

A typical CBT process begins with a shared understanding of the current problems and the mechanisms maintaining them. Treatment goals are agreed explicitly, and sessions are organised around those goals. The formulation may show, for example, how a threatening interpretation increases anxiety, bodily arousal and avoidance, or how withdrawal and inactivity maintain depression. The point of the formulation is practical: it guides which interventions are likely to be useful for this particular patient and problem.

Core techniques include **behavioural activation, exposure, cognitive restructuring and behavioural experiments**. These are not interchangeable techniques applied mechanically. Behavioural activation is especially useful where reduced activity and reinforcement are maintaining low mood; exposure targets fear and avoidance; cognitive restructuring examines the evidence and meaning attached to appraisals; and behavioural experiments test predictions in real situations. Technique choice should therefore follow the formulation and the disorder being treated.

CBT also relies on **between-session practice**, often called homework, because change is expected to generalise beyond the therapy hour. The patient may monitor patterns, practise new behaviours, test predictions or rehearse coping strategies between sessions. Progress is reviewed collaboratively and the formulation is revised if the expected change does not occur.

In psychiatry, CBT has established applications across several major disorders, including **depression, anxiety disorders, OCD and psychosis**, when clinically appropriate. The form of CBT differs across these problems: treatment should remain disorder- and formulation-specific rather than becoming a generic set of cognitive techniques.

The later phase should consolidate learning and include **relapse prevention**. This means identifying personal warning signs, maintaining strategies that have worked, anticipating high-risk situations and planning how the patient will respond if difficulties recur.

Contemporary CBT also includes evidence-based extensions and delivery formats. **Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy** is a legitimate development, particularly for relapse prevention in recurrent or higher-relapse-risk depression. Supported digital CBT and guided self-help can also be effective in selected disorders and settings, but they should not be assumed to be equivalent to full therapist-delivered CBT for every patient.

## Must-know board facts

- CBT is **collaborative, formulation-based, structured and goal-oriented**.
- Core methods include **behavioural activation, exposure, cognitive restructuring and behavioural experiments**.
- **MBCT** is a recognised contemporary extension, especially for relapse prevention in recurrent or higher-relapse-risk depression.
- Supported digital CBT can be evidence-based in selected settings, but is not automatically equivalent to full therapist-delivered CBT.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Turning CBT into a list of techniques without formulation or collaboration.
- Describing cognitive restructuring as simply replacing “negative thoughts” with positive thoughts.
- Turning the answer into a broad review of so-called third-wave therapies instead of answering what CBT is and how it is used.
