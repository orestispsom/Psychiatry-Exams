# Q35. How do you distinguish somatic symptom disorder from illness anxiety disorder?

## Recall spine

1. **SSD: distressing symptoms plus excessive response**
2. **IAD: illness preoccupation with absent or mild symptoms**
3. **Do not require symptoms to be medically unexplained**
4. **Medical disease can coexist**
5. **Keep DSM and ICD categories distinct**
6. **Use continuity, proportionate investigation and collaborative formulation**

## Model oral answer

The main distinction is **what dominates the clinical picture**. In DSM-5-TR somatic symptom disorder, the patient has one or more distressing or disruptive bodily symptoms together with excessive or disproportionate thoughts, anxiety or behaviour related to those symptoms. In illness anxiety disorder, the dominant problem is preoccupation with having or acquiring a serious illness while bodily symptoms are absent or only mild.

A crucial modern point is that **somatic symptom disorder does not require medically unexplained symptoms**. The patient may have genuine medical disease; the diagnosis concerns the excessive cognitive, emotional or behavioural response to the symptom burden and its impact. Negative investigations alone therefore do not establish the diagnosis, and finding a somatic-symptom disorder does not remove the need to investigate new or changing medical red flags appropriately.

In **illness anxiety disorder**, health anxiety and illness preoccupation are central. The patient may repeatedly check the body, seek reassurance or medical review, or at the other extreme avoid medical settings because of fear. The bodily symptom load itself is absent or relatively mild. If substantial distressing physical symptoms are the centre of the presentation, somatic symptom disorder is usually the more fitting DSM formulation than illness anxiety disorder.

The current **ICD-11 architecture is not a one-to-one translation of the DSM categories**. ICD-11 bodily distress disorder combines distressing bodily symptoms with excessive attention or preoccupation and associated impairment, again without requiring that the symptoms lack a medical explanation. ICD-11 hypochondriasis or health anxiety is placed primarily within obsessive-compulsive and related disorders and is cross-listed with anxiety or fear-related disorders, rather than being grouped with bodily distress disorder.

Management should avoid both dismissal and endless reassurance. I would **validate the symptoms and suffering without reinforcing a catastrophic interpretation**, establish continuity with a consistent clinician or team, and use planned rather than crisis-driven follow-up. Investigations should be proportionate to the clinical picture rather than repeatedly duplicated solely to reduce anxiety temporarily.

A collaborative **biopsychosocial formulation** should address the bodily symptoms, illness beliefs and attention, anxiety, behaviour, medical and psychiatric comorbidity, and the functional consequences. Genuine medical disorders should be treated appropriately, while new or changing red flags still trigger reassessment.

The practical distinction is therefore symptom-centred distress with an excessive response in SSD versus illness-centred preoccupation with minimal bodily symptoms in IAD, while remembering that neither modern framework requires the clinician to prove that symptoms are medically unexplained.

## Must-know board facts

- “**Medically unexplained**” is **not required** for DSM somatic symptom disorder or ICD-11 bodily distress disorder.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Diagnosing somatic symptom disorder because tests are negative.
- Dismissing genuine medical illness after making a somatic diagnosis.
- Repeating reassurance and investigations without a planned longitudinal approach.

## Exam answer vs current practice

**Classification distinction:** DSM somatic symptom disorder/illness anxiety disorder and ICD-11 bodily distress disorder/hypochondriasis overlap conceptually but are not direct equivalents.
