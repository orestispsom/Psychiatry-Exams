# Q70. How do you distinguish antisocial personality disorder from psychopathy?

## Recall spine

1. **Separate formal diagnosis from forensic construct**
2. **ASPD is behaviourally weighted**
3. **Psychopathy adds affective/interpersonal traits**
4. **Take developmental and substance history**
5. **Assess violence, exploitation and broader risk**
6. **Do not equate criminality with psychopathy**

## Model oral answer

Antisocial personality disorder and psychopathy overlap, but they are **not synonymous**. ASPD is a formal DSM diagnosis with substantial emphasis on persistent antisocial behaviour and disregard for others and social rules. **Psychopathy is not an independent DSM-5-TR or ICD-11 diagnosis**; it is a personality and forensic construct that places greater weight on affective and interpersonal traits in addition to antisocial behaviour.

In ASPD, I would expect a persistent pattern that may include deceitfulness, impulsivity, aggression, irresponsibility and disregard for the rights or safety of others, considered in the context of a relevant developmental antisocial history. The diagnosis should not be reduced to the mere presence of criminal behaviour, because offending can arise from many other pathways including substance use, social context, impulsivity or other psychiatric disorders.

Psychopathy is conceptually narrower and more trait-focused. In addition to an antisocial lifestyle, it emphasises features such as **superficial charm, manipulativeness, callousness, shallow affect and lack of empathy or remorse**. Someone may therefore meet criteria for ASPD without displaying the full affective-interpersonal psychopathy construct.

Current ICD-11 does not create a separate psychopathy diagnosis. Antisocial or dissocial phenotypes are represented dimensionally within personality disorder, particularly through the relevant severity and trait pattern. That is another reason not to present “psychopathy” as a routine diagnostic label in general psychiatry.

Assessment should include developmental history, relationships, education and employment, substance use, prior aggression and offending, impulsivity, capacity for empathy and remorse, patterns of exploitation and current risk. Risk formulation should be specific: violence, coercion, exploitation, recidivism-related factors, substance-related disinhibition and protective factors should be considered rather than assuming a diagnosis predicts behaviour automatically.

The **PCL-R** can be mentioned as a specialist forensic assessment tool for psychopathic traits, but it is not a routine general-psychiatry diagnostic test and should not be used casually outside an appropriate forensic context.

The practical distinction is therefore that ASPD is a formal diagnosis with a strong behavioural component, whereas psychopathy is a forensic/personality construct adding a characteristic affective-interpersonal style. Neither term should be used as a synonym for criminality, and both require careful developmental, substance-use and risk assessment.
