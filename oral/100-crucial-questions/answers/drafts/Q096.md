# Q96. What do heritability, familial risk and polygenic risk mean in psychiatry?

## Recall spine

1. **Heritability is a population statistic**
2. **Not individual genetic causation**
3. **Familial aggregation = genes + shared/developmental environment**
4. **Most disorders are highly polygenic**
5. **Rare variants matter in selected cases**
6. **Genetic overlap crosses diagnoses**
7. **PRS are not routine stand-alone tests**

## Model oral answer

In psychiatry, **heritability, familial risk and polygenic risk describe different aspects of genetic liability and should not be confused with individual destiny**. Heritability is a population-level estimate of how much variation in a trait, under particular environmental conditions, is associated with genetic variation. It does not mean that a fixed percentage of one person’s illness is “genetic.”

**Familial aggregation** means that a disorder occurs more often among relatives than would be expected by chance. That pattern can reflect shared genetic liability, but also shared or developmentally correlated environmental influences. Family history therefore informs risk, but it is not a direct readout of genotype and does not determine outcome.

Most major psychiatric disorders are **highly polygenic**. This means liability is distributed across many common genetic variants, each usually contributing only a very small effect. The clinically important consequence is that there is rarely one common variant that explains an ordinary adult psychiatric disorder in an individual patient.

There are also **rare variants and copy-number variants** that may have larger effects in selected presentations. These can be clinically important in particular patients, but they account for only a minority of ordinary adult psychiatric illness and should not be used to imply that most psychiatric disorders have a single identifiable genetic cause.

Another important concept is **pleiotropy and genetic overlap**. Some genetic liabilities are shared across conventional diagnostic boundaries, which is consistent with the observation that psychiatric diagnoses do not map neatly onto completely separate biological architectures.

Polygenic risk scores combine information from many variants into a statistical estimate of genetic liability. They are useful research tools and may contribute to future stratification approaches, but **they are not routine stand-alone diagnostic or prognostic tests in ordinary psychiatric practice**. A high score does not establish a diagnosis, and a low score does not exclude one.

The viva conclusion is that psychiatric genetics is probabilistic: heritability describes population variation, family history reflects mixed inherited and environmental influences, most common disorders are polygenic, some rare variants have larger effects, and none of these concepts should be translated into deterministic predictions for an individual patient.

## Must-know board facts

- **Heritability is a population statistic**, not the percentage of an individual illness caused by genes.
- Major psychiatric disorders are generally **polygenic**.
- **PRS are not routine stand-alone diagnostic or prognostic tests** in ordinary psychiatric practice.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Treating high heritability as individual genetic destiny.
- Treating familial aggregation as proof of purely genetic causation.
- Presenting a polygenic risk score as diagnostic by itself.
