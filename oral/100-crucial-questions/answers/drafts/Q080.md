# Q80. What antidepressant adverse effects and interactions matter, and how do you switch or discontinue antidepressants safely?

## Recall spine

1. **Serotonin toxicity**
2. **Hyponatraemia and sexual dysfunction**
3. **Bleeding, blood pressure and QT risk**
4. **Withdrawal liability**
5. **Individualised taper**
6. **Direct switch, cross-taper or washout**
7. **MAOI washout rules**

## Model oral answer

Antidepressant safety requires attention to both **adverse effects and transitions between drugs**. I would think about serotonergic toxicity, hyponatraemia, sexual dysfunction, bleeding, blood-pressure or QT effects where relevant, then plan discontinuation and switching according to the specific drug pair rather than using one universal taper or cross-taper rule. MAOI transitions require particular caution.

Important adverse effects vary by class and patient vulnerability. **Serotonin syndrome** becomes especially important when serotonergic drugs are combined or switched unsafely. SSRIs and SNRIs can also contribute to hyponatraemia, sexual dysfunction and increased bleeding risk, particularly when other bleeding-risk medicines are present. Depending on the drug and patient, blood pressure or QT effects may also influence treatment choice and monitoring.

Discontinuation symptoms should not be dismissed as trivial or necessarily brief. They can be prolonged in some patients. **Paroxetine and venlafaxine have relatively high withdrawal liability**, whereas fluoxetine tends to have lower liability because of its long half-life. Tapering should therefore be individualised according to the drug, dose, duration of use, previous withdrawal experience and emerging symptoms rather than forcing every patient through a fixed four-week schedule.

When changing antidepressants, the correct method depends on the two agents involved. Some changes can use a direct switch, others need a cross-taper, and others require a washout. The principle is to avoid both unnecessary treatment gaps and unsafe overlap. **MAOIs should not be cross-tapered blindly with serotonergic antidepressants** because of the risk of serious interaction.

Two exact washout facts are particularly useful for the viva. After stopping **fluoxetine, at least 5 weeks** should elapse before starting an irreversible non-selective MAOI. In the other direction, after stopping an **irreversible MAOI, at least 2 weeks** should elapse before starting fluoxetine, in accordance with current fluoxetine product information.

The practical approach is therefore to identify the adverse-effect risks of the current and proposed drugs, assess interaction risk, choose the appropriate switching method and taper at a pace the patient tolerates. If clinically significant withdrawal emerges, the plan should be reassessed rather than assuming that all symptoms represent relapse.

## Must-know board facts

- **Fluoxetine → irreversible non-selective MAOI:** wait at least **5 weeks** after stopping fluoxetine.
- **Irreversible MAOI → fluoxetine:** wait at least **2 weeks** after stopping the MAOI.
- Paroxetine and venlafaxine have higher withdrawal liability; fluoxetine is lower because of its long half-life.

## Common traps / examiner traps

- Teaching a universal four-week antidepressant taper.
- Cross-tapering automatically without considering the drug pair.
- Failing to use a washout when switching to or from an irreversible MAOI.
