# Stage 1B — C1 Foundations Manuscript Outline

Status: **PROPOSED — editorial outline for founder review before full prose rewrite**

Source basis: *Master Clinical Psychopharmacology Reference Compendium*, source PDF pages 4–19 plus the CYP450/interaction material on pages 514–515. This file is an editorial restructuring plan, not clinically validated copy.

## Editorial objective

C1 should become the reusable conceptual layer for the rest of the guide. It should explain only the pharmacokinetic, pharmacodynamic, signaling, transport, metabolism, pharmacogenomic and TDM concepts that later therapeutic sections genuinely reuse.

Working target: **13 pages**, with a hard editorial ceiling of **14 pages**. The current source has 16 relevant pages if pages 514–515 are counted. The goal is not to squeeze those pages but to remove duplicated drug-specific material and move it to the clinical sections where it belongs.

Core rule: **general law here; drug-specific threshold, rescue rule, dose adjustment, regulatory instruction or special-population exception at the drug/protocol home.**

## Source-page disposition

### PDF page 4 — module separator

**Decision: CUT as a standalone page.**

Retain only:
- the module title;
- one-sentence scope;
- compact navigation to PK, PD, signaling, BBB, metabolism/PGx, TDM and interactions.

Do not retain the dedicated separator, “15 pages total” language or the broad practice mandate as a full-page object.

### PDF page 5 — syllabus / cover

**Decision: CUT as a standalone page; MERGE navigation into page 1.**

Keep the useful topical map. Remove promotional framing and duplicated statements of purpose. The source’s distinction between kinetic/dynamic principles and cascades/transport/PGx is useful, but the final reader does not need an entire syllabus page.

### PDF page 6 — ADME principles

**Decision: KEEP, REWRITE, SPLIT across new pages 1–2 and 4.**

Keep:
- bioavailability and absorption;
- Vd and protein binding;
- clearance and half-life;
- loading-dose concept;
- steady-state logic;
- TDM sampling principle.

Move or reduce:
- ziprasidone/lurasidone/Cobenfy caloric instructions → drug profiles; one example may remain to demonstrate food-dependent bioavailability;
- “LOT in cirrhosis” → C5/C7; mention Phase II conjugation only as a general concept;
- categorical “complete washout after 5 half-lives” → rewrite as an approximation after verification.

### PDF page 7 — clearance, saturation and dialyzability

**Decision: KEEP the principles; MOVE clinical algorithms.**

Keep:

- Michaelis–Menten concept at a practical level;
- Vd/protein binding/hydrophilicity as dialyzability determinants;
- age/body-composition effect on half-life as a compact example.

Move:
- exact lithium hemodialysis thresholds → lithium toxicity / C4 or C7;
- TCA sodium-bicarbonate rescue → C3/C6 emergency material;
- phenytoin micro-adjustment prescription → C7 neurology profile;
- dramatic fixed fold-change claims → remove unless specifically verified.

### PDF pages 8–9 — receptor spectrum, allosteric modulation, potency/efficacy, tolerance

**Decision: KEEP and CONSOLIDATE into new page 5 plus part of page 7.**

Keep:
- agonist / partial agonist / antagonist / inverse agonist;
- orthosteric vs allosteric modulation;
- potency vs efficacy;
- receptor reserve as a concept;
- receptor downregulation/internalization, tolerance and supersensitivity as adaptation concepts.

Move:

## Writing constraints for C1 prose

- Lead with the clinical consequence, then give the mechanism.
- Prefer one reusable rule over several drug examples.
- Do not repeat a drug-specific instruction that will have a canonical profile later.
- Use equations only when they change interpretation or prescribing.
- No promotional language, absolutes or “gold standard / breakthrough / zero risk” rhetoric unless an authoritative source truly warrants it.
- Distinguish established pharmacology from explanatory models where mechanism remains incomplete.
- Each page should answer one practical question and remain readable at normal A4 scale.
- Cross-references should be designed during text drafting, even though final page numbers will be assigned only after layout.
